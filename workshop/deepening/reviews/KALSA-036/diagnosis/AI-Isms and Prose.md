## Findings

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** all three Papsenai paragraphs.
- **Evidence:** repeated four-to-six item lists cover targets, inputs, support,
  damage, and evidence.
- **Effect:** readers see criteria satisfied rather than an institution lived.
- **Repair direction:** keep concise bearings; move dense event sequence into a
  source organized by store, cohort, watch, and damaged section.
- **Collateral risk:** do not remove functional inputs merely to shorten prose.
- **Confidence:** High.

### AIP36-02 — Corrective/checksum endings pre-explain every paragraph

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `A favorable field does not prove...`; `If provisions fail...`;
  final `are still cited...` sentence.
- **Evidence:** each paragraph closes by interpreting the mechanism.
- **Effect:** cadence becomes predictable and distrusts the reader.
- **Repair direction:** preserve one necessary limit; let the source's damage,
  evidence, and unpaid loss carry the rest.
- **Collateral risk:** the `apparently whole barrier` image is concrete and may
  be worth retaining.
- **Confidence:** High.

### AIP36-03 — A new source could become another procedural template

- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** prospective source.
- **Evidence:** the event contains several owners, records, reforms, and losses.
- **Effect:** a balanced chorus, exhaustive settlement list, or hook-menu close

- **Repair direction:** use asymmetrical sequence and changed objects; end on a
  surviving material receipt, not a thesis.
- **Collateral risk:** keep the military claim audible and incomplete.
- **Confidence:** High.
