# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The hidden Selza'a body is causally mature: family territories, shared-work compacts, named maintainers, water users, third witnesses, emergency stores, material inspectors, prophets, case tables, risk pools, and review each have bounded authority. Public compresses that machine into family beliefs and an outsider contrast. A reader can learn that families disagree about dimna, but cannot yet follow how a failed shared work becomes rescue, evidence, a bounded finding, payment, correction, and a lasting social consequence. The smallest coherent repair is one situated Public teaching source grounded in [[The Broken Windtrap Settlement]], with the existing two Public overview notes linking to it instead of acquiring a duplicate institutional essay.

## Protected successes

- `Selza'a Family Territories` keeps operation, allocation, host territory, compact membership, family rite, and temporary defense distinct. No single family relation silently owns all of them.
- `Luck Harm and Restitution` supplies a reproducible sequence: an affected owner opens a case; emergency aid precedes blame; evidence is preserved; material and prophetic findings stay separate; pools pay; compact review, refusal, and exit remain possible.
- `The Broken Windtrap Settlement` already turns those procedures into historical pressure without granting prophecy a culprit. The keeper's corrected record does not erase lost work, hunger, or suspicion.
- The Public notes preserve several useful boundaries: adoption and marriage matter; shared works and emergency water cross family lines; a third witness can interrupt feud logic; compact and federation coordination exist without becoming a universal state.

## Findings

### IC34-01 — Public names beliefs but omits the institution that turns uncertainty into consequence

- **Severity:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, `## Selza'a`, especially the paragraph beginning “Outsiders say”; `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, `## What people compare`.
- **Problem:** Public says dimna may protect, pass through inherited relations, shape territorial balance, or look like predation, and it warns that coincidence is not proof. It does not show who can act after a shared work fails, what must happen before accusation, which evidence changes a finding, who pays, or how a bad record is repaired. “Families argue” is currently doing the work of an institution.
- **Causal consequence:** The public setting cannot reproduce its own stated restraint. A player sees a cultural disagreement but no route from material failure to emergency welfare, investigation, remedy, appeal, or an adventure-relevant choice. The hidden owner notes therefore remain the only place where the anti-accusation limit has practical force.
- **Repair requirement:** Add one bounded Public source, situated as an account or teaching around [[The Broken Windtrap Settlement]]. It should carry the existing handoffs: shared work fails; rescue, replacement water, care, restraint, and evidence preservation begin; maintainers and an accepted worker inspect wreckage; a prophet may widen precaution but cannot identify a bearer; separate findings allocate repair and responsibility; stores or the compact pool pay; restrictions and records receive review; procedure can correct a finding without undoing hunger, lost labor, or suspicion. The overview notes should link to that carrier and remain concise.
- **Do not invent:** a citywide tribunal, universal guild, permanent neutral family, named people or families, dialogue, a standard transcript, or a case table used by every Selza'a compact.

### IC34-02 — The outsider contrast can make an internal evidentiary limit look optional

- **Severity:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, `## What witnesses can know`: “Selza'a families may recognize a pattern of protection that no outsider can turn into a universal test.”
- **Problem:** The sentence correctly refuses a universal detector, but its grammar places the limit on outsiders. The hidden owners impose the stronger and more useful boundary on everyone: family doctrine, inherited signs, and prophetic bias cannot by themselves prove membership, transfer office, identify a bearer, establish magical attack, assign guilt, or settle liability.
- **Causal consequence:** A Public reader may infer that insiders possess a valid private test and therefore an unreviewable jurisdiction. That steals authority from material inspection, compact procedure, consent, and bounded findings.
- **Repair requirement:** Make the limit consequence-specific and reciprocal. Family interpretation may guide rite, memory, caution, or a request for wider safeguards; it cannot by itself identify a bearer, move compact or family standing, or assign responsibility for harm. Demonstrate this through the same event carrier rather than appending an abstract rule list.
- **Do not invent:** an omniscient counter-doctrine or a claim that Selza'a interpretations are false. The distinction is between meaningful situated knowledge and authority to impose consequences.

### IC34-03 — “Families maintain windtraps” collapses four authorities into one social noun

- **Severity:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, `## Selza'a`, especially the list beginning “Families maintain windtraps”.
- **Problem:** The overview makes “families” appear to operate works, allocate water, own territory, judge harm, and coordinate emergencies. The hidden body distributes those powers: a named maintainer or technical custodian operates the work; users hold an allocation schedule; the host territory owes access and shelter duties; the compact owns shared-work terms and a risk pool; an affected owner can open a case; a third witness gains standing only for that case.
- **Causal consequence:** The Public surface weakens consent and contestability. It becomes unclear who can be refused, replaced, appealed, or made to pay, and every disagreement risks reading as a total feud between kin blocs.
- **Repair requirement:** In the event carrier, name roles at the points where they act. Preserve the distinction between operation, allocation, compact obligation, host duty, and case standing, while making clear that local compacts may distribute those roles differently.
- **Do not invent:** a universal office hierarchy or standardized Selza'a administration. The needed clarity is functional, not bureaucratic.

## Pattern or dependency map

```text
shared windtrap failure
  -> affected owner / compact opens the matter
  -> rescue + replacement water + care + restraint + evidence preservation
  -> maintainer and accepted worker inspect material causes
  -> prophet may add a scoped warning, never an identity or verdict
  -> distinct findings: failure, maintenance, restitution, precaution
  -> emergency stores / risk pool move resources
  -> restrictions and records receive compact review
  -> practice changes, while material and reputational harm may remain
```

The missing Public edge is not another explanation of Luck. It is the visible handoff from situated interpretation to limited institutional action.

## Tempting edit to resist

Do not solve readability by giving Selza'a a Luck detector, a citywide court, a permanent neutral caste, or a universal water ministry. Do not give the settlement named protagonists or dialogue. Do not fully restore the blamed keeper at the end: corrected procedure matters precisely because it cannot refund hunger, lost labor, and social injury after the fact.

## Ranked actions

1. Create the bounded Public carrier for [[The Broken Windtrap Settlement]], preserving the existing factual owner and showing the complete rescue-to-review handoff.
2. Repair the outsider sentence so the evidentiary limit constrains insiders and outsiders alike without denying situated family knowledge.
3. Tighten the Selza'a overview to distinguish maintainer, users, host territory, compact, affected owner, and third witness at the moment each authority matters; link outward rather than duplicating the machinery.
