# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** Selza'a is legible as a promising outline but cannot be encountered through the Public surface. The reader learns that fogbound territorial families maintain windtraps, cisterns, routes, shrines, and boundaries; that adoption and marriage matter; that third families witness disputes; and that dimna relations may be described as protection, inheritance, balance, or predation. No Selza'a person, place, source, dispute, practice, or onward link lets those claims become lived knowledge.

Attention rises sharply at `dimna's protection` and `dangerous stranger`, then stops. `Magic and Miracles` repeats the same uncertainty in survey language rather than opening a new route. I could imagine repairing a windtrap, carrying a third-family witness mark, negotiating emergency water, or being adopted across a disputed boundary, but the Public body does not yet show how any of those acts proceed or whom they place at risk.

## Protected successes

- The root consent gate is explicit, and every followed Public link remained outside `Spoilers/`. All eleven listed Git blob hashes matched the packet.
- The Selza'a section resists biological essentialism. Family can involve adoption and marriage as well as descent, and outsiders' claim of impossible fortune is explicitly less precise than Selza'a disagreement.
- Preserve the fog, windtraps, cisterns, shared-work compacts, emergency water, third-family witnesses, and dimna. These details distinguish Selza'a from a generic woodland luck culture.
- Preserve the uncertainty over whether a dimna relation is protection, inheritance, territorial balance, or predation. The missing object is inhabited practice, not a metaphysical answer.
- Low Sere, the Basin Table, the broken sluice, and the Three Ledgers source demonstrate strong situated presentation without trespassing on Selza'a; they should not be repurposed or made to explain this culture.

## Findings

### REN34-01

- **Finding ID:** REN34-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, `## Selza'a`, through the page footer; `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, Selza'a references under `## What people compare` and `## What witnesses can know`
- **Evidence:** The Selza'a section contains two survey paragraphs and no link. The Peoples footer routes to general surveys and a Ju'onai source. Magic repeats the dimna/protection/predation distinction but offers only general magic pages, Low Sere, and Ju'onai sources. No followed Public route contains a Selza'a-owned place, speaker, record, or event.
- **Effect:** Curiosity enters a loop of paraphrase. Selza'a remains the people outsiders call lucky rather than a culture a reader can meet or play within. The section's caution against stereotype is clear, but there is no honest fulfillment of the more precise Selza'a arguments it promises.
- **Repair direction:** Add one bounded, situated Public carrier and link it directly from the Selza'a section. A family boundary, water, adoption, shared-work, or witness dispute could carry the material already established; the carrier need not explain the whole culture.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not turn one family into the Selza'a norm, invent a central government, add a generic culture encyclopedia, or make the new source an answer key for luck.
- **Dependency:** Public presentation owner plus the canonical owner of the selected Selza'a family, place, or practice.
- **Confidence:** High; every available Public route was followed and none supplies the encounter.

### REN34-02

- **Finding ID:** REN34-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, final Selza'a paragraph: `A family may speak of a dimna's protection, an inherited relation, a territorial balance, or a dangerous stranger.`
- **Evidence:** `dimna` is the only Selza'a-specific term offered, and four abstract interpretations are attached to it. The reader never sees someone address, invoke, shelter, accuse, inherit, refuse, or witness a dimna relation. Magic restates the term without adding an act, sign, or consequence.
- **Effect:** The word carries intrigue but little usable meaning. It risks reading as a veiled luck-spirit category whose mystery is maintained by withholding all practice, rather than by presenting disputed evidence.
- **Repair direction:** In the same carrier proposed for REN34-01, show what people do because one party claims a dimna relation and what another party contests. Give the reader an observable act, record, object, boundary, or cost while leaving the entity or relation itself unresolved.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not define what a dimna truly is, establish a universal test, decide protection versus predation, or translate the term into a familiar spirit/familiar/patron category.
- **Dependency:** Selza'a situated-knowledge and magic owners.
- **Confidence:** High; the term recurs but never gains operational context.

### REN34-03

- **Finding ID:** REN34-03
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, first Selza'a paragraph
- **Evidence:** Territorial families maintain six kinds of infrastructure and use adoption, marriage, feud, witnesses, compacts, emergency water, and temporary federations. The list establishes breadth but not who can call a witness, what third-family standing changes, how emergency water crosses a hostile boundary, or what happens to someone whose family relation is disputed.
- **Effect:** `Territorial family` risks collapsing into the familiar fantasy clan despite the distinctive infrastructure and non-descent membership. The reader can name institutions but cannot picture an interaction, character role, or consequence.
- **Repair direction:** Let the situated carrier follow one decision through family custody, a third-family witness, and a concrete shared work or resource. One consequential sequence is preferable to expanding the institutional list.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not standardize procedure across all families, resolve feud into polite mediation, or portray adoption and marriage as automatically equal or uncontested routes to belonging.
- **Dependency:** Selza'a family and material-practice owners; institutional criticism may test the witness role.
- **Confidence:** High; the paragraph names multiple practices but no handoff between them.

### REN34-04

- **Finding ID:** REN34-04
- **Priority:** Optional
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, closing Selza'a sentence: `None of those claims makes coincidence proof against a person.`
- **Evidence:** The sentence warns against turning a pattern of fortune into proof, but Public supplies no accusation, forum, witness, or consequence that explains why a person would need that protection.
- **Effect:** The narrator delivers the correct evidentiary caution before the reader has encountered the social danger. It feels instructional and slightly opaque rather than culturally situated.
- **Repair direction:** Once the carrier exists, test whether the action makes this sentence redundant. If the limit still needs stating, attach it to the relevant witness, family, or record practice rather than another general maxim.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not remove the protection against treating coincidence as proof unless the new source clearly carries it.
- **Dependency:** Depends on REN34-01 and the evidence practice chosen there.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high; the sentence performs a needed safeguard, but its current reading effect comes from missing context.

## Pattern or dependency map

- `Peoples and Polities` introduces Selza'a infrastructure, family politics, and dimna disagreement.
- `Magic and Miracles` repeats the dimna relation and rejects a universal test.
- Neither page links to a Selza'a carrier.
- Every Public source belongs to another place or institution.
- One situated Selza'a source can therefore resolve the navigation loop, give `dimna` practical pressure, and make territorial family institutions playable without broad expansion.

I wanted to follow a third-family witness, an adopted member whose standing is contested, or a household asking emergency water from a hostile boundary. Nothing on the route became unsafe or spoiler-bearing; the failure is absence of contact, not excess disclosure. The section is too short to induce skimming, but attention drops immediately when its last sentence offers no next step.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not add a Selza'a glossary, complete family taxonomy, national map, universal feud code, luck probability system, or definitive description of dimna. Do not simplify the apostrophe or replace `dimna` with a familiar fantasy category. Mystery should survive inside observable practice.

## Ranked actions

2. **High-value:** Use that same encounter to show a disputed dimna relation and one concrete third-family witness handoff without deciding either universally.
3. **Optional:** Remove or situate the coincidence-proof maxim if the encounter has made its warning visible through consequence.
