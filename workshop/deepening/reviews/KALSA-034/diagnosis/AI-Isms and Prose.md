# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** Selza'a receives only two short Public explanations, and both are
assembled from the habits this seat exists to catch. `Peoples and Polities`
uses an explicit outsider-praise/insider-correction hinge, followed by a
four-item viewpoint list and a narrator's evidentiary checksum. `Magic and
Miracles` repeats the same content as two balanced family claims and another
general limit. The distinctions are sound; the prose makes them feel supplied
by the vault's institutional essayist rather than learned in fog, water work,
family dependence, accusation, or repair.

The hidden event supplies an unusually good replacement: one damaged windtrap,
an adopted keeper, two accusing families, a prophet who refuses to name a
bearer, corroded metal, work records, emergency water, and a correction that
does not restore livelihood. A bounded Public rendering can carry almost all
of the existing explanation without inventing a speaker or purging useful
terms.

## Protected successes

- Preserve the fogbound country, territorial families, windtraps, cisterns,
  adoption, marriage, feud, shared work, and emergency water. These details
  make Selza'a strange and materially habitable.
- Preserve `dimna` as an unresolved Selza'a term rather than translating it
  into the author cosmology or deleting it for reader ease.
- `At the Basin Table` remains the strongest Public counterexample: tools,
  water, work, names, wages, and refusal let uncertainty emerge without a
  narrator balancing every moral account.
- The Public Magic page is correctly selective. Its Ju'onai technical register
  and Papsenai work evidence were previously reviewed and should not be reopened
  merely because Selza'a is thin.

## Findings

### AIP34-01

- **Finding ID:** AIP34-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md#Selza'a`, paragraph
  beginning `Outsiders say the selza'a are impossibly fortunate.`
- **Evidence:** The paragraph stages a familiar wonder, immediately announces
  that inhabitants are `more precise and less comforting`, distributes one
  clause each to protection, inheritance, balance, and danger, then closes by
  explaining the correct evidentiary limit. The baseline packet identifies
  this exact outsider/insider construction as the known fault. Comparable
  prose elsewhere has already been repaired by letting a source-owned event or
  mechanism carry the distinction.
- **Effect:** The sentence anticipates and corrects a shallow reader before any
  Selza'a person, object, or consequence has appeared. `More precise and less
  comforting` praises the narrator's own nuance. The four claims become a
  viewpoint chorus with identical weight and no reason any family preserves
  one rather than another.
- **Repair direction:** Delete the outsider/insider hinge. Let the Broken
  Windtrap case show why fortune, dimna stories, family suspicion, prophecy,
  and material evidence do not collapse into guilt. Route the overview to that
  case rather than replacing the paragraph with another maxim.
- **Collateral risk:** The repair must still establish famous Selza'a fortune,
  real family interpretations, and the impossibility of direct detection. Do
  not turn restraint into modern enlightened consensus or make the accusing
  families stupid foils.
- **Dependency:** Culture, History, and Reader Experience; the event owner
  limits every Public detail.
- **Confidence:** High. The packet names the construction and the whole cluster
  occurs in six sentences of available Selza'a prose.

### AIP34-03

- **Finding ID:** AIP34-03
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** proposed Public windtrap carrier, constrained by
  `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Broken Windtrap Settlement.md`.
- **Evidence:** The event is presently arranged as `Failure and accusation`,
  `Restraint and repair`, `What remained broken`, and `Institutional
  consequences`. Copying that structure directly would produce a clean
  accusation-procedure-repair-moral sequence, including a final balanced
  lesson about procedure and reputation. The strongest retained facts are
  more awkward: the prophet refuses the demanded identification, corrosion is
  found beneath an unremoved covering, every family preserved better records
  of its neighbours, and the cleared keeper still loses contracts.
- **Effect:** A faithful but mechanically summarized Public adaptation could
  become the same symmetrical moral ledger in longer form: institution saves
  water, then narrator invoices it for inequality. It would preserve facts and
  lose experience.
- **Repair direction:** Organize the source around the work and the evidence
  rather than the owner's analytic headings. Let water open before blame,
  record comparisons expose shared neglect, and the corrected keeper's changed
  affiliation close the case without a concluding thesis. Avoid invented
  dialogue; asymmetry can come from sequence, omission, and material result.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not understate that the compact genuinely prevented
  armed crossing, restored water, paid care, and improved later practice.
  Failure to restore reputation is not proof the procedure was empty.
- **Dependency:** Event provenance, History, Material, and Reader Experience.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. The defect is prospective, but prior Public
  passes show the exact risk and the event offers enough concrete action to
  avoid it.

all, do not invent dialogue for the unnamed keeper just to make the prose look
less processed.
