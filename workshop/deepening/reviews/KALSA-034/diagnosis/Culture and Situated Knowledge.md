# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The hidden Selza'a owners contain a genuine knowledge ecology: family rites and lineages preserve dimna relations; maintainers and users preserve work records; witnesses remember boundaries and losses; prophets report bounded forecast bias; compacts preserve findings; territorial councils and victims remember the Broken Windtrap Settlement differently. Public suppresses that ecology into two polished narrator summaries. “Outsiders say” supplies a foil, then “Selza'a arguments” distributes four neat interpretations without an inhabitant owner, evidence path, reproduction path, or characteristic blind spot. The smallest coherent repair is one explicitly reconstructed Public source grounded in [[The Broken Windtrap Settlement]], followed by narrow links and wording repairs in the two surveys.

## Protected successes

- `Peoples and Polities` refuses to reduce belonging to ancestry: adoption and marriage carry political weight, and its opening permits kinship, residence, work, worship, exile, and choice to coexist.
- The hidden owners protect useful opacity. A dimna may be remembered as ancestor, territorial protector, lineage continuity, or God Beast, but rite and family doctrine do not settle author truth, magical identity, office, guilt, or moral status.
- `The Broken Windtrap Settlement` already supplies incompatible social memory: territorial councils remember successful restraint, while victims remember water repaired before reputation and lost work. Neither memory is promoted to the event's neutral moral.
- The event preserves the adopted mixed-heritage keeper and poor households as people with consequences beyond the magic category. Correction changes the record but does not restore contracts, affiliation, rations, or labor.
- Existing Public sources demonstrate several valid source forms without imposing one template: a situated meeting, a circulating teaching dialogue, and a declared comparison of incomplete records. The Selza'a repair need not invent a transcript or named speaker to become situated.

## Findings

### CSK34-01 — The Public Selza'a explanation has no inhabitant owner or transmission body

- **Finding ID:** CSK34-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, `## Selza'a`, paragraph beginning “Outsiders say”; `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, `## What people compare`, paragraph beginning “Household and route testimony travels differently.”
- **Evidence:** The surveys attribute statements generically to outsiders, “Selza'a arguments,” a family, and a rival family. They do not identify whether the explanation comes through family rite, shrine memory, household teaching, route testimony, work records, compact findings, territorial councils, victims, or a synthesis of named source classes. Hidden canon establishes all of those as distinct carriers with different authority.
- **Effect:** The reader receives an author-balanced taxonomy with local nouns pasted over it. The four interpretations appear equally available to a neutral narrator, while nobody in the setting bears the material conditions, interests, or blind spot of producing them. Public therefore fails its own inhabitant-model rule even though the claims themselves are bounded.
- **Repair direction:** Add one Public source reconstructed from the adopted event and explicitly declare its basis and omissions. The event already authorizes a source chain: preserved wreckage and maintenance records; the prophet's refusal and bounded report; the compact finding and corrected keeper record; later compact record practice; territorial-council memory; victim memory. The source should say that family names, place, date, and participants remain omitted and that it is not a transcript. Link both overview passages to this carrier and leave the overviews incomplete.
- **Collateral risk:** Inventing a named family, person, quotation, native legal term, or universal document form would counterfeit provenance. Treating the event account as an omniscient reconstruction would merely move the narrator problem to a longer page.
- **Dependency:** The event note remains sole owner of factual sequence. The Public source owns presentation only and must preserve the packet's publication boundary.
- **Confidence:** High; the packet names the `Outsiders say... Selza'a arguments...` construction as the epistemic fault, and the event explicitly owns two incompatible memories plus the changed record practice.

### CSK34-02 — Public plurality stops at rival families and leaves most Selza'a people outside culture

- **Finding ID:** CSK34-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, `## What people compare` and `## What witnesses can know`; `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md`, `## Selza'a`.
- **Evidence:** Public presents family protection versus rival-family predation, followed by an outsider-facing limit. Hidden owners show that “family” contains adopted people, spouses, clients, dependents, hosts, shrine affiliates, workers, mixed and Null people, and people changing affiliation. The adopted mixed-heritage keeper, poor households, windtrap worker, prophet, compact users, and victims in the event encounter the same failure from incompatible positions.
- **Effect:** Selza'a risk becoming a polity of personified kin blocs whose only cultural life is interpreting Luck. Adoption and marriage are named, but the reader cannot see how affiliation, labor, class, technical knowledge, injury, or reputation changes what a person can say and what their account costs them. Subordinate people remain evidence for a balanced moral rather than subjects with interests.
- **Repair direction:** Let the event carrier keep roles and stakes distinct without inventing personalities: the accused keeper's corrected record and changed affiliation; poor households' rations and lost work; private-cistern comfort; the worker's material inspection; the prophet's refusal; compact and territorial memories. Show that these accounts travel through different records and obligations and do not merge into one “Selza'a view.”
- **Collateral risk:** Do not make the keeper, poor households, or Null and mixed people morally omniscient countervoices. The event does not authorize their dialogue, full doctrine, or consensus, and the protected residual harm should not become a modern editorial lesson pasted into their mouths.
- **Dependency:** Material allocation and institutional consequence remain owned by the family-territory, restitution, and event notes; this finding concerns whose knowledge becomes visible and how it travels.
- **Confidence:** High; the contrast between Public's family-level speakers and the event's role-, class-, and affiliation-specific consequences is explicit.

### CSK34-03 — The outsider limit obscures the useful domain and blind spot of family knowledge

- **Finding ID:** CSK34-03
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md`, `## What witnesses can know`: “Selza'a families may recognize a pattern of protection that no outsider can turn into a universal test.”
- **Evidence:** Hidden canon treats family interpretation as meaningful for rite, memory, affiliation, territorial caution, and inherited obligation. It also establishes a characteristic blind spot: success, survival, lineage story, prophetic bias, and improbable coincidence cannot identify a bearer, prove dimna acceptance, transfer office, or assign responsibility for harm. That limit applies within families as well as beyond them.
- **Effect:** The current sentence can be read as cultural deference with a trapdoor: outsiders lack the test, but insiders may possess one. That makes situated knowledge either a private detector or a decorative belief protected from consequence. It does not tell readers where the knowledge is useful, who contests it, or what happens when it outruns its evidence.
- **Repair direction:** Reframe the survey around use and limit rather than insider essence. Family accounts can guide rite, memory, kin claims, caution, and questions asked of a damaged system; the settlement shows that responsibility still follows material access, work, breach, and preserved evidence. The Public source should stage the conflict rather than announce a universal epistemic rule.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not resolve whether any dimna account is metaphysically true or replace situated belief with author mechanics. Luck remains real and directly undetectable; the repair is about evidentiary reach, not skeptical debunking.
- **Dependency:** `Kaos` and `Magic Styles` retain backstage mechanics. Selza'a polity, family, and restitution notes retain local doctrine and consequences.
- **Confidence:** High; the public grammar and the hidden owners' repeated consequence-specific limits are unambiguous.

## Pattern or dependency map

```text
family rite / lineage memory -----> dimna relation, affiliation, obligation
work record / wreckage -----------> maintenance and material-cause claims
prophetic report -----------------> scoped precaution, not identity
compact case and corrected record -> bounded finding and later work practice
territorial-council memory -------> restraint as successful government
victim memory --------------------> repair that left livelihood and standing broken
                         \
                          -> bounded Public reconstruction -> survey links
```

The live pluralism is carried by incompatible source bodies and consequences. The current Public survey flattens those paths into one balanced paragraph before the reader can encounter them.

## Tempting edit to resist

Do not invent a family, keeper name, native legal vocabulary, quotation, or recovered transcript to make the source feel alive. Do not decide whether dimna are ancestors, territorial protectors, lineage continuities, or God Beasts. Do not make the settlement representative of every family or compact. Its omissions and incomplete repair are part of the evidence, not holes to plaster with competent-sounding lore paste.

## Ranked actions

1. Add the bounded Public reconstruction of [[The Broken Windtrap Settlement]], declaring its record basis, incompatible memories, and protected omissions.
2. Replace the outsider-led taxonomy in the two surveys with concise links into that source and language that names a use domain and evidentiary limit without implying insider omniscience.
3. Preserve role-, class-, and affiliation-specific stakes in the source so “family” does not swallow the keeper, poor users, worker, prophet, victims, and people who change affiliation.
