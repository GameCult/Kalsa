# Material Life and Ecology Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The Selza'a owner notes already contain a coherent material chain,
but the current Public projection strips it to a catalogue of windtraps,
cisterns, routes, emergency water, and abstract disagreement about Luck. A
Public reader cannot yet follow fog and wind through shared infrastructure,
labor, allocation, failure, bodily harm, or uneven recovery. The smallest
repair is one bounded, linked reconstruction of the Broken Windtrap Settlement;
the hidden material model does not need expansion.

## Protected successes

- `Selza'a Family Territories#Shared works and commons` separates windtrap
  operation from water allocation. A keeper may stop an unsafe machine without
  acquiring its output, while users retain inspection, emergency-baseline, and
  repair obligations. Preserve that clean material authority boundary.
- `The Broken Windtrap Settlement` carries a complete evidenced chain: a high
  windtrap and lower cistern shared by three territories; deferred maintenance
  and hidden anchor corrosion; worker injury, broken catchment surfaces, and
  contaminated stored water; emergency stores, baseline water, independent
  inspection, pooled care and repair; then maintenance-weighted contributions
  and a rebuilt trap.
- The event makes unequal recovery mechanical rather than decorative. Private
  cisterns shelter wealthier families while poor households consume emergency
  rations and lose work; the cleared keeper receives a corrected record but
  loses contracts and changes affiliation. Later lost-work support is a
  historical response, not a claim that the original remedy was complete.
- Luck remains genuine and directly undetectable. Material inspection can find
  corrosion and missed duty without proving that Luck was absent, present, or
  attached to the accused keeper.

### MLE34-01

- **Finding ID:** MLE34-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md#Selza'a`, especially
  its first paragraph; `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md#What people
  compare`; owner chain in `Kalsa/Spoilers/Institutions/Selza'a Family
  Territories.md#Shared works and commons` and `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The
  Broken Windtrap Settlement.md#Failure and accusation` through `#Restraint and

- **Evidence:** Public names windtraps, cisterns, shared-work compacts, and
  emergency water but shows no handoff among them. The owner notes establish an
  available chain: fog and wind -> cloth, vanes, catchments, anchor, cistern,
  access paths, maintainers, schedules, and pool contributions -> shared water
  with an emergency baseline -> hidden corrosion, structural failure, injury,
  and contamination -> preserved wreckage, independent inspection,
  replacement water, care, pooled repair, and rebuilt service.
- **Effect:** Selza'a water reads as set dressing behind a culture of mysterious
  fortune. Readers cannot see what ordinary competence keeps the city alive,
  what a failed wonder does to bodies and stores, or where intervention is
  possible without adjudicating Luck.
- **Repair direction:** Publish one presentation-only reconstruction of the
  adopted windtrap event and link it directly from the Selza'a survey. Carry
  the evidenced chain through one failure and recovery; retain the event's
  protected omissions and stop before inventing exact windtrap engineering,
  water volumes, names, or a universal compact procedure.
- **Collateral risk:** A careless repair could turn the source into a technical
  manual, establish one settlement as citywide law, invent named families or
  native offices, or imply that material corrosion disproves magical pressure.
- **Dependency:** The event owner supplies the sequence; Family Territories and
  Luck Harm and Restitution supply the bounded present practices. Public owns
  only the reconstruction and link.
- **Confidence:** High. Every handoff is explicit in the supplied owners and
  absent from the two relevant Public survey passages.

### MLE34-02

- **Dependency:** Event owner and Luck Harm and Restitution; historical-change
  review should protect the sequence from becoming timeless current practice.
- **Confidence:** High. The owner notes explicitly distinguish immediate
  system repair, later cost allocation, and losses that remain with people.

## Pattern or dependency map

The material chain is not absent from canon; it is unavailable at the Public
handoff:

`fog and wind -> shared windtrap labor and records -> cistern allocation ->
anchor failure, injury, and contamination -> emergency baseline and pool ->
inspection and rebuilt service -> unequal residual loss`

`Peoples and Polities` compresses the first half into infrastructure nouns.
`Magic and Miracles` carries the uncertainty around Luck but not the material
practice that makes uncertainty survivable. One event-bounded Public source can
join those two surfaces without expanding hidden canon.

Food, fuel, waste, medicine beyond immediate care, exact seasons, water volume,
and detailed windtrap construction remain absent or explicitly unresolved in
the supplied canon. They are not required to repair this event-sized chain and
should not be invented merely to complete an ecological inventory.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not design a complete Selza'a water network, quantify fog yield, invent a
  standard windtrap, name participating families, or universalize the case
  table. Observable cloth, vanes, catchments, anchor, cistern, records, stores,
  labor, injury, and repair already provide enough material grip.
- Do not manufacture drought or mundane scarcity to make the wonder “cost.”
  The specific costs are interdependence, maintenance access, contaminated
  storage, injury, lost work, ration use, private reserve asymmetry, and an
  accused keeper whose livelihood outlasts the repair.

## Ranked actions

1. **Essential:** Add and directly link one bounded Public reconstruction of
   the Broken Windtrap Settlement that carries the established material chain
   from shared atmospheric water infrastructure through failure and recovery.
2. **High-value:** Preserve the uneven allocation and incomplete recovery:
   emergency aid, private cistern advantage, poor-household ration and work
   loss, contribution shifts, and the keeper's unrecovered livelihood.
3. **High-value:** Make preserved wreckage, access, corrosion, work schedules,
   and inspection the material means of acting without pretending to detect or
   disprove Luck.
