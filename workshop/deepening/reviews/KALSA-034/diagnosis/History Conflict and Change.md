# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

Block. The hidden owners give Selza'a evidence practice a strong historical
formation: a shared windtrap fails, magical suspicion targets an adopted
keeper, reciprocal restraint prevents feud, material inspection distributes
responsibility, and later compacts inherit both reforms and unfinished harm.
The factual event remains correctly owned. Public does not carry that history.
It presents families as already possessing a precise shared wisdom, with no
crisis, constituency, failed remedy, or inherited argument showing how the
practice acquired its current shape. The result is a timeless culture summary
where the canon actually contains conflict and path dependence.

All 21 Git blob hashes in `KALSA-034-baseline-01` were verified and match.

## Protected successes

- `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Broken Windtrap Settlement.md` §§ “Failure and
  accusation” and “Restraint and repair” makes procedural change arise from a
  compound material crisis: corrosion, contaminated water, missed maintenance,
  cross-family suspicion, a prophet's bounded refusal, and the immediate risk
  of armed crossings.
- The event § “What remained broken” refuses a clean reform story. The keeper's
  record is corrected without restoring contracts or affiliation; poor
  households consume reserves and lose work while private-cistern families
  remain comfortable.
- The event keeps distinct constituencies and memories. Territorial councils
  remember a distributed system stopping feud; victims remember that water was
  repaired before reputation and that clearance did not repair the cleared
  person.
- Neighboring hidden owners respect factual ownership. `Luck — Selza'a.md`,
  `Selza'a Family Territories.md`, and `Luck Harm and Restitution.md` carry
  institutional consequences and current failure pressures without changing
  the event's sequence or declaring a magical culprit.

## Findings

### HCC34-01

- **Finding ID:** HCC34-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md` § “Selza'a”;
  `Kalsa/Public/World/Magic and Miracles.md` §§ “What people compare” and “What
  witnesses can know”; current `Kalsa/Public/index.md` § “Read another
  argument.”
- **Evidence:** Public describes territorial families, shared works, third
  witnesses, emergency water, dimna claims, and the limit on treating
  coincidence as proof. It does not show the Broken Windtrap crisis that formed
  a durable reciprocal-restraint precedent, changed maintenance recording and
  lost-work support, or left the accused keeper and poor households only
  partly repaired. Navigation offers no Selza'a historical or situated carrier.
- **Effect:** The current practice appears fully formed and culturally natural.
  Readers cannot see why families accept restraint despite feud, which failure
  changed the records, who bore the cost of correction, or what present
  pressure could reopen the argument. The event's consequences stop at hidden
  owner references instead of reaching the reader surface.
- **Repair direction:** Use the allowed Public surfaces to route readers into
  one declared reconstruction of the Broken Windtrap Settlement. Carry the
  causal sequence from shared failure and accusation through restraint,
  inspection, pooled repair, changed contributions, corrected record, and
  incomplete social and class remedy. Link it from the Selza'a survey and the
  Public source route; use the Magic survey only as much as needed to connect
  evidence limits.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not invent the families, keeper, place, date, native
  legal terminology, or magical cause. Do not present the event as the origin
  of every compact or proof that all present Selza'a practice is uniform.
- **Dependency:** Public voice, navigation, and spoiler-safe projection from
  the sole event owner.
- **Confidence:** High. The hidden event already supplies a complete formation
  and consequence chain, while Public contains none of it.

### HCC34-02

- **Finding ID:** HCC34-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Peoples and Polities.md` § “Selza'a,”
  paragraph beginning “Outsiders say”; compare `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The
  Broken Windtrap Settlement.md` § “What remained broken” and
  `Kalsa/Spoilers/Institutions/Luck Harm and Restitution.md` §§ “Poor, mixed,
  Null, and outsider perspectives” and “Historical consequence.”
- **Evidence:** The Public contrast makes “Outsiders” simplistic and “Selza'a
  arguments” collectively precise. Hidden canon instead preserves incompatible
  Selza'a memories and unequal exposure: councils celebrate restraint, victims
  preserve failed restoration, poor keepers lose future labor, and households
  with private reserves can outlast a shared crisis.
- **Effect:** Internal historical conflict becomes author consensus spoken in a
  cultural plural. The people most changed by the settlement disappear from
  the account, and later practice reads as a lesson learned by everyone rather
  than a contested precedent with unequal beneficiaries.
- **Repair direction:** Replace the outsider/insider verdict with asymmetrical
  situated evidence. Let the event carrier show what the compact, inspecting
  worker, prophet, cleared keeper, harmed households, and better-insulated
  families could each preserve or contest. It needs no balanced chorus; two or
  three materially opposed positions are enough if their different memories
  change present claims.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not turn every family into a viewpoint slot or imply
  that councils, victims, keepers, and poor households form universal named
  factions. Preserve local roles and selective memory.
- **Dependency:** Culture and situated knowledge; material inequality is
  evidence here only insofar as it changes historical memory and reform.
- **Confidence:** High. The contrast between Public consensus and the event's
  explicitly divided memories is direct.

## Pattern or dependency map

Shared windtrap and cistern -> accumulated missed maintenance -> failure and
Luck accusation -> reciprocal restraint, emergency water, and preserved
wreckage -> material inspection and pooled repair -> corrected record plus
unequal unresolved loss -> later maintenance and lost-work changes ->
**missing Public carrier for the contested precedent**.

The pressure can recur without a new event: another shared-work failure,
prophetic bias report, compact exclusion, or feud threat can force present
families to decide whether the settlement is binding precedent, useful memory,
or somebody else's expensive lesson.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not resolve the event's names, date, location, magical cause, or the
  keeper's Luck status to make the history feel vivid. Its material sequence
  and incomplete restitution are already concrete.
- Do not call the settlement a Selza'a constitutional moment or make its case
  table a citywide court. The historical strength is that a bounded compact
  changed later practice without becoming central government.

## Ranked actions

1. **Essential:** Carry the Broken Windtrap formation and incomplete repair
   into one situated Public reconstruction with honest navigation.
2. **High-value:** Replace the outsider/insider consensus frame with the
   event's internally divided, materially consequential memories.
3. **Optional:** Add no new event or chronology. The existing settlement and
   its possible recurrence already supply sufficient change pressure.
