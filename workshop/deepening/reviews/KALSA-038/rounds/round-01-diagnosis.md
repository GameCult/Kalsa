# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The Public vault has strong objects, pressures, and institutional
distinctions, but too many pages deliver them in the same processed cadence:
source disclaimer, orderly mechanism, explicit qualification, corrected
procedure, residual injury. The recurrence is now audible across cultures. The
repair should preserve the setting's exact distinctions while varying where a
page begins, what kind of sentence carries knowledge, and whether the narrator
needs to explain the moral after the objects have done so.

All fifteen raw SHA-1 hashes matched `KALSA-038-baseline-01` before review.

## Protected successes

- `At the Basin Table` has an actual scene voice. The drain key, name cloth,
  copied warning, wet rooms, and knock let disagreement arrive through gesture
  and pressure. Its opening `The table is not a table` is deliberate
  antithesis, not correction theatre, and should survive.
- `Four Readings of a Broken Sluice` gives its schools distinguishable methods
  and lets the keeper puncture each method with knowledge of fields, seed, and
  channel work. The repeated dialogue frames belong to a teaching exercise and
  are not narrator-template leakage.
- The living-claims inventory in `Relics and Ruins` and the operational terms
  in the delving compact are functional play references. Their elements should
  not be deleted merely to lower a comma count.
- Low Sere's forty-one hearths, warm grey water, black pressure door, wet lower
  rooms, Grey Beds, and named disputants give the vault a physical center. Its
  inventories mostly locate things a visitor can touch.

## Findings

### AIP38-01 — Five archival sources enter through the same disclaimer mould

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** opening paragraphs of `After the High Windtrap Broke`, `What
  the Three Ledgers Kept`, `Who Kept the Heat`, `The Missing Relief Cohort`,
  and `The Closed Lift Appeal`.
- **Evidence:** each opening inventories absent metadata or unnamed actors,
  identifies surviving records or copies, and then limits what `this rendering`
  or `the account` claims. The Windtrap omits `families, keeper, prophet,
  worker, place, and date`; Three Ledgers omits `cult, its god, the district, or
  the year`; Ash-Valve omits `people, branch, tributary ward, storm, campaign,
  and date`; Shield Levy omits `campaign, its authorities, or the communities`;
  Closed Lift omits `city's name, its date, the houses involved, or the
  captain's name`.
- **Effect:** provenance that should distinguish five archives instead exposes
  one authorial form. Readers learn to skim the first paragraph as a mandatory
  waiver before the story starts.
- **Repair direction:** retain every epistemic limit, but let each page enter
  through its own surviving carrier or use: a compact cited to halt a crossing,
  three records ordered into one register, a copied valve page, store marks
  beside a missing watch, or rival forecasts joined on appeal. Move or compress
  missing metadata where needed; do not make every source announce a
  `rendering` in the same position.
- **Collateral risk:** provenance and unknowns must remain explicit. Do not
  invent names, dates, institutions, native titles, or a faux quotation from an
  archive merely to manufacture voice.
- **Dependency:** source ownership and situated-knowledge review constrain the
  retained limits.
- **Confidence:** High; the construction recurs in five of seven Public source
  notes with closely parallel content and placement.

### AIP38-02 — Institutional repair repeatedly pays a polished moral invoice

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** final substantive sections of `After the High Windtrap Broke`,
  `Who Kept the Heat`, `The Missing Relief Cohort`, `The Closed Lift Appeal`,
  and `What the Three Ledgers Kept`.
- **Evidence:** the Windtrap passes inspection, then the keeper's contracts do
  not return; the heat remains on, then the ward pays the levy and fugitives
  remain uncertain; Shield Levy creates receipts and handoff protections, then
  leaves claims for seed, dwellings, and disabled workers unpaid; Closed Lift
  corrects court practice, then leaves work and housing uncompensated; Three
  Ledgers settles custody, then closes with a four-party memory chorus and the
  thesis that divided paper keeps one office from finishing the argument.
- **Effect:** material aftermath matters, but its repeated position and cadence
  turn it into a house signature: competence or reform is immediately balanced
  by an ethical remainder, then certified as complicated. The reader begins to
  anticipate the invoice instead of feeling the particular loss.
- **Repair direction:** keep the costs. Vary their dramatic work. Let a loss
  interrupt the procedure earlier, let one surviving object or claimant close
  a page, or end on a source-specific action without adding a sentence that
  interprets it. The five histories do not all need a corrected-rule paragraph
  followed by an unresolved-compensation paragraph.
- **Collateral risk:** do not turn any settlement into a clean victory, erase
  missing remedy, or imply that technical success cancels dispossession. Do not
  replace the present cadence with five clipped tragic aphorisms.
- **Dependency:** historical and material reviewers own whether the residual
  costs remain sufficiently clear.
- **Confidence:** High; both the semantic move and its location recur across
  five institution-focused histories.

### AIP38-03 — Survey narration stages nuance through serial correction

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** especially `Relics and Ruins`, paragraphs beginning `Discovery
  makes these claims visible`, `Their rites are not empty theatre`, `Answers
  remain incomplete`, `A compact names`, and `The best compact`; also `Peoples
  and Polities` under `Peoples outside the great magical polities`, and `Magic
  and Miracles` under `What witnesses can know`.
- **Evidence:** `Relics and Ruins` repeatedly uses `It does not erase them`,
  `not empty theatre`, `not universal fear or one theory ... It is
  preservation...`, `It does not turn non-signatories into scenery`, and `does
  not eliminate betrayal or error. It makes...`. The peoples survey says lack
  of recognized magic `does not make them one people, politically neutral,
  resistant to miracles, or naturally suited to ordinary labor`. The magic
  survey opens its limit with `Visible effect is not complete explanation` and
  ends with evidence made `legible` inside or outside a `frame`.
- **Effect:** the narrator appears to rebut an invisible simplistic reader at
  regular intervals. Nuance becomes a performed correction rather than a
  property of the examples, and the workshop's boundary vocabulary begins to
  sound like Kalsa's universal prose register.
- **Repair direction:** state the live relation directly where an object or
  actor already supplies it. Reserve antithesis for passages with a real
  opponent, such as dialogue, doctrine, or a named legal formula. In survey
  narration, prefer what the compact binds, what the armed party can take, or
  what the aftermark fails to identify over an abstract false premise followed
  by its correction.
- **Collateral risk:** keep necessary scope limits and the distinction between
  evidence and explanation. `The table is not a table`, Tavi's speech, and the
  schools' argumentative dialogue are voice-bearing uses of negation and should
  not be normalized away.
- **Dependency:** none for the prose defect; lore owners constrain the positive
  statements used in repair.
- **Confidence:** High; this is a dense local cluster in `Relics and Ruins` and
  recurs in two adjacent surveys.

### AIP38-04 — The world survey advertises breadth with catalogues and repeated
`A ... may` frames

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `World/Kalsa`, especially `A living high-fantasy world`; also
  `Gods and the Dead` under `Covenant and submission` and the opening of
  `Peoples and Polities`.
- **Evidence:** `Kalsa` gives a ten-verb life catalogue (`plant, haul, study,
  marry...`) and then four equally weighted examples beginning `A household
  may`, `A clerk may`, `A healer may`, and `A worker may`. `Gods and the Dead`
  gives three more consecutive social types beginning `A household may`, `A
  child may`, and `A defeated people may`. `Peoples and Polities` introduces
  belonging through eight coordinated categories before any particular person
  appears.
- **Effect:** the pages sound as though they are demonstrating complete setting
  coverage. Different lives become examples in a pitch rather than uneven
  glimpses of a world, and the repeated sentence weights produce the tireless
  essayist rhythm named in the critic brief.
- **Repair direction:** keep the breadth but break the exhibition cadence. One
  concrete sequence can carry several domains; another example can be shorter,
  stranger, or omitted. Vary grammatical subjects and sentence lengths instead
  of replacing one catalogue with another.
- **Collateral risk:** do not narrow Kalsa to heroic delvers, elite magical
  institutions, or a single settlement. The reader still needs ordinary work,
  family, worship, coercion, and technical knowledge in view.
- **Dependency:** reader navigation may care which bearings remain on the world
  landing page.
- **Confidence:** High for the repeated-frame defect; medium on the exact
  amount to cut because the overview still must range widely.

### AIP38-05 — Evidentiary workshop dialect has diffused beyond the offices
that earn it

- **Priority:** Optional
- **Location:** narration across the five World surveys and the institutional
  Sources, most audibly in `Magic and Miracles` (`evidence legible`, `outside
  its frame`), `Peoples and Polities` (`records make their land legible`),
  `Relics and Ruins` (`preservation, bounded tests, witnessed decisions`), and
  the repeated source formulas `the records/marks established`, `the account
  preserves`, and `the claim survived`.
- **Evidence:** a raw word-family scan of the thirteen non-index Public notes
  finds 47 uses of `record*`, 38 of `claim*`, 19 of `preserve*`, 8 of
  `authority*`, 6 of `material*`, and 4 of `bounded*`. Counts are not a verdict:
  records and claims are real setting objects. The defect is their recurrence
  in the same analytic syntax across Ju'onai schools, Papsenai store workers,
  Selza'a compacts, divine succession, Jamnai heatworks, ruins, and the neutral
  world narrator.
- **Effect:** culturally distinct practices are filtered through a common
  review-board diction. The precise terms stop marking an office's way of
  knowing and become the author's default connective tissue.
- **Repair direction:** trace and keep technical terms where a school, court,
  compact, office, or source form owns them. Elsewhere trade a few abstractions
  for the thing handled or the act performed: a copied page, a store mark, a
  sealed door, a worker's warning, grain moved, a body withheld. This is a
  selective diction pass, not a synonym purge.
- **Collateral risk:** `claim`, `record`, `material inspection`, and `bounded`
  sometimes name exact procedures. Removing them everywhere would blur
  authority and produce decorative fantasy language.
- **Dependency:** culture and situated-knowledge review should decide which
  registers are locally owned.
- **Confidence:** Medium; the diffusion is audible, but many individual tokens
  are legitimate and require sentence-level judgment.

### AIP38-06 — Uncanny incidents repeatedly pass through the same managed-
ambiguity machine

- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Who Kept the Heat`, the paragraphs beginning `Rhythmic knocks
  sounded` and `Material inspection upheld`; recurring in `After the High
  Windtrap Broke`, `The Closed Lift Appeal`, `Magic and Miracles` under
  `Household and route testimony`, and more faintly in `What the Three Ledgers
  Kept`.
- **Evidence:** Ash-Valve supplies an uncanny event, a believer's reading, an
  opponent's rational counterreading, a neutral appeal that keeps both, an
  independent material inspection, and the closing correction that the knocks
  drew attention but `had not answered the technical question`. Broken
  Windtrap repeats the sequence with hostile Luck, a prophet who finds bias but
  refuses a bearer, corrosion, and a finding that assigns repair without
  deciding Luck. Closed Lift pairs opposed forecasts and a Luck accusation with
  brake fatigue, then preserves forecast pressure while denying that the
  reports identify a bearer. The magic survey summarizes the Windtrap again as
  prophecy kept beside corroded metal, followed by `it did not identify a
  bearer`.
- **Effect:** each instance is defensible alone. In accumulation, the uncanny
  starts to feel processed through a reliable authorial safety device: every
  believer receives a skeptic, every dispute receives a neutral container,
  engineering resolves exactly its allotted question, and a final sentence
  assures the reader that metaphysics and material cause remain properly
  separated. The symmetry domesticates wonder and makes culturally different
  arguments share one adjudicative rhythm.
- **Repair direction:** preserve both the unexplained knocks and the inspection
  that upheld the steward. Remove at least one intermediate piece of symmetry
  or explanation where the source can carry the distinction without it. Across
  the vault, let some uncanny events alter action without receiving a paired
  rational countervoice; let some technical findings stand without a narrator
  stating what they did not prove; and let interested keepers preserve a
  dispute without the prose itself becoming a neutral bench.
- **Collateral risk:** do not make the knocks prove the valve warning, erase
  the independent inspection, declare hostile Luck absent, or collapse
  metaphysical uncertainty into mechanical ignorance. `At the Basin Table`
  reaches ambiguity through a scene and should not be stripped of its knock;
  `Four Readings` is explicitly a staged teaching dialogue and earns its
  chorus.
- **Dependency:** situated-knowledge review constrains who may retain each
  reading; material review constrains the independent inspection and corrosion
  findings.
- **Confidence:** High; the full six-stage architecture appears in Ash-Valve
  and close variants recur in two other institutional histories plus their
  survey summary.

## Pattern or dependency map

- **Source mould:** archival waiver -> named institutional mechanism ->
  evidence reconciliation -> corrected practice -> unpaid or unknowable
  remainder. The five case histories vary in facts more than in rhetorical
  movement.
- **Managed-ambiguity mould:** uncanny occurrence -> believer -> skeptic ->
  preserving record -> material finding -> explicit limit on what the finding
  proves. Ash-Valve makes the whole chain visible; Windtrap and Closed Lift
  reuse most of it.
- **Survey mould:** broad proposition -> catalogue of cases -> correction of a
  possible overreading -> abstract limit. This is strongest in `Relics and
  Ruins` and the closing sections of `Magic and Miracles`.
- **Healthy counterexamples:** `At the Basin Table` trusts scene, interruption,
  objects, and silence. `Four Readings` trusts interested speakers to expose
  limits. Low Sere's place note trusts rooms, water, ash, tools, and named
  pressure. Those pages show how the same distinctions can survive without a
  narrator continually certifying them.

## Tempting edit to resist

Do not run a global ban on lists, negation, `record`, `claim`, or `bounded`.
Keep the Relics living-claims inventory, the delving compact's actual terms,
the Gods opening's incantatory verbs, the teaching dialogue's corrections, and
Low Sere's physical inventories. A lexical cleanup would merely install a
dumber house style.

## Ranked actions

1. Break the managed-ambiguity machine while preserving genuine uncertainty
   and independent material findings (`AIP38-06`).
2. Break the five-source provenance and corrected-procedure/enduring-harm
   moulds without losing limits or costs (`AIP38-01`, `AIP38-02`).
3. Remove serial correction theatre from survey narration and vary the repeated
   overview frames (`AIP38-03`, `AIP38-04`).
4. During those sentence-level repairs, demote workshop diction only where no
   in-world institution owns it (`AIP38-05`).
