# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Pass with findings.** All nine permitted Public files match their raw SHA-1
hashes. Kalsa reads as a world where futures crowd because people act on them:
prophets, shamans, Channelers, divine intermediaries, officials, workers, and
ordinary households alter one another's reachable outcomes without gaining a
single shared explanation. The sources are concrete, situated, spoiler-safe,
and rich in play choices. The main weakness is presentation: the Public index
has accumulated a long flat shelf of arguments, and several otherwise distinct
sources end in the same boundary-enforcing cadence. A small route repair and a
small prose cut would restore momentum.

The root consent gate was not included in this blind packet, so this report does
not certify it.

## Reader trace

I understand Kalsa as permanent twilight organized around dangerous inherited
works and social practices for acting on uncertain futures. Magic does not
erase material causality: grain, seed, routes, shelters, lifts, stores, crews,
records, and bodies remain the means through which an anticipated future can be
reached or lost. Different practices sense and change different pressures, and
their successful local corrections can collide.

The pages I most want to follow are `The Narrow Shield`, where a central refuge
is saved by releasing a road and fields; `The Road That Returned`, where an
imperial road becomes local warning, disputed wages, and surviving damage; and
`At the Second Gate`, which hands the reader a sealed report and a live delivery
problem rather than explaining the answer.

I can imagine escorting the grain, deciding who hears the report before the
bell, bargaining with the understore key holder, preserving a changed order,
recovering incompatible bridge records, reopening a depot, or contesting who
enters a shelter claim. The memorable images are the refuge too small for an
army plan, the line where a shield becomes weather, numbered road stones that
can be warning or imperial claim, the miracle holding an empty bridge, and the
sealed comparison waiting outside Reed Ward.

Attention fell in the latter half of `Public/index.md#Read another argument`,
where individually strong invitations become a long unranked catalogue. I also
began anticipating the final sentence of several sources: the records disagree,
no owner receives the whole answer, and the missing fact remains missing. Those
limits are important, but their repeated delivery makes the narrator briefly
more visible than the cultures carrying them.

## Protected successes

- `At the Second Gate` is an excellent direct-entry page. It supplies a clear
  charge, named people, two usable routes, bounded authority, time pressure, and
  meaningful verbs—ask, carry, delay, disclose, touch—without revealing the
  sealed comparison.
- `The Narrow Shield` makes interacting magic spatial and bodily. Central
  shelter survives; road, slopes, seed, dwellings, stored work, injuries, and
  separation bear the narrowing. “The line beyond which the shield became
  weather” deserves protection.
- `The Empty Bridge Vigil` lets an undeniable miracle remain a material and
  ethical failure without declaring the god false or the Channeler omniscient.
  Bridge, spill, shelters, missing dead, and jurisdiction carry the dispute.
- Difficult names gain meaning through use. Sunwall, Circle, Sarxe, Papsenai,
  dimna, and Houses of Attestation do not require a glossary before the reader
  can follow what their holders do.
- Every permitted source stays within Public and offers a return route. No link
  inspected here crosses the spoiler boundary.

## Findings

### REN39-01 — The argument shelf has lost reader hierarchy

- **Finding ID:** REN39-01
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/index.md#Read another argument`, especially the
  sequence from `The Three Orders at Sunwall` through `At the Second Gate`.
- **Evidence:** After the Low Sere invitation, five surveys, and six established
  source invitations, the index presents six more full-paragraph entry choices
  in succession. The new sources share a discoverable concern—several actors
  crowding or redirecting one another's futures—but the index does not name a
  route through them or distinguish bearings, historical cases, and immediate
  play entry.
- **Effect:** Each blurb is attractive alone, but the section becomes a
  catalogue. Attention and recall flatten precisely where the new material is
  strongest; a casual reader has no obvious next link after the first few.
- **Repair direction:** Add one small hierarchy inside the existing index: a
  labeled route or short sequence that begins with `Magic and Miracles#When
  futures crowd`, offers two or three contrasting cases, and ends at `At the
  Second Gate`. Keep the remaining direct links available without promising a
  complete taxonomy.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not replace the sources with an exhaustive
  cross-style matrix, rank them as a compulsory syllabus, or demote Low Sere as
  the primary live-problem entry.
- **Dependency:** Public navigation owner only.
- **Confidence:** High; the attention drop follows the visible length and flat
  structure of the index, not hidden intent.

### REN39-02 — Distinct sources converge on one instructional closing voice

- **Finding ID:** REN39-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/Sources/The Three Orders at Sunwall.md`, final
  sentence; `The Grain at Two Gates.md`, final paragraph; `The Empty Bridge
  Vigil.md`, final sentence. The pattern is reinforced by several earlier
  “does not” and “could not” boundary statements in those sources.
- **Evidence:** The Three Orders closes by denying any copy the other orders;
  Grain closes by explaining what neither clause can prevent; Empty Bridge
  closes by explaining what joined records cannot supply or command. Each
  sentence correctly limits knowledge or authority, but the syntax repeatedly
  steps outside the record to certify the intended lesson.
- **Effect:** After several sources, their different custodians begin to sound
  like one editorial intelligence checking the same boxes. Mystery becomes
  slightly pre-digested, and the reader has less room to feel the unresolved
  residue before being told its boundary.
- **Repair direction:** Cut or concretize the minimum one or two weakest closing
  checks. Let the refuge capacity, spoiled grain, disputed wages, flooded
  approach, missing dead, and separated copies perform the limit. Preserve
  explicit scope where it is an actionable charge, especially in `At the
  Second Gate`.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not remove the culturally owned record differences,
  turn ambiguity into vagueness, or force every source into dialogue or
  literary pastiche.
- **Dependency:** AI-Isms and Prose for cadence; Culture and Situated Knowledge
  for record ownership.
- **Confidence:** High; the recurrence is visible across three otherwise
  distinct pages.

### REN39-03 — The live gate scenario lacks its nearest onward precedent

- **Finding ID:** REN39-03
- **Priority:** Optional
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/Sources/At the Second Gate.md`, final return line.
- **Evidence:** The page returns to `Magic and Miracles` or the Public index.
  `The Grain at Two Gates` is the permitted source that most directly develops
  the same readable components: a named grain promise, recipient, two cargo
  gates, a house comparison, a route crowded by informed actors, and preserved
  corrections. No link joins them.
- **Effect:** The strongest play entry ends by sending the reader back to an
  overview or the long catalogue. A reader interested in how similar deliveries
  have gone wrong must rediscover the adjacent precedent rather than follow the
  curiosity the scene just created.
- **Repair direction:** Add one clearly comparative onward link from the end of
  `At the Second Gate` to `The Grain at Two Gates`, labeled as another copied
  delivery record rather than an answer to the sealed report.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not imply that the two texts describe the same event,
  reveal Iven Tal's sealed comparison, or make precedent determine the current
  choice.
- **Dependency:** Public source navigation.
- **Confidence:** Medium; the index already provides an indirect route, so this
  is momentum lost rather than inaccessibility.

## Pattern or dependency map

`Public index -> long flat source shelf -> individual cross-pressure case ->
return to index or broad survey`.

The strongest prospective route is already latent:
`Magic and Miracles#When futures crowd -> Three Orders or Narrow Shield -> a
trade, road, or divine contrast -> At the Second Gate`. The missing handoff is
navigation, not lore.

## Tempting edit to resist

Do not disclose the sealed report, define what the first bell will do, decide
which gate is safe, identify a Luck bearer on the returning road, choose the
correct account of the empty bridge, reconcile the shield's death count, or
explain every magical interaction through one universal model. Those unknowns
are the source of inquiry and play.

## Ranked actions

1. **Essential:** None.
2. **High-value:** Give the new interaction sources one light reader hierarchy
   (REN39-01), then remove the minimum repeated editorial checksum identified in
   REN39-02.
3. **Optional:** Link the Second Gate scenario to the Two Gates precedent
   without implying shared facts or revealing the sealed report (REN39-03).
