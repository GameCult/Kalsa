# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Pass with findings.** The exact combined council manifest matches all 40 raw SHA-1 digests, including both council replacements. The candidate now carries history as path dependence rather than a date list: the six attested events create procedures, disputed memories, physical remains, unpaid claims, altered routes, and constituencies able to reuse the past. Absolute dates and mutual ordering remain honestly unresolved. Two high-value propagation gaps remain. `The Three Orders at Sunwall` states a durable cause of the Divided City's separated authorities without reaching the polity note that owns those authorities, and `The Narrow Shield` gives Sarxe route practice a remembered lesson without that memory reaching the Sarxe polity owner.

## Protected successes

- Preserve the single-event-owner pattern. Each event owns its factual sequence; neighboring notes inherit only practices, arguments, scars, and present claims. `The Road That Returned`, `The Grain at Two Gates`, `The Empty Bridge Vigil`, and `The Ash-Valve Refusal` all observe this boundary cleanly.
- Preserve incompatible memories that arise from different evidence rather than author-balanced opinion. Ju'onai road audits, Selza'a family recitations, bridge keepers, shelter households, cult ledgers, high-branch accounts, Hearth testimony, and tributary memory remain capable of disagreeing without one being declared the vault verdict.
- Preserve material path dependence. Numbered road stones become warning marks and imperial claims; abandoned depots carry wage and territorial disputes; copied forecast records alter later contracts; withdrawn miracle grants survive beside the successful bridge; the ash valve and custody record continue to support technical refusal.
- Preserve the unresolved chronology. The timeline fixes only relations the packet supports—such as the Three Orders occurring after Ju'onai tribute and during Terjamna contest—without manufacturing dates, a complete event order, or continuous institutional descent.

## Findings

### HCC39-01 — The Three Orders has no hidden present-day carrier in the Divided City

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Three Orders at Sunwall.md`, `Settlement` and `Consequences`; `Kalsa/Spoilers/Polities/Prophecy - The Divided City.md`, `A horizon no claimant owns`, `Tributary government`, and `Historical stress: the Closed Lift Appeal`; `Kalsa/Public/Sources/The Three Orders at Sunwall.md`, `What later readers kept`.
- **Evidence:** The event says later reports carried sponsor responses, Sunwall changes became independent interventions, and the episode became one reason the city preserves separate owners across forecast, judgment, material operation, and force. The Public source likewise says stewards and house comparers still copy the case. The Divided City note contains exactly that present separation, but its only historical carrier is the Closed Lift Appeal; it neither links nor attributes any part of the current arrangement to the Three Orders. No other polity or institution owner in the attested body carries the event by name.
- **Effect:** The event has excellent internal aftermath and reader-visible survival, but the hidden owner makes the matching present institution look fully formed. The causal bridge from contested occupation and Sunwall crisis to current divided authority stops at the event boundary, so future repairs can change the city without realizing they are cutting an adopted historical inheritance.
- **Repair direction:** Add one bounded consequence in the Divided City owner that links the Three Orders to the present separation of forecast, imperial force, and Sunwall operation, while preserving the Closed Lift Appeal as a distinct later or parallel pressure. Do not duplicate the sequence or decide how directly either episode created the Mixed Bench.
- **Collateral risk:** A careless repair could turn one episode into the constitutional founding of the city, settle the armistice chronology, or imply the Stewardship defeated both empires. The event establishes a remembered reason and procedure, not sole origin or civic victory.
- **Dependency:** Institutional Causality only insofar as the present owner boundaries must remain intact; no institutional expansion is required.
- **Confidence:** High. The event and Public source explicitly claim a later practice, while the canonical polity owner contains the practice but no historical provenance.

### HCC39-02 — The Narrow Shield's Sarxe consequence stops in the event and Public source

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Narrow Shield.md`, `Settlement` and `Adventure consequences`; `Kalsa/Public/Sources/The Narrow Shield.md`, `What the order kept`; `Kalsa/Spoilers/Polities/Channeling - Sarxe.md`, `Saxfoldi as a mobile polity`, `An ordinary movement`, and `Static and mobile fractures`.
- **Evidence:** The event says Sarxe route practice remembers that accepting common command can prevent shared disaster while closing the movement by which a household meant to survive; its surviving witnesses can obtain standing through recovered copies. Public gives this memory to “Sarxe route keepers.” The Sarxe polity fully explains how routes, common stores, delegates, boundaries, separated people, and refusal work after Ji'esti, but it does not carry the Narrow Shield or the historically earned tension between temporary shared command and household departure.
- **Effect:** The Papsenai institutional consequence reproduces through `Stormshield Circles`, while the Sarxe half becomes an isolated event anecdote. Present Sarxe route politics therefore bear Ji'esti strongly but do not visibly inherit this later cross-polity failure, despite the event assigning them a durable memory and an evidence-bearing constituency.
- **Repair direction:** Give the Sarxe polity one narrow historical consequence or cross-link: identify the route-keeper/household dispute over when a common signal may bind a convoy, what record preserves consent and expiry, and how separated witnesses can challenge the command afterward. Keep the underlying storm facts in the event owner.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not universalize one convoy's practice across all Sarxe, make Papsenai settlement binding on Saxfoldi, or imply that accepting shared command was either betrayal or the uniquely correct response.
- **Dependency:** None; the event already supplies the adopted fact and the Sarxe polity owns its present memory and practice.
- **Confidence:** High. Both hidden event and Public presentation name a continuing Sarxe memory whose canonical polity owner is silent.
