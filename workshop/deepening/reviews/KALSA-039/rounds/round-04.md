## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** Candidate 04 resolves both round-three findings and preserves all
earlier repairs. Sorcery now has a genuinely non-reporting temporal relation,
and the Ash-Valve Refusal lets its selected act fire, leave evidence, and fail
against a material configuration changed outside the champion's knowledge. The
Empty Bridge Vigil gives a God hierarchy a finite, useful, causally bounded
allocation which an unbound Channeler makes tragically mistimed without making
either act false.

The next defect is exposed by the Sorcery repair itself. “A desired departure”
now presses backward upon unconscious action, but no owner says whose desire
defines that departure or distinguishes the immediate selected result from the
larger command it is meant to serve. The Ash-Valve sequence consequently sits
between two readings that imply different mechanics and different personhood:
either the impossible valve opening selected a lever that could not reach it,
or the actually reachable broken collar was the selected departure and the
holder's larger goal was only the champion's mistaken interpretation. The
setting also now has enough historical examples to reveal a play projection
gap: its adventure consequences overwhelmingly ask parties to recover the
records left after oracle competition, rather than placing them inside a live
crowded horizon whose information and levers they can change.

## Findings

### OETC39-11 — Sorcery's temporal relation has no owner for the desired departure

- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos.md`, opening, **Hard
  constraints**, and **Observing Kaos**; `Kalsa/Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle
  Ecology.md`, **Differently shaped oracles**;
  `Kalsa/Spoilers/Polities/Sorcery - Jamnai and the Terjamna.md`, opening and
  **Office and branch life**; `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Ash-Valve Refusal.md`,
  **Diversion and refusal**.
- **Evidence:** The foundations now say that a “near desired departure” presses
  backward and selects a lever able to reach it, but do not say whether the
  desired state belongs to the embodied Sorcerer, their unconscious Beast, a
  bound God, a superior whose command created the conflict, or some interaction
  among them. That omission matters in Jamnai, where magical submission,
  ordinary oath, civil office, patron command, and coerced dependency are
  deliberately non-equivalent. In the Ash-Valve case, the champion acts “to
  make the order real.” If the selected desired departure is opening the main,
  the removed transfer pin means the existing local chain cannot carry the act
  to that result, contrary to the foundation's wording. If the selected
  departure is merely breaking the locking lug, that local result is reachable
  and occurs, but the note never distinguishes it from the holder's larger
  objective or says whose desire made that break the selected answer.
- **Effect:** This is not a vocabulary nicety. Goal ownership determines
  whether Sorcery is the bearer's oracle, a Beast's autonomous appetite, or a
  command hierarchy's involuntary actuator. Without it, submission can silently
  become mind control, patron orders can acquire magical authority the polity
  otherwise denies them, and a resolver cannot tell whether Sorcery failed,
  succeeded locally, or selected an invalid branch. The ambiguity weakens the
  setting's promise that bound identities remain and that social submission is
  not automatically metaphysical submission.
- **Repair direction:** In the mechanics owner, distinguish the immediate
  departure selected by the temporal relation from the larger aim, command, or
  explanation attached to it. Name whose live orientation supplies that local
  aim and what happens under coercion, divided intention, or a God hierarchy's
  routed potential, without solving all free-will metaphysics. In the Ash-Valve
  owner, identify the actually selected reachable departure and the mistaken
  higher-order assumption defeated by the missing pin. Let Jamnai traditions
  disagree over whether an answer in the motion serves the champion, branch,
  Beast, or superior; the backstage constraint must still be adjudicable.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not make Sorcery a morally protective refusal power,
  give the Gift access to hidden motives, let every conscious wish trigger it,
  or decide that magical submission erases the bearer's agency. Preserve its
  intermittent, unconscious, local, conflict-bound character and the
  distinction from Luck's survival attractor.
- **Dependency:** Mechanics and Causal Coherence should test the local-goal and
  reachability rule; Culture and Lived Difference should preserve Jamnai's
  interested interpretations rather than receiving the backstage answer.
- **Confidence:** High. The newly specified temporal transaction requires a
  desired result as an input, while every owner currently leaves the authority
  over that input implicit.

### OETC39-12 — Oracle ecology remains historical evidence more often than a live Ghostlight situation

- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `workshop/Direction and Constraints.md`, **Ghostlight knowledge
  and play boundary** and **Fundamental setting question**;
  `Kalsa/Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle Ecology.md`, **Ghostlight consequences**;
  the **Adventure consequences** sections of `The Ash-Valve Refusal`, `The
  Empty Bridge Vigil`, `The Grain at Two Gates`, `The Narrow Shield`, `The Road
  That Returned`, and `The Three Orders at Sunwall`.
- **Evidence:** The foundation gives a useful generic list: conceal a plan, free
  an oracle, sever divine communications, preserve an archive, move people
  outside a Luck-dominated route. The complete event hooks overwhelmingly
  begin after the oracular contest has ended. Parties recover ledgers, orders,
  route tokens, boundary markers, pay records, miracle-route evidence, or old
  infrastructure so that present claims can be judged. Reopening the Bridge
  approach may threaten the mill settlement, and recovering Narrow Shield
  records may affect a present order, but no complete owner places players
  inside a current crowded horizon with named active oracles, information
  custody, intended responses, material levers, expiry, and several reachable
  state changes.
- **Effect:** At the table, the setting's fundamental question can recede into
  archaeological and legal backstory. Players alter who can prove what about a
  past oracle collision more often than who can observe, coordinate, conceal,
  bind, escape, or keep open a future now. Record recovery is a strong Kalsa
  play mode, but it cannot be the main playable projection of a world inhabited
  by competing oracles.
- **Repair direction:** Give one current site, faction pressure, or event sequel
  a bounded live situation. Name the active question or target, which oracle
  shapes currently receive which evidence, which responses each patron intends,
  the material and communication levers players can change, what expires an
  observation, and two or more state changes that remain resolver-dependent.
  The party need not be magical: carrying, delaying, disclosing, moving,
  refusing, cutting, sheltering, or reopening can enter the causal field.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not build a universal oracle minigame, publish author
  terminology to players, script a correct solution, require every party to
  include a Gifted character, or turn one scene into a seven-Gift showcase.
  Preserve the existing historical hooks as evidence and consequence rather
  than replacing them.
- **Dependency:** Play Consequence should own scenario usability; a present
  dungeon, place, or faction owner should own the live actors and state rather
  than making `Oracle Ecology` a second campaign database.
- **Confidence:** High for the exact candidate. The mechanism-to-history chain
  is now strong enough that the repeated post-event shape of the playable hooks
  is a specific projection gap rather than a request for generic depth.

## Pattern or dependency map

`OETC39-11` is an engine and authority defect. It must be resolved before a
live Sorcery situation can be adjudicated without letting command, intention,
Beast appetite, and reachable local result blur together. `OETC39-12` is a
projection defect: the repaired engine and histories need one current owner
that turns information custody and material response into playable state. The
live situation need not use Sorcery, so the two repairs should not be forced
into one demonstration.

## Leave-alone guidance

- Leave Intuition, Gift frequencies, inheritance rates, the person-to-Beast
  relation, and the exact metaphysics of binding unresolved.
- Leave the Empty Bridge God's style, identity, thought, and ultimate judgment
  unresolved. The event already establishes everything the ecology needs: the
  hierarchy's evidence, allocation, finite result, outside intervention, and
  interested aftermath.
- Leave the Ash-Valve's shrine knocks disputed and its technical finding
  material. Goal ownership does not require identifying the dead or granting an
  aftermark access to private experience.
- Leave the archive and record-recovery hooks in place. The repair needs one
  live complement, not a purge of Kalsa's evidentiary play.
- Leave the Road unable to prove Luck. A live adventure need not solve an
  attractor that the world's institutions cannot directly observe.

## Tempting edit to resist

Do not declare that a Sorcerer's “true desire” overrides commands and then use
that as the moral key to every Jamnai conflict. That would replace one missing
authority with an infallible inner self, turn coercion into a magical lie
detector, and make Sorcery choose the author-approved person rather than a
bounded local outcome.

## Ranked actions

1. Specify who or what supplies Sorcery's immediate desired departure and
   repair the Ash-Valve distinction between local success and higher-order
   failure.
2. Adopt one present, bounded Ghostlight situation in which players can change
   the information, lever, response, or independence of active competing
   oracles.
