# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

Block. The hidden history is causally strong: shortage and a dead anchor create
a succession crisis; authorities with different material bases fight; the
settlement repairs only part of the damage; reformers and centralizers learn
opposite lessons. The factual event also remains correctly owned by `The Three
Ledgers Schism.md`. The break is between that history and the present reader
surface. Public presents divine fallibility and witness practice as timeless,
partly through an unowned “cult schools” claim, while the neighboring hidden
owners cite a durable Schism precedent without yet delimiting how its records,
office lineages, or partisan memories travelled. The result risks turning a
good event into an author-level explanation hovering over current institutions.

- `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Three Ledgers Schism.md` §§ “The initiating

## Findings

### HCC31-01

- **Finding ID:** HCC31-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, opening paragraphs
  before § “Covenant and submission,” especially “Some cult schools call this
  the limit of delegated attention”; §§ “Covenant and submission” and “The
  unquiet dead.”
- **Evidence:** Public describes current disagreement about divine knowledge,
  covenant origins, and mortuary custody, but supplies no formation crisis,
  inherited precedent, partisan memory, or situated constituency behind those
  practices. The packet explicitly establishes that the referenced “cult
  schools” have no hidden inhabitant owner. The adopted Three Ledgers history,
  with its conflicting memories and incomplete repair, does not reach the
  reader surface at all.
- **Effect:** Divine witness practice reads as timeless good sense shared by a
  generic learned class. Readers cannot see why separated records exist, who
  paid for the lesson, why some cults reject it, or how a present succession
  might reopen the conflict. Public loses the strongest evidence that these
  institutions were made under pressure rather than issued fully formed.
- **Repair direction:** Replace the unowned school claim with a bounded,
  situated historical carrier derived from the Schism's adopted facts and
  present institutional memories. One Public source or compact passage should
  show at least two constituencies remembering the crisis differently and one
  present practice or dispute inherited from it. It need not identify the
  cult, god, place, date, or true manifestation.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not create a single correct public history, a
  continent-wide reform movement, or a universal three-ledger custom. Public
  should carry selective memory and disagreement, not the author event note in
  cleaner clothes.
- **Dependency:** Public voice, institutional ownership, and spoiler safety.
- **Confidence:** High. The hidden event supplies the required causal history;
  the current Public note contains no owned route to it.

### HCC31-02

- **Finding ID:** HCC31-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Spoilers/Events/The Three Ledgers Schism.md` §§ “Path
  dependence” and “Unresolved”; `God-Cult Covenants.md` § “The Three Ledgers
  consequence”; `The Potential Economy.md` § “The Three Ledgers consequence”;
  `Death and Binding Law.md` § “The Three Ledgers consequence”; `Soiru'i
  Sects.md` § “The Three Ledgers consequence.”
- **Evidence:** The event says later reform and centralizing cults learned from
  the crisis, and each neighboring owner cites its precedent as a durable cause
  of present practice. The event simultaneously leaves unresolved which
  copies, sites, offices, and successor traditions survive. `Soiru'i Sects.md`
  supplies the strongest plausible memory carriers through its divided sect
  traditions, but the broader route by which covenant, allocation, and
  mortuary bodies recognize or reject the precedent is not bounded.
- **Effect:** Path dependence is asserted across several institutions without
  an inspectable transmission path. A reader may infer that one unnamed event
  standardized cult administration across Kalsa, contrary to the event's own
  authority limits. Selective archives and regional discontinuity have no
  place to act.
- **Repair direction:** Bound the precedent to carriers rather than expanding
  the event. Clarify that its lessons persist where duplicate records,
  covenant or mortuary office lineages, sect networks, household coalitions, or
  successor traditions carried and recognized them; elsewhere the event may
  be unknown, contested, or only analogical. Give at least one existing
  constituency a characteristic memory and use of the precedent, while
  preserving the unresolved survival of any particular original copy or site.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not invent a named transcontinental archive, council,
  church, or reform federation merely to connect the notes. A thin, partisan
  transmission boundary is historically stronger than a new institution.
- **Dependency:** Canon and provenance ownership across the event and the four
  institution notes.
- **Confidence:** High. The causal claims recur explicitly, while their
  transmission and scope remain unresolved.

## Pattern or dependency map

Failed transport and harvest + anchor death -> contradictory rites and urgent
succession -> merged record transfers authority -> coalitions mobilize shrine,
stores, body, witnesses, sanctuary, and force -> partial settlement separates
custody -> reformers and centralizers preserve opposing lessons -> **missing
bounded carrier into present institutional and Public memory**.

The pressure capable of changing the system again already exists: another dead
anchor, false reserve, dangerous body, or emergency merger can reactivate the
same conflict. Repair should make the inherited arguments legible, not invent a
new crisis.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not resolve the Schism's cult, god, location, date, divine style, or true
  manifestation to make the history feel concrete. Its material sequence and
  partisan memories are already concrete.
- Do not make the Schism the origin of all separated covenant, allocation, and
  mortuary practice. Some institutions can inherit its precedent, reject it,
  forget it, or arrive at similar boundaries through other local crises.

## Ranked actions

1. **Essential:** Give Public one situated, partisan route from the Schism's
   adopted history into current divine-witness practice; remove the unowned
   timeless-school formulation.
2. **High-value:** Bound the Schism precedent to existing record, office, sect,
   household, and successor carriers so cross-note path dependence does not
   imply universal diffusion.
3. **Optional:** Add no new event. Existing succession, shortage, and custody
   pressures are sufficient to make future change possible once their
   inherited arguments are visible.
