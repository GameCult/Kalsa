# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Pass with findings.** The candidate has a complete material chain for divine
practice. Households supply work, produce, information, bodies, and care;
cults maintain stores, roads, shrines, burial grounds, archives, transport, and
trained workers; miracle allocation depends upon ordinary inputs; concentrated
work creates predatory magical pressure; interruption and dispersal move aid
and risk unevenly; audits, mutual aid, and appeals create recovery paths. The
one high-value defect is at the Public boundary: `Gods and the Dead` names
dangerous concentration and restraint but omits the hidden, already-owned
consequence that restraint relocates healing, travel, funeral, and care burdens.
No new material institution or ecological mechanism is required.

## Protected successes

- `Gods and the Dead#Covenant and submission` gives the Public page a compact
  but real exchange: household work and offerings support healing, burial,
  defence, and food, while cults require cloth, guards, messengers, shelters,
  stores, and trained labor. The ruined-shrine image makes material capacity,
  not architecture or piety alone, decide whether promises can be kept.
- `The Potential Economy#Material leverage` refuses miracle-as-free-output.
  Seed, soil, water, workers, bodies, medicine, transport, tools, and reachable
  conditions remain inputs, while granaries, hostels, workshops, roads,
  suppliers, and burial grounds let divine access become ordinary provision.
- `Death and Binding Law#Formation, training, and support` follows the bodily
  work of death through washing water, fuel, cloth, preservation, burial space,
  guards, transport, copies, food for mourners, witness support, and several
  possible payers. It also shows how delay and fees make poor families surrender
  claims earlier.
- `The Three Ledgers Schism` converts failed transport, poor harvest, stores,
  carriers, healers, interrupted distribution, displaced witnesses, and outer-
  settlement deprivation into coalition power. The hardship produces choices
  and leverage rather than serving as an automatic misery ledger.

## Findings

### MLE31-01

- **Finding ID:** MLE31-01
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md#Hungry holy places`,
  especially “Many practitioner lineages therefore limit concentrated work
  near such places. Desperate ones call the danger proof that the miracle
  matters.”
- **Evidence:** The Public page establishes the source and hazard: repeated
  miracles gather power around old deaths, failed successions, and forgotten
  shrines. It then jumps directly to generic restraint. The hidden owners carry
  the missing remainder. `God-Cult Covenants#Leakage, restraint, and holy
  geography` says dispersal can move reliable healing away from poor districts
  with the most unquiet dead. `The Potential Economy#Allocation and reserve`
  and `#Organized allocation factions` show stewards and leakage wardens
  choosing brief local grants, interruptions, and route closures while poor
  districts may be unable to travel. `Death and Binding Law` shows that funeral
  work itself consumes transport, fuel, cloth, guarded space, and care.
- **Effect:** Public readers receive a supernatural hazard but not the landscape
  or routine it creates. “Limit concentrated work” has no visible patient who
  loses nearby healing, mourner who must move a body, household that cannot
  travel, worker who closes a route, or settlement deciding whether present aid
  is worth a hungrier site later. The ecology becomes an atmospheric warning
  rather than a playable distribution problem.
- **Repair direction:** Add one bounded, situated material consequence to the
  existing Public section. Show that dispersing, moving, or interrupting a rite
  can protect a place while shifting travel, burial, care, or missed relief onto
  particular households, and that immediate need can make continued use a real
  choice rather than mere superstition. Attribute the act to an existing role
  or local body; do not explain backstage thaumavore mechanics.
- **Collateral risk:** A careless repair could turn all miracle use into a
  pollution allegory, imply every shrine follows one closure law, or make poor
  people exist only as the invoice attached to spectacle. Preserve successful,
  abundant, and joyful miracles alongside the specific risk of concentration.
- **Dependency:** Public presentation and institutional authority must identify
  who may schedule, stop, move, or appeal a particular working. The material
  mechanism itself is already present in the canonical owners; it is not absent
  from canon.
- **Confidence:** High. The complete source → infrastructure → allocation →
  leakage → dispersal → uneven-access chain is explicit in the supplied hidden
  notes, and the exact broken handoff is the Public compression after the
  hazard.

## Pattern or dependency map

The evidenced chain is:

household work, produce, offerings, bodies, and information
→ cult stores, roads, shrines, burial grounds, workers, medicine, and transport
→ stewards and local custodians schedule a materially possible intervention
→ repeated concentration attracts competing manifestations and drains control
→ rites are staggered, sites dispersed, routes closed, or funerals restricted
→ poor, remote, dead-burdened, sick, and travel-limited households absorb more
distance, delay, missed relief, and care work
→ mutual-aid stores, copied promises, audit, appeal, relocation, or continued
risky use become available responses.

All links in this chain exist in author canon. Public currently presents the
chain through concentration and then compresses the distribution and recovery
half out of view. That compression is the finding; the underlying ecology does
not need expansion.

The ordinary welfare-store handoff was also inspected. `God-Cult Covenants`
places household and guild custodians where offerings and complaints become
food allotment or burial access; `The Potential Economy` scopes appointments to
material stores and requires emergency aid during review; the Schism supplies
store workers and a concrete failure. Local titles and store constitutions are
appropriately unresolved rather than a missing universal granary office.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not assign a mundane price, injury, or depleted village to every miracle.
  Kalsa benefits from genuine divine abundance and spectacle; this magic needs
  the specific consequences of concentrated routing, not a generic conservation
  tax stapled to wonder.
- Do not invent a universal leakage-cleanup order or standardized contaminated
  zone. Existing cult, mortuary, household, site, and polity owners should keep
  their local authority and incompatible needs.

## Ranked actions

3. Leave the hidden welfare, mortuary, Schism, and magical-residue mechanisms
