# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The Public surface is readable, spoiler-safe, and compelling around Low Sere, but it does not yet deliver the encounter named by this pass. `Kalsa/Public/index.md` promises gods who bargain for worship and service; the reader then reaches a survey containing no named god, cult, covenant, divine witness, supplicant, allocation decision, or inhabited source. I understand the rules for distrusting divine claims, but I never meet anyone making or answering one.

The dead fare better. Tavi Kes, the Ash Names, the prohibition on a great blessing, and the recorded but uninterpreted knock show inhabitants acting under uncertainty. What remains absent is the equivalent lived contact with a god and its material cult.

## Protected successes

- `Kalsa/index.md` maintains an explicit consent gate. Every followed Public route remained outside `Spoilers/`, and all listed Public Git blob hashes matched the blind packet.
- `Low Sere` and `At the Basin Table` demonstrate situated presentation at its strongest: named people disagree over custody, evidence, wages, injury, technical authority, and obligations to the dead without deciding what knocked below.
- `Gods and the Dead` protects valuable uncertainty. Gods are powerful but not established as all-knowing; covenant records do not prove divine acceptance; manifestations do not settle identity; local mortuary safeguards do not become universal law.
- Memorable images should survive repair: a god dying badly enough to poison a district, a divine protector missing the household cheating its storekeeper, a magnificent shrine without the stores to keep its promises, names forming in smoke, and a forgotten shrine that still answers.

## Findings

### REN31-01

- **Finding ID:** REN31-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/index.md`, opening promise “Gods bargain for worship and service”; `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, page entrance through `## Covenant and submission`
- **Evidence:** The landing page promises active bargaining. The destination explains divine limits, covenant categories, cult logistics, and evidentiary caution entirely through generalized examples. It names no god, cult, covenant party, divine servant, ledger, witness, disputed clause, or decision.
- **Effect:** Gods remain authorially described setting machinery rather than beings encountered through inhabitants. A player cannot imagine whom they might petition, serve, investigate, refuse, or accuse. “Gods bargain” functions as an attractive premise with no honest onward fulfillment.
- **Repair direction:** Add one bounded Public case or source, linked clearly from `Gods and the Dead`, in which named inhabitants deal with a named god or cult. Let the case show what was directly witnessed, what arrived through servants, what the god claims, what a covenant record establishes, and what remains disputed. One coherent case is enough.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not make the case a universal pantheon entry, settle whether the god is truthful, establish divine ontology, or imply that one culture's covenant practice governs Kalsa.
- **Dependency:** Public presentation owner plus the canonical owner of the selected divine relationship; divine institutional criticism may test the case's material consequences.
- **Confidence:** High; no followed Public page supplies the promised encounter.

### REN31-02

- **Finding ID:** REN31-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, final paragraph of `## Covenant and submission`
- **Evidence:** God-cults are said to require food, cloth, guards, burial ground, clerks, clergy, messengers, shelters, and mortal aid, but no inhabitant allocates scarce provision or loses access when a cult cannot keep its promise. `Four Readings of a Broken Sluice` mentions possible temple grain only as one conditional answer in a teaching exercise.
- **Effect:** The reader understands that miracles have infrastructure but not how divine favor competes with hunger, burial, defence, or ordinary aid. Cult materiality reads as a correct explanatory principle rather than a playable pressure.
- **Repair direction:** In the same bounded case proposed for REN31-01, make one miracle or cult promise require an allocation with a named beneficiary, refused claimant, responsible office or household, and visible cost.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not turn every miracle into a modern administrative hearing or suggest all cults allocate service through the same offices.
- **Dependency:** Same owner path as REN31-01; material-culture and institutional review may test feasibility.
- **Confidence:** High; the Public surface lists inputs but presents no allocation event.

### REN31-03

- **Finding ID:** REN31-03
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, page voice across `## Covenant and submission` and `## The unquiet dead`
- **Evidence:** The prose repeatedly uses broad categories—people, households, defeated peoples, Kalsans, ancestral households, release sects, frightened rulers, and some mortuary offices—without locating the claims in a named tradition or document. Low Sere's named testimony immediately feels more inhabited by contrast.
- **Effect:** The page occasionally becomes instructional and morally pre-digested: the reader receives the correct distinctions before seeing why a particular culture made them, rejected them, or paid for them. Cultural asymmetry is described but not experienced.
- **Repair direction:** Use the new source to anchor the survey's distinctions in identifiable speakers, records, and local practice. Revise only enough surrounding prose to route the reader toward that case; do not multiply examples for representational balance.
- **Collateral risk:** A careless repair could produce an artificial consensus, force symmetrical viewpoints, or bury the useful survey under fictional-document scaffolding.
- **Dependency:** Situated Public presentation and cultural owner of the selected case.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high; the cumulative voice effect is clear, though one strong source may resolve it without broad revision.

### REN31-04

- **Finding ID:** REN31-04
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, footer navigation
- **Evidence:** The onward links lead to `Magic and Miracles`, `Relics and Ruins`, and `Low Sere`. Those pages deepen general evidence, ruin claims, and death-adjacent caution, but none leads to an inhabited divine covenant or cult decision.
- **Effect:** Curiosity about the page's central subject enters a loop of surveys and the Low Sere ambiguity. There is no honest route from explanation to encounter.
- **Repair direction:** Once REN31-01 has a Public source, make it the page's explicit lived-example route. The existing cross-links can remain secondary.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not imply that the source is required reading or the representative model for every god-cult.
- **Dependency:** Navigation owner; dependent on REN31-01.
- **Confidence:** High; the available links were followed and do not supply the missing encounter.

## Pattern or dependency map

- `Public/index` makes the promise: gods bargain, protect, and demand.
- `Gods and the Dead` supplies careful survey distinctions but no named event.
- Its footer routes to more surveys or Low Sere.
- Low Sere successfully shows how inhabitants face possible dead presence, but not a divine bargain.
- Therefore one situated divine source can resolve the central experience, the allocation gap, and the navigation loop without expanding into an encyclopedia.

Reader attention rose around the opening claims and the images of divine ignorance, weak cults, and unquiet death. It fell during the generalized covenant and mortuary category lists because no person or place carried them. I wanted to follow a covenant clerk, shrine household, hostile witness, refused supplicant, or depleted cult store; none was available.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not add a complete pantheon, universal covenant system, miracle-price table, glossary, or authorial answer about what gods and returning dead truly are. Do not explain the Low Sere knock. The needed repair is contact with one culturally owned dispute, not backstage certainty.

## Ranked actions

1. **Essential:** Publish and link one named, situated divine case that joins testimony, covenant, and unresolved divine claim.
2. **High-value:** Let that same case expose one material miracle-allocation decision, then route to it directly from `Gods and the Dead`.
3. **Optional:** No additional orientation apparatus is warranted until the single lived route is tested. Preserve the current consent gate, Low Sere opening, difficult names, and metaphysical uncertainty.
