# Critic Report

## Concise diagnosis

**Block.** The hidden institutional body can reproduce distinct covenant,
allocation, mortuary, and sect practices. Each has workers, formation, material
support, records, succession, discipline, and bounded appeal. The Public account
nonetheless replaces that plural body with otherwise unowned “cult schools” and
then says priests, prophets, champions, and enemies learn one source-testing
discipline without showing which institution teaches, records, or carries it.
That is a named doctrine pretending to be a mechanism. The repair should expose
the existing role-specific evidence chain, not manufacture a universal divine
college or truth court.

## Protected successes

- `God-Cult Covenants`, `The Potential Economy`, and `Death and Binding Law`
  divide covenant origin, miracle allocation, and mortuary evidence while
  reproducing each office through distinct recruitment, training, support,
  records, succession, discipline, and appeal. Repair must preserve those
  separate authorities rather than make one witness practice owner of all
  divine disputes.
- `The Three Ledgers Schism` makes separation materially causal. The successor
  has shrine, champions, and recognized archive; stewards have stores,
  beneficiaries, and local delegates; mortuary workers have body, testimony,
  and sanctuary routes. None can substitute for the others, and the settlement
  does not become universal law.
- `Soiru'i Sects#Championing` already provides an excellent bounded precedent:
  it distinguishes the living person's acts, later promises, observed
  manifestations, sect interpretation, and resources moved in the dead name.
  It does not let a good record decide identity or metaphysics.
- `Gods and the Dead#Covenant and submission` preserves the ordinary welfare
  bargain and the distinction among voluntary promise, inherited attachment,
  and conquest without leaking the backstage divine taxonomy.

## Findings

### IC31-01

- **Finding ID:** IC31-01
- **Priority:** Essential
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, opening paragraphs,
  especially “Some cult schools call this the limit of delegated attention.”
- **Evidence:** The packet explicitly identifies the phrase as having no hidden
  inhabitant owner. The candidate contains cult formation, patron schools, and
  plural doctrines, but no institution or lineage that owns this named model,
  teaches it, preserves its adverse cases, or can be held responsible for its
  characteristic error. The only occurrence of “delegated attention” in the
  candidate is the Public assertion itself.
- **Effect:** Readers are handed a frictionless scholarly consensus in two
  anonymous camps. The phrase smuggles the author mechanism of hierarchical
  perception into cultural speech, while “cult schools” supplies no entrants,
  teachers, archives, material base, discipline, or relationship to the cult
  offices that would make such a tradition persist.
- **Repair direction:** Remove or replace the unowned school/doctrine claim.
  Attribute the comparison to existing inhabitant owners—covenant witnesses,
  potential stewards or auditors, mortuary witnesses, sect memory keepers, and
  hostile record keepers—and limit each to the evidence its institution can
  actually produce. If a doctrine remains, adopt it in an existing canonical
  owner with a reproduced teaching and adverse-record path; do not create a
  school merely to save the phrase.
- **Collateral risk:** A careless repair could paste the backstage hierarchy
  into clergy speech, invent named cults or a continent-wide curriculum, or
  flatten real disagreement into one approved skeptical method.
- **Dependency:** Subject ownership/provenance and Public reader presentation;
  `Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores` remains the forbidden author-level
  mechanics owner.
- **Confidence:** High. The packet names the fault and exact search finds no
  canonical inhabitant owner for the term.

### IC31-02

- **Finding ID:** IC31-02
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md`, final sentence of the
  opening; `God-Cult Covenants#Records that make a cult governable`; `The
  Potential Economy#No common measure` and `#Audit, discipline, and appeal`;
  `Death and Binding Law#The death inventory`; compare `Soiru'i
  Sects#Championing`.
- **Evidence:** Public says priests, prophets, champions, and enemies learn to
  distinguish what a god witnessed, what reached it through servants, and what
  it insists is true. The hidden owners preserve many necessary pieces:
  covenant witnesses and intermediary names, petitions and observed outcomes,
  bodily and manifestation witnesses, and rival copies. They do not yet state
  a legible handoff showing how a divine utterance keeps its claimed speaker,
  intermediary route, observed manifestation, institutional interpretation,
  and material consequence distinct as it crosses those records. The Soiru'i
  record already demonstrates that distinction locally.
- **Effect:** The evidence body is strong inside each ledger but can lose
  provenance at the seam between them. A clergy interpretation can arrive in an
  allocation dispute as divine refusal, or a manifestation can enter a death
  inventory already carrying the sect's identity verdict. Separated custody
  then preserves three clean records of the same collapsed claim.
- **Repair direction:** Give each existing owner the smallest role-specific
  source distinction it needs and link the handoffs. Covenant practice should
  preserve who claimed the message and through which intermediary; allocation
  practice should separate claimed divine reason from observed delivery and
  material outcome; mortuary practice should preserve manifestation and
  witness conditions separately from identity and meaning. Let comparison be
  performed by the forum already competent for the consequence. Do not create
  a common form, central archive, or universal witness office.
- **Collateral risk:** A shared schema could quietly reunify the three ledgers,
  while an overpowered “divine examiner” would let evidence competence become
  covenant, allocation, court, or mortuary jurisdiction.
- **Dependency:** Record/provenance review and the exact event boundary of `The
  Three Ledgers Schism`; no new Schism facts are needed.
- **Confidence:** High. Every component exists, but only the Soiru'i practice
  currently makes the full observation/interpretation/resource distinction
  explicit.

### IC31-03

- **Finding ID:** IC31-03
- **Priority:** High-value
- **Location:** `Kalsa/Public/World/Gods and the Dead.md#Hungry holy places`,
  especially “Many practitioner lineages therefore limit concentrated work
  near such places”; hidden counterparts in `God-Cult Covenants#Leakage,
  restraint, and holy geography`, `The Potential Economy#Allocation and
  reserve` and `#Organized allocation factions`, and `Death and Binding
  Law#Severance and refusal`.
- **Evidence:** Hidden owners divide the relevant acts: potential stewards
  schedule or disperse grants, leakage wardens observe and may control named
  sites or routes, mortuary workers may stop a hazardous rite, champions
  protect within a grant, and local owners still bear welfare and access
  consequences. Public collapses these into “practitioner lineages” that simply
  limit work. It does not say what authorizes the restriction, which material
  aid is interrupted, or where an adjacent site owner or household can refuse
  or appeal.
- **Effect:** Competence silently becomes jurisdiction. In play, a lineage can
  close a shrine, road, funeral, or relief route by declaring danger without a
  visible payer, boundary, review point, or responsible authority.
- **Repair direction:** Attribute the distinct decisions without importing the
  whole hidden procedure: who observes the hazard, who can stop a rite or
  schedule a grant, who controls site access, who bears lost relief, and where
  review returns when those authorities disagree. Preserve local variation and
  allow emergency action to remain bounded.
- **Collateral risk:** Overcorrection could invent one universal leakage code or
  make every urgent containment wait for a modern administrative hearing.
- **Dependency:** Material-life and player-consequence remits may test who bears
  interruption; this finding is limited to reproducing authority and handoff.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. The hidden role split is clear, although local
  law intentionally remains variable.

## Pattern or dependency map

The candidate already supports this plural machine:

`claimed divine act or statement`
→ covenant witness preserves terms, origin, claimant, and intermediary
→ potential steward records promised grant, material inputs, delivery, failure,
and beneficiary order
→ mortuary witness preserves bodily event, manifestation conditions, and last
statements
→ sect or clergy records its own interpretation
→ the authority competent over the actual consequence acts
→ copied evidence, discipline, appeal, and correction remain available.

Public currently skips from the claimed act to anonymous school doctrine. The
repair should expose enough of the middle to make disagreement causal without
turning the chain into a universal procedure.

## Tempting edit to resist

- Do not invent a College of Delegated Attention, a universal divine-witness
  office, or a continent-wide tribunal that authenticates gods. That would
  convert a missing owner into a larger duplicate owner and erase the exact
  institutional friction the Three Ledgers material successfully preserves.
- Do not make every cult keep three standardized physical books. The event
  explicitly establishes neither a universal medium nor universal adoption.

## Ranked actions

1. Remove or canonically owner-back the anonymous cult-school doctrine in
   Public; use existing role owners rather than a new universal school.
2. Make the claimed-speaker/intermediary/observation/interpretation distinction
   survive the handoffs among covenant, allocation, mortuary, and sect records.
3. Clarify the bounded authority and review path behind Public restrictions on
   concentrated work near hungry holy places.
