# KALSA-036 — Papsenai Shield-Levy Knowledge

## Objective

Test whether Public lets inhabitants carry Papsenai knowledge about long-horizon
shamanic risk, seed and subsistence, continuous shield labor, wartime demand,
and reserve failure. Add a source only if the current overview cannot sustain
an evidence-bearing encounter without hidden canon.

## Source inventory

- **Seed:** hereditary southern-gulf agrarian tribes; valuable surplus;
  Ti'asantatca beside a perpetual storm; powerful shamans continuously maintain
  a shield until exhaustion and replacement. The seed's Alliance and Intuition
  counterpart remain unresolved.
- **Adopted canon:** Papsenai polity owns land, agricultural cycle, risk, and
  political memory; Stormshield Circles owns training, shifts, handoff, rest,
  consent, refusal, warnings, and failure response; Shield Levy Winter alone
  owns the double demand, damage, evidence, settlement, and omissions.
- **Unknowns:** campaign, date, duration, claimants, participants, quantities,
  exact losses, local terms, present adopters, shield layout, and Alliance or
  Intuition involvement.

## Falsification

- **Marginal view:** a land-poor household loses both seed protection and a
  worker to obligations made by authorities with deeper stores.
- **Stress case:** the Shield Levy Winter demands grain and a relief cohort
  while transport, field labor, and the stormshield are already under pressure.

## Institutional model

- **Function:** protect long-horizon field and shield work from decisions that
  consume their reserves before visible failure.
- **Authority and limits:** households and local assessors classify seed,
  subsistence, and negotiable grain; campaign authorities seek contribution;
  Circle leads judge viable handoff and overlap. None inherits the others'
  competence.
- **Material support:** field labor, transport, stores, seed, provisions,
  attendants, competent relief, dry rest, warning records, and household care.
- **Doctrine versus practice:** wider protection can justify emergency demand;
  the double demand joined grain collection to loss of the relief body that
  sheltered what remained.
- **Beneficiaries, costs, exclusion, refusal:** the campaign sought food and
  trained workers; supplying communities lost reserve and exposure capacity;
  active workers carried longer watches; later practice gave Circle leads a
  bounded refusal without settling every material claim.
- **Historical formation:** the breach made reserve classification, paired
  receipts, shield-worker levy limits, consent, replacement, and operational
  refusal part of later practice.
- **Internal contradiction:** contribution remains possible while the people
  responsible for local survival can stop a demand whose wider purpose may be
  real.
- **Player-facing consequence:** a store mark, absent assignment, warning,
  household copy, or later receipt can alter collection, watch coverage,
  exposure, recovery, and old claims without resolving the war.
