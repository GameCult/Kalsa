## Falsification

- **Marginal view:** the non-house captain loses paid work, housing standing,
  and clients before the city narrows the claim made against them.
- **Stress case:** two occupying powers predict incompatible disasters around
  a failing grain lift while each conceals an intended intervention.

## Institutional model

- **Function:** compare futures without letting a forecast, sponsor, court, or
  machine owner absorb every decision that follows.
- **Authority and limits:** houses own reports and internal discipline; sponsors
  own their intended acts and scoped claims; civic judges own findings and
  remedy; Sunwall stewards own safe operation; clerks own public copies.
- **Material support:** house training and records, grain loads, lift crews,
  brake assembly, inspection, civic officers, public notice, housing, paid
  work, and client relations.
- **Doctrine versus practice:** disclosure makes a forecast challengeable, yet
  the first court converted inferred Luck into custody before the sponsor plans
  or material fault were visible.
- **Beneficiaries, costs, exclusion, refusal:** both occupiers sought control of
  grain movement; closure displaced city pressure and the non-house captain;
  later restriction and repair moved grain while compensation remained open.
- **Historical formation:** the appeal made sponsor intervention, evidence
  scope, reversible precaution, separate actuation, and correction duties
  harder to collapse.
- **Internal contradiction:** a city dependent on prophetic service must allow
  forecasts to change action while refusing to let their prestige identify a
  culprit or operate a lift.
- **Player-facing consequence:** forecast copies, disclosure, brake evidence,
  movement orders, corrected records, and wage or housing claims reach
  different owners and produce non-substitutable outcomes.
