## Institutional model

- **Function:** make large divine workings allocatable, interruptible,
  inspectable, and consequential without making them ordinary.
- **Authority and enforcement limits:** gods choose expenditure; cult offices
  authorize mortal rites and stores; material operators own physical systems;
  mortuary witnesses own observations; none alone owns identity or cosmology.
- **Material support, records, places, tools, maintenance, supply:** source
  stores, energy routes, prepared sites, miracle-route maps, presence rolls,
  interruption thresholds, containment equipment, recovery reserves, and
  aftermath accounts.
- **Doctrine versus practice:** radiance may be taught as glory or
  purification while mortuary copies preserve recognizable faces, missing
  presences, and overfed dead outside the protected route.
- **Beneficiaries, costs, exclusion, refusal:** a champion and intended
  beneficiary may be protected while neighboring dead, residents, withdrawn
  claimants, workers, and cleanup crews bear the expenditure.
- **Dependencies:** Kaos reachability, divine hierarchy, potential allocation,
  covenant procedure, mortuary evidence, relic/material custody, and Oracle
  Ecology.
- **Player-facing consequences:** spectacular workings leave material sources,
  old preparations, diverted routes, destroyed posthumous agencies, overfed
  margins, contested records, and cleanup claims that can be investigated.

## Critique

- Generic fantasy defaults: divine spectacle cannot become costless conjuring
  or teleportation.
- Missing material constraints: every apparent creation, movement, radiance,
  or destruction needs matter, energy, preparation, leverage, and potential.
- False consensus or absent subordinate perspectives: cult glory, divine
  self-defense, mortuary loss, neighborhood exposure, and feeding desperation
  must remain different claims.
- Consequences that stop at the page edge: concentrated miracles alter later
  relief, burial, manifestation, cleanup, covenant trust, and divine rivalry.
- Duplicate or ambiguous owners: the proposal must cease carrying live lore.
