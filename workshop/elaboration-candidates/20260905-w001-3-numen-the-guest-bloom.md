# The Guest Bloom

In a proposed cluster of papsenai farming hamlets beside the southern gulf, people keep flowering plants for someone else's visit. A friend returning from market, a child coming home from apprenticeship, or two households meeting after a long quarrel may find a garden approaching its finest bloom. Growers call the practice the **guest bloom**. These English words render a local expression whose native wording remains open.

The hamlets belong among the farming communities of [[Spoilers/Polities/Shamanism - Papsenai|the papsenai]], beyond Ti'asantatca itself. Their households live chiefly by growing food and exchanging surplus. Their devotional gardens occupy sheltered yard corners and containers that can be carried into better light. Most contain ordinary plants raised without magical help. A few become the subjects of a shaman's sustained attention: a living thing whose flourishing someone has chosen to welcome.

## Making ready

A host begins with a cutting or seed and the expected interval of a visit. Experienced growers choose plants whose flowering already falls near that interval. They save water, mend wind screens, turn containers toward the light and ask neighbours which stems are likely to survive. Children learn to test the soil with their fingers before learning the welcome verses. They also learn that an impressive blossom does not excuse a dead root.

Where a shaman joins the work, the preparation follows the existing practice of [[Public/World/Magic and Miracles#Working toward a harvest|holding a field's future]]. The shaman bonds with the planting and attends to pressure on its prospects; growers keep tending it. This can favour a reachable flowering, with sufficient time and resources. It cannot make a seed flower at the end of a song. Trance requires an attendant, and a tired shaman may decline even a much-loved household's invitation.

The gathering happens when the visitor and flowers are both ready, if that can be managed. People eat beside the planting, trade cuttings and perform the welcome verses with different names fitted into them. Children prefer versions containing the visitor's least dignified achievement. Someone who once rescued a cooking pot from a ditch may hear about it for years.

The wonder is intimate. Buds watched through many periods of work begin loosening while the person they were tended for sits within reach. Hosts often leave the flowers attached so that guests can return and see what follows. A bloom missed because a traveller was delayed may become a gift of seed instead.

## What growers believe

The hamlets' garden teachers describe welcome as a sacred obligation toward living things that cannot promise repayment. They address the plant during tending and thank it when it flowers. Some believe it receives affection. Others say the words prepare the grower to notice needs that impatience would miss. Neither interpretation settles whether plants have minds, and no answering voice is promised.

People may keep these gardens alongside their household god's rites. Some dedicate a flowering to that god; others address only the plant and the expected guest. Garden teaching does not explain how gods obtain power or claim that every use of a Gift is worship. Its proposed magical successes remain within [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|the established limits of Kaos]]; its account of sacred welcome belongs to its practitioners.

The practice persists because people enjoy it, because cuttings and instruction travel cheaply between households, and because a small planting lets an apprentice undertake a meaningful bond without a village's food depending on success. Good teachers are admired for bringing awkward, ordinary plants to health. Rich hosts can still turn the gathering into a competition over rare flowers and hired talent.

Growers disagree most sharply when work is scarce. Some teachers want every apprentice to tend one flowering for pleasure. Others prefer the same lessons taught through food crops and find the distinction indulgent. A tenant who loves her garden may still need the yard for vegetables. A gifted child may want to become an excellent flower grower while her family hopes for a harvest shaman. Both ambitions ask something real of the household.

## Unresolved hooks

- An apprentice earns a place with a respected harvest teacher just as neighbours begin relying on her flowering lessons. Can she leave without letting admiration choose her life?
- Two estranged siblings agree to meet when their mother's cutting flowers. Its buds are ready; one sibling wants more time. Their mother must decide whether to postpone a gathering she has spent months preparing.
- A prosperous host offers sheltered growing space to land-poor neighbours, provided their best flowers remain there for visitors. Several growers want the offer; others want cuttings they can take home.
