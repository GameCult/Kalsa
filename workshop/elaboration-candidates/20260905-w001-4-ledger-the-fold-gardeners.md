# The Fold Gardeners

The Fold gardeners live on both sides of a low ridge, carrying baskets of living roots between bright fields and dark growing sheds. They raise a bitter-leaved crop called hearthroot in the light, then lift its swollen roots into damp beds behind the ridge. There, sheltered from direct sunlight, the roots spend their stored nourishment on pale, tender shoots. A bowl of those shoots, cooked with sour broth, is the food people miss when they leave.

This proposed community occupies a habitable stretch of [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|Kalsa's twilight continent]]. The ridge casts a lasting shadow because the sun does not cross the sky. Its two sides are close enough for loaded walking journeys; this is a local fold in the land, not a migration between planetary climate zones. Springs supply household water and irrigation. Thick walls, shuttered entrances and shared cooking rooms make the shadow-side houses habitable; their sheds need cool, frost-free conditions, not subterranean heat.

## A life across the ridge

Several neighbouring villages call their shared way of life “the Fold,” rendered here in English rather than assigned an invented ancient etymology. Most established households descend from surface communities of [[Spoilers/Polities/Null and Control Descendants|Control ancestry]], but marriage, apprenticeship and settlement have brought other families into them. Neither ancestry nor the presence of a Gift decides who belongs. Membership is learnt in kitchens, fields and friendships; some residents never work a growing bed.

The bright slopes hold seed plots, mature root fields and ordinary food crops. Shadow-side villages pack their sheds around paved washing courts. Roots travel wrapped in damp fibre, while the same carriers bring back compost and repaired baskets. Young plants and roots selected for seed stay in the light. Forcing sheds produce a limited harvest before the roots are exhausted: shade cannot feed a village by itself.

Families stagger plantings and reckon visits by batches of roots and local work intervals. Their speech preserves growing terms that outsiders find useful but often misunderstand, consistent with the regional variation in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Language and Reckoning|Kalsan language and reckoning]]. A promised “next lifting” means a particular bed, not a universal date.

Children learn to handle a root without bruising its crown and to recognize rot before the smell fills a shed. They also learn songs with a carrying pace, which adults continue long after admitting that the words are ridiculous. A good grower admires somebody who can teach a difficult skill without making the learner feel small. At shared meals, cooks compete over crispness and broth; judges are ordinarily whoever has already stolen a second helping.

Some households maintain places on both slopes. Others remain field growers, shed renters, potters, carriers or cooks. Young adults often spend an apprenticeship across the ridge without moving their whole family. Staying home to care for someone can be an honoured choice, though that praise does not carry water or mend a roof.

## What prosperity would buy

Hearthroot provides part of the local diet; preserved shoots also buy grain, oil, cloth and metal tools from passing traders. Households grow other crops and keep stores against failed batches. Fresh shoots fetch more nearby, but distant sales require preservation, sound vessels and reliable transport. Water, manure, fertile field space and carrying labour set limits that a larger order cannot wish away.

Owners of bright growing land can sell roots to several sheds. A shed renter has already spent labour preparing beds when a grower raises the price. Families with property on both sides can absorb losses and offer credit; poorer households increasingly work for them. The respect accorded skilled shed workers does not give those workers a field.

The villages disagree about what success should look like. Export-minded growers want covered carrying paths, better jars and larger orders. Some shed families want surplus spent on dry sleeping rooms, washing courts and space where children can play away from the crop. Others want small plots of their own. Many want all three. A carrier may favour expansion because covered paths would save her knees, while her grower brother fears borrowing against another harvest.

## Unresolved hooks

- A cook returns with an offer to supply a distant wealthy household. Can neighbours fill it without surrendering the shoots they most enjoy eating themselves?
- Two renting families have saved enough to buy one bright-side plot. One wants seed independence; the other needs food and income immediately.
- A new covered path would shorten the carry but cross a village's best outdoor gathering place. Its strongest advocate is also the musician who brings people there.
