# The Almost Fall

At a Sarxe play table, a wooden bead rolls toward the edge. Three spectators reach to catch it. The performer turns its motion along the rim and lets it drop into a cup beside her elbow. Her brother has kept his hands folded throughout. He ruins this small triumph by announcing that he knew she would do that. She hands him the bead.

Among a small circle of households travelling with [[Spoilers/Polities/Channeling - Sarxe|Saxfoldi]], these performances are called **almost falls** in translation. They are short arrangements of rolling beads, tilting boards, cups and interruptions. People attend to enjoy the instant when an expected landing becomes something else. A favourite sequence is asked for repeatedly; knowing its ending can make the wait more delicious. An ambitious performer wants a familiar audience to reach for the same bead twice.

The equipment packs into a shallow wooden tray. For a performance, the board rests across it with its edges clear. A performer tips the board or rolls a bead by hand, then uses Channeling to turn its existing motion. The close-range sensing described in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]] lets some spectators feel the approaching crossing as well as see it. A later deflection changes that approach. Nobody receives the performer's plan. The trick depends on movement already underway, and a bead that stops needs another push. Soft catching cloths surround the tray; nothing has to fly at a face to be impressive.

A good performance has rests. Several quick turns can become a blur, while one slow approach gives an audience time to become certain and then be wrong. Some enthusiasts prefer an almost silent board and a very small circle within sensing reach. Others like painted beads, audible cup landings and a performer who makes a production of choosing the wrong cup. Watching, hearing and sensing offer different pleasures, and people with little or no Channeling can enjoy the visible trick without pretending to have felt the crossing.

These tastes divide households. Vesi, a well-liked performer, has begun ending her favourite sequence with a conspicuous clatter of nested cups. Her mother, Oren, says the noise spoils the turn: everyone looks at the cups before the bead reaches the rim. Vesi says that is where she wants them looking. Her cousin, who does not express Channeling, now asks to see the piece again. Oren used to have that response to herself.

Oren's objection is more than wounded prestige. She loves the held breath of a close audience, when a tiny movement is enough. Vesi loves drawing in people who were previously standing behind the enthusiasts, waiting for somebody to explain why the trick was good. Neither wants to surrender the household's best board. Vesi has also started calling the quiet version unfinished, which is an efficient way to make her mother stop lending it.

The circle persists because its pleasures travel cheaply. Most players use spare cups and wooden beads; smoother boards are prized, but a familiar warped tray can demand more skill than a costly new one. Hosts lend a surface during rest, and performers exchange sequences when households meet. The circle gathers wherever its [[Spoilers/Institutions/Saxfoldi Households|households]] are already resting; its invitations pass between people who have met there. A private showing can be two relatives and one cup. Some household members find the entire enthusiasm tedious and would prefer the cup back.

Children invent catches and argue about whose version deserves a name. Adults compete for invitations and sometimes copy a rival's ending badly. Invitations concern the objects on the board; they do not authorize magical attention to a spectator's body. Someone who finds the close sensing tiring can watch from farther away or stop. The household remains theirs when they dislike its favourite amusement.

## Unresolved hooks

- Oren agrees to perform beside Vesi, but asks her to introduce the quiet piece as complete. Vesi has already promised their cousins a combined finale.
- A visitor performs a celebrated sequence entirely by tilting the tray. Its inventor loves the imitation until the visitor receives more requests than she does.
- Two siblings devise different endings together. Their friends remember the louder sibling as the inventor; the quieter one refuses to show the crucial turn again.
