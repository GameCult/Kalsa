# The Bowl Asks After You

At Reed Elbow, someone who wants a friend to visit may say, “The bowl asks whether you have found a better bowl.” A good answer gives the bowl something to complain about next time. “Tell it I have found three, but none can keep a secret.” People exchange these remarks over meals and along the path between their houses. Half the pleasure is hearing what unreasonable opinion a familiar possession has acquired in your absence.

Reed Elbow is a proposed cluster of [[Spoilers/Polities/Shamanism - Papsenai|papsenai farming households]] beside a bend in an irrigation channel in the southern gulf region. Residents grow food, tend the channel and dry crops for storage; several houses share a sheltered place for sorting what they bring in. Neighbours repeatedly handle one another's baskets, bowls and mats. An invitation spoken through one of these things can recall a particular visit without telling the whole story again.

The English title translates the local habit for readers. Its actual wording belongs to Reed Elbow's ordinary speech, within the regional variation described in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Language and Reckoning|Language and Reckoning]]. Speakers invent the objects' remarks. A bowl's complaint gives no evidence that the bowl has spoken.

## What a possession remembers

Chavi's wide serving bowl is jealous. It first acquired that disposition when her friend Udrem praised another household's cooking within its hearing. Chavi enjoyed pretending to console it; Udrem returned with a particularly good portion for himself and announced that reconciliation required privacy. Years later, Chavi can ask after him through the bowl without repeating the meal that started it.

These little characters belong to relationships. Another visitor may have eaten from that bowl a hundred times without knowing its quarrel. Children learn by asking why a mat dislikes somebody or why a basket thinks it deserves an invitation. Adults usually tell the incident, then let the child supply the object's next remark. A child who makes every possession furious soon discovers that nobody particularly wants to answer the entire house.

Some families favour extravagant complaints. Others give their belongings small, exact preferences: a cup misses the visitor who always turned its chipped side away from their mouth. Remembering that gesture can please its subject more than an elaborate exchange. There is no admired household style that everyone manages, or wants, to copy.

## When the invitation arrives badly

The practice relies on shared experience, like much everyday interpretation in [[Spoilers/Institutions/High Lojban and Descendant Speech|High Lojban and Descendant Speech]]. It travels unevenly even between nearby households.

When Chavi told a new neighbour that her bench was asking after him, he returned with wedges and tightened its loose joints. He had helped mend it before. She thanked him, fed him, and only then explained that she had wanted him to sit down. He still maintains that the bench had a legitimate complaint.

A harder ambiguity has grown between Udrem and Chavi. Since his daughter established her own household nearby, he has often eaten with her. Chavi's bowl now complains whenever they meet. He loves their old exchange, but has begun carrying food to Chavi before going to his daughter's, as though he must placate the bowl to enjoy another table. Chavi thinks the extra food is his latest contribution to the joke. He has not told her how carefully he divides it.

Chavi dislikes blunt invitations because a refusal can sound larger than the occasion warrants. Udrem likes knowing whether she actually expects him. Both enjoy the bowl. Their choice is whether to keep using its voice when it lets them avoid asking each other a question whose answer matters.

## Unresolved hooks

- Udrem has brought enough food for two tables again. Chavi reaches for the jealous bowl before he can explain why.
- The neighbour invites Chavi to a meal through the repaired bench. She must decide whether he has joined the joke or genuinely needs help carrying it.
- Chavi's youngest nephew wants to take the bowl when he starts his own home. He knows all its complaints, but Udrem has never eaten at his table.
