# The Crust Walk

In a lower-shadow neighbourhood of the [[Spoilers/Polities/Prophecy - The Divided City|Divided City]], friends meet at a bakehouse to buy long strips of hot, brittle bread. They leave with most of their supper still to find. One seller has a sour root pickle, another a warm grain paste, another a dark, deeply toasted crumble. Walking between them, people break off pieces, add a little of whatever is ready, and argue about the next stop. Someone who knows the neighbourhood can put together a fine meal cheaply. Someone who knows it very well can make the same argument about a different route every time.

The walk grew around bakers using the falling heat after their larger batches. Thin bread could finish quickly while neighbouring food sellers prepared small accompaniments for workers leaving their jobs. The walkers still follow batches rather than a sunset. Here, beneath the Sunwall's shadow, [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|the world's lasting twilight]] supplies no daily evening. Friends arrange to meet when a particular baker brings out the strips; a delayed batch leaves time to fetch somebody, mend a fastening, or hear why a cousin has changed trades again. Local working intervals matter, without everyone using one [[Spoilers/Foundations/Language and Reckoning|reckoning of time]].

Several generations of bakers, carriers, clothes-menders and food sellers have made the walk a neighbourhood accomplishment. Families remember which shop first served a grandparent's favourite sour paste, but newcomers also acquire followings. A recent arrival who finds a good use for a coarse flour can become someone worth introducing to your friends. People admire a host who knows an inexpensive delicious route, notices when a companion has had enough, and leaves enough bread for the final shop. Finishing it early is a common failure of foresight in a city with a considerable reputation for the subject.

There are walks for different pleasures. Some friends pursue the sharpest pickles. Others want a long conversation with only two stops. Courting pairs can learn a surprising amount from sharing one strip: who chooses, who asks, who saves the last crisp corner. Children run short errands between familiar sellers and return with opinions their elders had hoped to postpone. Older residents often settle beside one shop while relatives bring back small portions for everyone to compare. Walking is the name of the custom, not a measure of membership.

Neret learned to cook beside her aunt Osa's pickle counter. She now wants to rent a little eating shop where she can serve a whole crust meal at once. She has been testing shallow bowls of warm paste with bread laid across their rims, so each person can choose when to dip it. The flavours arrive together, the bread stays dry until wanted, and friends can sit through a whole conversation. Neret wants people from the upper city to come down expressly to eat her food. She is tired of hearing that the best thing about her cooking is how little it costs.

Osa thinks the bowls taste excellent. She also thinks Neret has mistaken the sellers for ingredients. Osa's counter survives partly because the baker sends hungry people onward; she recommends the crumble seller in turn. If Neret buys their food wholesale and serves it herself, the suppliers gain dependable orders but may lose the customers who know them by name. If she cooks every part herself, she gains freedom to change a dish and takes on considerably more work. Her younger cousin would gladly leave the carrying trade to help her. Osa had expected him to learn pickling.

The neighbourhood has no single answer. Some walkers look forward to having a place for a seated birthday meal. Others fear that a successful shop will become the place visitors call the entire neighbourhood's cuisine. Neret still takes the walk on her free stretches. She wants its cooks to be proud of her, and wants to make something they did not teach her.

## Hooks left open

- Osa offers to supply Neret's first opening batch at the ordinary shop price. Neret must decide whether that leaves enough money to employ her cousin, or whether her first menu needs a different pickle.
- A baker offers to recommend Neret's shop as the last stop on the walk. The crumble seller, whose counter currently fills that role for their shared customers, has a new dish ready and wants Neret to taste it.
