# The Last Note

In the Hollow Courts, a proposed outer neighbourhood of the [[Spoilers/Polities/Luck - Selza'a|Luck Crown]], a good throw can make a whole crowd hold its breath. A shallow metal bowl hangs by cords across a fog-filled court. An attendant draws it sideways, taps it, and releases it to swing. The player listens, waits, then throws a soft cloth ball where the bowl should be. A hit answers with a dull clang. An early throw usually finds empty air; a late one earns unsolicited advice from people who have never hit anything.

The game is called **last note** here, an English rendering of an undecided local name. Its best-known players can follow a bowl after its ringing has faded. They listen to the release, judge the swing, and correct for a cord hanging slightly crooked or a gust entering the court. The fog sometimes thins enough to reveal the target. Nobody demands a blindfold: noticing what the weather gives you is part of playing.

The Hollow Courts occupy a proposed cluster of sheltered hollows within the Crown's inhabited [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|coldward plateau]]. Households repair cloth, tend catchments, carry water, and trade across neighbouring [[Spoilers/Institutions/Selza'a Family Territories|family territories]]. Courts used to spread and sort cloth become playing grounds between work sessions. Balls stuffed with worn cloth cost little; a dented bowl can serve several households. Matches travel between these nearby courts, bringing customers to repairers and stalls without requiring anyone to leave the isolated city.

Players receive the same number of throws from a shared marked line. Someone beside the target counts impacts; nobody retrieves balls until throwing stops. The greatest applause comes when a player waits through an apparently hopeless silence and still lands the shot. Children practise against stationary pans. Adults with uncertain balance can throw seated from the line. Some residents care only for a quiet match with a familiar rival; others spend their spare cloth on balls of obsessively consistent weight.

Team loyalties cross family membership. A formidable pair may quarrel over a water-carrying fee and still refuse to play with anyone else. Lovers recognise one another's throwing rhythm. A popular player can become insufferable without becoming a household authority. Families sometimes credit a victory to their dimna, understood locally as a protector or ancestor; other spectators praise a well-judged release. Nobody can settle that argument by inspecting the winning throw for Luck.

## The crowd enters the game

The newest fashion permits spectators to call, clap, and make ridiculous speeches after the bowl is released. Players must hold its movement in mind while the sound disappears under the crowd. Famous opponents recruit callers who know exactly which insult will make the other laugh. Fans remember a throw that survived a perfectly timed imitation of the player's own boast. These matches attract people who find the quiet game unbearably solemn.

Now a crowded match has swallowed a warning from the adjoining carrying path. A porter approaching a damaged step failed to hear the call to stop and fell, injuring a shoulder. The route's cords remained in place; they could guide a hand without announcing that the next foothold had just broken.

The next challenge is already promised at the same court. Its host wants to divert carriers around the gathering. The longer route adds an exposed stretch and another climb to every loaded journey. The injured porter's sister, herself an enthusiastic caller, wants the loud rounds moved to a smaller enclosed workroom. Its owner will lend it, but there is space for few spectators. Several players offer to return to quiet throws. The current favourite refuses: she earned her reputation playing through interference and resents having her strongest skill treated as the accident.

The court's stallholders have stocked for the challenge. Carriers are threatening to walk straight through the playing ground if diverted. A shouted confrontation would obscure the path calls as effectively as the match. Local families can settle use of their own spaces, but no ruler can simply decree a new sporting ground for everyone. The choice must be made before the crowd arrives.

## Hooks left open

- The favourite offers to demonstrate that warning calls remain audible during play. Her own supporters will supply the noise; the injured porter wants ordinary carriers present too.
- A rival has borrowed the enclosed workroom and announced an invitation-only challenge there. The loud game's finest caller has accepted, leaving her usual team short.
- A carrying crew arrives early to repair the broken step. Their materials occupy the throwing line, and the first visiting team is already warming up.
