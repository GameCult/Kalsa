# The Far Sound

At Warmhook, a returning boat can draw a crowd without bringing a single fish. Someone has rounded a headland nobody here has seen beyond. Wet sailors stand on the hauling stones, describing the shape of the shore with their hands. People interrupt to ask about landing beaches, strange lights, or whether the enormous cliff was enormous before the storyteller fell beneath it.

The Far Sound is the community that has grown around these voyages: fishing families, boatwrights, shore observers, and newcomers who want to discover how far the coast continues coldward. They live at an inlet on [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|Kalsa's twilight coast]], before the continuous coldward sea ice. A steep headland shelters their landing from the prevailing wind. Hot springs warm stone houses through shallow covered channels; a separate upland stream supplies drinking water. Storms can still close the beach for long stretches.

The inlet supports a fishery in twilight waters. Households dry and salt their catch, selling part to warmer coastal settlements for grain, timber, cloth, and metal. Boat repair brings additional work. Exploration uses fishing boats fitted for longer journeys, paid for by crews pooling earnings and neighbours buying shares in the catch. Most voyages combine unfamiliar fishing grounds with short advances along the shore. A disappointing discovery need not mean a hungry return.

Many founding households trace their ancestry through [[Spoilers/Polities/Null and Control Descendants|Control-descended surface communities]]. Marriage and immigration have brought other histories into the inlet. Belonging rests on living here and taking part, with no required ancestry or Gift. Some residents never go afloat. A shore observer who can recognise a returning hull through spray has admirers; so does a boatwright whose ugly repair survives three captains.

Children compete to describe a hidden object precisely enough for someone else to draw it. Adults hold much noisier contests over the shapes of newly seen capes. Naming is a pleasure and a source of embarrassment: a grand name can lose to the crew member who remembers where the captain was sick. In the inlet's [[Spoilers/Foundations/Language and Reckoning|mixed working speech]], visiting crews contribute words as well as stories. A borrowed word for a headland may spread before anyone agrees which headland it means.

## Whose discovery?

Two rival loyalties cross households. The Long Reach crews admire distance and want a vessel that can remain away through several spells of bad weather. The Bottom Readers admire accurate soundings, seabed samples, and returning to the same place until its dangers become intelligible. They have found useful fishing grounds close to home and resent being called timid by people who borrow their depth lines. Neither group owns a separate guild. Spouses argue across the divide; captains change sides after a voyage.

Nearby fishing hamlets lend crews and beach space because Warmhook shares descriptions of shoals and shelters. Their cooperation lets the explorers travel farther. They are less enthusiastic when a Warmhook captain names a headland their grandparents already know. Discovery sometimes means discovering that someone else has been getting on with life.

A ruler in one of the grain-selling coastal towns now offers timber and provisions for a large exploring vessel. The offer is public: it must sail under the ruler's banner, and its captain must present the voyage at that court before telling the inlet. The ruler wants maritime prestige and informed sailors for a future fleet. Some Long Reach crews find this an excellent bargain. Bottom Readers fear neighbouring hamlets will see the ship as a territorial claim and stop helping it. Several fish sellers support the offer because court attention could win customers; others dislike having the grain supplier become their public patron as well.

Captain Vessa has already ordered a banner pole. Her brother, who negotiates landings with neighbouring crews, has asked the boatwright to leave it unfitted.

## Hooks left open

- A neighbouring crew offers Vessa a guide beyond the last charted cape, provided she sails under their colours alongside Warmhook's. The ruler expects only one banner.
- A Bottom Reader brings back an unfamiliar living catch. Keeping it alive for observation requires the flooded fish-well of the boat Vessa has promised for a public trial sail.
- A young shore observer recognises a supposedly new cliff in a visiting fisher's account. The celebrated captain asks her to wait until after his court presentation before saying so.
