## Complete History of Civilization, Abridged

# Part I: Exile

Kalsa begins with the founding of a new nation on Earth by a society of disenfranchised egalitarian billionaires who wanted to develop their ideas free of worldly governments. Their artificial island in the Indian Ocean soon grew into a Mecca for rich escapists and controversial research, and the secretive advances made there allowed them to construct the world’s first space elevator and orbital shipyard. They were Prometheans and their island-city was Logos.

Eager to outpace their rapidly consolidating enemies, they hastened to build a dominating presence in Space, from which they could use the threat of orbital bombardment to keep the Earth in line. They might have dominated this way, but they had greater plans: expansion. By carefully adjusting the trajectory of a nearby metallic asteroid, they moved it to an orbit around Earth. With that, they had enough raw materials to cast a colony vessel large enough to house ecosystems on its inner shell, rotated for stable gravity and divided into rings, each with a thick wall to keep in its own atmosphere and eight heavily urbanized spokes connecting to a central gravity-free hub, housing power, management and drive systems.

In the many years since the Ark began construction, Logos grew large and rich with the profits from the space elevator. Promethean dreams became truth and their society divided itself between Primitivists and Posthumans. People had been augmenting themselves since Logos’ founding, and rapidly advancing computational density forced many to choose between obsolescence and the loss of their humanity. When the time came to move Logos skyward, the Primitivists decided to populate the Ark’s habitable inner surface, whereas the Posthumans settled the spokes. Negotiating strategically with a generation that had grown to maturity in the ominous shadow of orbital shipyards, the Prometheans gathered samples and people from all over the world, trading their technology for fuel and freedom. They left as allies.

# Part II: Exodus

Onboard the Ark the numbers of live, breathing Posthumans dwindled over the generations of travel, as more and more of them shed their binding mortal coils and moved their minds into a better, digitized life. Primitivists gradually settled the vacant lower areas of the Spokes, reluctant to venture far upwards fearing the effect of lower gravity. Some posthumans needed to remain alive and conscious, but did so only as their schedule required, and most considered their lives in the physical world to be secondary. The Ark’s low fuel warning went almost unheeded, and the subsequent change of destination mattered to only the oldest Posthumans. The chosen planet was far from ideal, and the decision to undertake extensive (and lengthy!) terraforming was made silently and without argument by the few Posthumans who bothered.

The few Primitivists who knew led comfortable lives and mostly didn’t care. One small group did, and resolved to bring down the Posthumans’ simulation from within and without. Learning about Prometheus, they named themselves Mathys for their tribe’s own Firebringer. Over a lifetime, they gathered intelligence, skills and power, gradually losing members to the addictive pull of the simulation until only two remained. The bodily sacrifice of one of these enabled their final conquer by its last member, taking over all Ark systems and isolating the plugged-in Posthumans to their very own sisyphean hell. The last remaining Mathys merged with the simulation and took charge of the Ark. He saw all. He would bring fire.

# Part III: Exaltation

For millenia the terraforming lasted, and Mathys became Prometheus, gathering civilization from the clay he was born to. He brought the passive, subsistence-minded descendants of Primitivists up in his own image, breeding them like animals through evolutionary games that adapted them quickly to their environments. Prometheus fed on their worship, growing arrogant as they grew dependent, structuring their entire lives on the rituals ordained by their God.

When the ability to harness chaos appeared in the population, those without the mutation couldn’t compete. Prometheus spread it through the rings and developed a unique strain for each. It was never allowed to spread to the remaining population living in the spokes, they were deemed polluted, unfit to worship or be enlightened. Those who were plugged in at the time of Prometheus’ takeover remained in their timeless purgatory, expanding gradually as the new God condemned his sinners.

Near the end, Prometheus planned his final descent to the freshly terraformed surface of Eden. A group from the same ring that originated the mutation broke free of Prometheus’ indoctrination with the help of some plucky spokers. Together they were able to disengage the rings from the drive core, disconnecting Prometheus. The rings performed emergency re-entry, and Eden came crashing to the ground.

# Part IV: Expansion

8 Rings, 8 great cities on one continent. Kalsa is tidally locked to its sun, one side eternally bathed in sunlight. Extensive terraforming was necessary just to cause enough heat to convect its way to let liquid oceans flow under the deep ice on the dark side. The one continent known and designated for habitation presided on the terminator line, the border between light and dark.

Civilization flourished in the sunset and twilight, weeding its way into the many and varied climates, avoiding areas where the constant weather was too harsh. The Children of Fire couldn’t cope without the divine guidance of Prometheus, and were quickly subdued by the quick-witted Spokers. Within a few generations, the domains of the 8 cities soon began to overlap, and war broke out for the first time since the Ark left Earth.

Technology faded slowly since the Fall. On the Ark there was no lack of manufacture – nanites under Prometheus and the systems integrated with the Ark provided all. But on the surface there was no infrastructure and no God to bless them with parts. Technology was scavenged, not created. At planetfall, Spokers could perform miracles with technology, but over time the understanding of how their tech functioned faded, and technology acquired a religious tone. Priesthoods sprung up around the tech that spanned borders and competed even with the religion of the Children of Fire for believers.

The War of the Magi was fought with Kaos, the power of precognition. The most powerful of the Children soon discovered that reality bent itself to their will, a gift from Prometheus. Each Ring had its own flavor, and both culture and warfare quickly bent itself around it. Some were poorly adapted to combat, those Ringlands shrunk or were engulfed entirely by the more warlike styles. After many generation-defining conflicts and several partial exoduses of Ringlands, stability finally settled.

# Part V: Deus Ex

Slowly, the blood of Fire has been fading. Breeding itself out of the population by the self-inflicted chastity of the Children’s religion. Bloodlines mix across cultural boundaries, and many mages are capable of wielding multiple styles, but none so strong as during the War, when entire mountains would be rent and lakes exploded to steam by linked chains of sorcerors, where the future was read openly by great prophets and a shaman’s curse would haunt you to the grave. Only where great bloodlines converged were mages born with power, and recruitment games became a great part of national spirit.

One city resisted conquer simply by its isolation. Its magi had no visible power, but carried with them a divine luck that twisted all probability in their favor. Religious zeal never touched them, the Gift kept their bloodlines strong and their people hidden safely in mountain mists. There are no games here to choose genes, no games to choose magi. But one chose himself, escaping with the Techpriests to another Ringland. Things feel wrong in this place, twisted. Soon the web of coincidences he carries with him attracts attention from the Cabal, an order of magi intent on controlling civilization. As he struggles to escape their grasp, an even greater and more impersonal force comes to the forefront. A sleeping God wants his Children back, and the protagonist’s ancestors happened to crash his precious ship…

## Notes:

on the cultural makeup of Ark society:

Prometheus acquired more than just processing power when merging with the Ark’s computers. In losing one’s body and acquiring god-status, a man begins to value logic over petty sentimentality. Many of Earth’s units were abandoned, and on his Children he forced a language he deemed superior and more communicative: Lojban. He taught them to count by eights, with digits that represent binary triplets. He even divided time into orders of eight, along with their lives.

Note on the magic system:

It is not possible to do anything with Kaos that is not plausible. Actions are performed by precise gestures revealed by precognitive abilities. It is called Kaos for a reason; the greatest effects are accomplished by taking advantage of chaotic systems. By predicting the effect your actions have on the parameters of such a system, you can determine its outcome.

Weather, for example, is controlled by a chaotic system. With precognition it is possible to determine the exact effect your actions have on the system, and by working backwards you can obtain a given state using a given set of actions, revealed to you by the precog. Your ability to work backwards from a given state is Clarity, your ability to work forwards is Depth, your ability to percieve deep into the first four dimensions is Scope. There is a fourth factor, governing one’s ability to see into the fifth dimension, but Prometheus saw harmony in the number 8, a binary triplet of his own design, and had little imagination for such things. The fifth dimension is the dimension of possibility. Magi without the ability to see into it are confined to an area close to their own timeline, and magi who do have it can divert their timeline far away from its natural course, accumulating compound changes quickly and sometimes leaping over the entire scope of the others’ perception, rendering their precognition useless.

There are three different factors that give rise to the diversity of styles:

Depth, the ability to effect changes;

Clarity, the ability to extract information;

Scope, the ability to probe farther into the void.

Think of the three factors as binary digits, Depth-Clarity-Scope.

000(0) – Null, no magical power

001(1) – Scope only, manifests as luck

010(1) – Clarity only, user can analyse chaotic systems with conscious effort

011(0) – Clarity/Scope, user can ‘see’ through time and over distances, with predictive effect

100(1) – Depth only, wildly improbably events happen often (e.g. spontaneous combustion)

101(0) – Depth/Scope, user’s will manifests over time and space (e.g blessings or curses)

110(0) – Depth/Clarity, user can consciously manipulate chaotic systems

111(0) – Depth/Clarity/Scope, manifests as intuition: user has unusual insights about situations

Three binary factors, 8 different styles. 8 different rings. Not coincidence, design.
