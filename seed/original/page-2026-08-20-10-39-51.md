## IV – Analysis

Prometheus saw that his approach in the previous evolutionary games could be taken no further. The games were producing individuals whose approach to problem solving generally involved a generously sized weapon and the will to use it. Shamans had a mind for tactics and a longer worldview, but Prometheus wanted to create a more enlightened society. Therefore the next game needed to require a more comprehensive ability to understand complex systems.

## Evolution

He gave them technology, biological interfaces to a virtual world, and constructed a subtly dangerous arena populated by programmed behaviors, contests of coded artificial intelligence. Victory in virtual, gladiatorial games were the only way to be granted breeding rights, a system that was rather unnatural. This approach produced a much more perceptive trait, at the expense of physical prowess.

## Magic

Analysis magic isn’t very magical, just a very specific type of precognition that tells the analyst about how a system is put together. This means that exploitation of the system is fully conscious and changes cannot be as extreme as with Sorcery, but with conscious exploitation comes a great deal of control. An Analyst experiences his magic as a profound and sudden understanding, as a veil being lifted from the world. An intelligent Analyst can carry this knowledge with him and reapply it in the future. Analysts are also the only mages who can perceive the influence of magic (minus Intuition, Analysis and Luck), which do not affect the world enough to be noticed.

## Attributes

Analysts can apply their unique skill to a large variety of situations, and have an innate ability to communicate with computers as well as Prometheus’ nanites. Their systems knowledge allows them to exploit mechanical appliances, too, making them prodigious engineers as well as technologists. All of this savvy comes at a tremendous price; narrowing selection pressures to such an abstract goal allowed muscles and bones to atrophy over many generations. The modern Analyst suffers from this still, though the extent of this atrophy has been reduced by post-fall interbreeding with the control group, as has the extent of their power.

## Culture

The Ju’onai Hegemony is home to the Analysis trait. During the Fall, the fourth ring landed just outside the shadow of the sunward coastal mountain chain, Santabi’u. They are the  last in the sequence capable of generating Gods, and this provided the drive to create their expansive empire. Their gods are heavily pantheistic, each the embodiment of both an idea and a feeling. The gods are crafty in expansion and political in their hierarchies, manifesting and warring in a memetic struggle which shifts with the ideas of its people. Ju’onai ideas carry the weight of their Gods, and much cultural emphasis is placed on one’s standing in the holy hierarchy: Gods gain power by shifting cultural lines. One’s status is measured by the God that he represents, and ideological warfare is considered the equivalent of the physical kind. Junsenai are, because of this, very dogmatic and insular; even those considered subservient in the hierarchy (farmers, etc.) are under the dominion of the gods which grant them power. When junsenai fight, they do so with utter tact: to enter into battle without already having won is considered foolish.
