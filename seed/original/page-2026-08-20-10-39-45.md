## III – Shamanism

Sorcery was a mild success, Prometheus accomplished exactly what he was looking to accomplish. Compared to Luck  it did indeed have focus, but it was no good at ensuring survival in the long term. The type of individual that the game of conquest selected for were shortsighted, violent and militaristic to a fault; not the kind of individual a stable society could tolerate.

## Evolution

Sorcery was bred into the next ring, which became host to a subtly modified version of the struggle present in the previous one. In order to select for a more balanced individual, the goal of the tribal conquest became a fixed location, and the rules were changed to only give breeding rights to the tribe which held the goal for half a generation. This way, the game required not only the ability to carry out offense efficiently, but also to plan and defend, to deal with larger problems over longer times.

## Magic

Shamans, like sorcerers, invoke their abilities on a level just below full consciousness. Their will becomes apparent through blessings and curses, making shamanism a very directed form of Luck, more conducive to the long-term prosperity of its people. In order to achieve greater control over their magical effects, shamans enter into a trance where their subconscious perceptions become more concrete. Just as the effect of Luck manifests in dreams, Shamans must be in a dream-state in order to perform their magic. In it, they bond intimately with the fate of their target, and can feel the shifting of their time-self into a different place. As a result, a bonded Shaman can detect the presence of magic or conflict in the future of the individual he is bonded with. In the real world, this trance manifests as ritualistic dancing and chanting.

## Attributes

Shamans, being descended from Sorcerers, have their features, but a modified aesthetic has bred their physique into a less actively violent, more docile form. They are large, regularly reaching 8 feet in height, and have almost bovine features. They aren’t particularly intelligent, but passively kind in nature, ferocious in battle.

## Culture

Battles between shamans often involve the use of forces way larger in scope than those sorcerers use: shamans can bend the raging will of thunderstorms and earthquakes, and when they attack, they do so with overwhelming force. They are, however, naturally conservative, and rarely wear their aggression on their sleeves as sorcerers do. They are organized into hereditary tribes, and rarely fight amongst each other, living very agrarian lifestyles. Their ability to affect chaotic weather and yield patterns allows them to field the most profitable agricultural empire in the world, gaining them a solid, if stoic, place in the Alliance.

## Wonders

Ti’asantatca – the Great City of the papsenai – named itself “stormshield” by having landed in precisely the wrong place, just on the edge of a massive storm front on the southern gulf. There can be found at this city a circle of druids that tirelessly weave a massive barrier against the terrible weather. The circle must be kept constantly stocked with the most powerful of shamans, as old ones collapse out of the trance from exhaustion.
