# Magic and Miracles

Magic in Kalsa can help people understand how something will move, influence a harvest months away, warn of danger at a boundary, or change the course of a storm. The people who practise and record it disagree about what makes it reliable. Their accounts describe both extraordinary results and work that failed.

A storm can divide around a city. Stone can give way along an old fault. A god may grant a champion so much favor that witnesses begin building a shrine before the smoke clears. One Jamnai record describes [[../Sources/The Faces in the Ash Halo|faces appearing in the light while iron raiment closes]] around a champion.

Such accounts do not supply a single set of common rules. Failures offer some of the clearest limits: a storm shaman has nothing to divide in an empty sky, and a reader cannot find a yielding joint in an unloaded wall. Practical preparations also matter. Delving compacts fund the crew and its recovery before inspiration begins. Workings at other sites have interfered with each other, while repeated miracles near old deaths have drawn answers nobody asked for. Courts, shrines, and mortuary keepers disagree about what these failures establish.

## Working toward a harvest

A Papsenai shaman may begin attending to one field months before harvest. This work, called a field bond, involves the household's decisions as well as the shaman's attention.

Before the bond begins, the household names the seed and subsistence it will keep out of the work. The shaman directs the remaining labor and reserves toward a harvest the household can reach, pressing weather and chance toward that season. Seed is reserved and work changes as the effort proceeds. Later field records preserve the decisions to continue pursuing that future, release it, or abandon it.

## Reading how things work

Ju'onai readers experience **openings**: moments when they suddenly understand the joins and hinges of a particular, bounded system. In a loaded wall, an opening may reveal how its parts bear weight together. Someone shifts a brace before the wall moves; the reader's sudden understanding concerns those connected loads.

Ju'onai schools disagree about why such knowledge can be trusted. Concordance Temples look to a god; Trial Boards to disciplined testing; Four-Because Chairs to precise speech about causes; and Named-Edge Circles to an honest account of where the reader's knowledge stops. They compare demonstrations of openings, but they do not share one explanation of them.

A House of Attestation certifies what a reader demonstrated. That certificate does not settle what should be done: a court may still reject the judgment the reader proposes. Client colleges examine this distinction through exercises such as [[../Sources/Four Readings of a Broken Sluice|Four Readings of a Broken Sluice]]. Its keeper repeatedly brings the schools' arguments back to the failed gate and the lower fields it was supposed to serve.

An opening may also leave evidence behind. Ju'onai schools call certain local traces of how events happened **aftermarks**. An inspector following one may return to a shifted brace, a cracked seal, or a sequence of movements. Even the strictest school accounts require other evidence before naming who acted or what they intended.

## Listening at boundaries

Along guarded Sarxe routes, listeners can feel pressure crossing a boundary soon enough to redirect a patrol or turn danger aside. Their testimony identifies the listener, the boundary, and the patrol that acted on the warning. Knowledge accumulates around these crossings rather than in classrooms.

Outsiders tell stories of a shared Sarxe mind. Listeners describe flows between distinct people and places.

## Forecasts change what follows

A Prophet's announcement can alter the future it describes. People who hear a forecast may act because of it, so courts need to know more than what was predicted.

City courts ask what a sponsor intended to do after hearing a forecast. The surviving files of [[../Sources/The Closed Lift Appeal|the Closed Lift Appeal]] preserve those intended interventions alongside the forecasts offered as evidence.

## When futures crowd

Divided City house comparers call the future covered by a forecast **crowded** when people within it can act on the report or respond through another Gift. To compare such forecasts, they keep separate leaves for the question asked, who received the answer, what those recipients intended to do, what changed in actual operations, and when the report expired. If the record leaves something unexplained, comparers retain the gap until a named witness or material trace narrows it.

This practice developed around disputes such as [[../Sources/The Three Orders at Sunwall|the Three Orders at Sunwall]]. Two patrons prepared incompatible armed movements. The steward responsible for the loaded wall shut its machinery, removing both the planned counter-route and the opening the opposing champion meant to use. The houses now ask what each sponsor intended to do after receiving a report. Sunwall records what its operators changed while that report was still live.

“Crowded” also has uses outside Prophetic work. In [[../Sources/The Narrow Shield|the Narrow Shield]], Papsenai workers protecting their targets and a Sarxe boundary redirected pressure into one another. Their separate corrections kept changing what the other groups had to handle, until one watch lead narrowed the defense. Circle tenders explain this collision by tracing signals and the targets released from protection. Their account does not describe a gap in a Prophet's vision.
