# The Three Orders at Sunwall

Grain and defenders had already moved around one Sunwall cargo gate. Ju'onai
readers had studied the loaded wall, lift intervals, stores, and approaches.
Their order held the gate and prepared a counter-movement through the cargo
galleries.

A Terjamna champion waited beyond the claimed line. His order was shorter: take
the first opening created by the defenders' movement. A house under his
patron's protection had found futures in which that opening appeared.

The two sponsored house reports did not agree about what followed. They did
agree that armed movement would pass through the cargo galleries. Neither file
included a complete instruction from the Sunwall office responsible for the
configuration.

## The gate closes

The wall record names loaded structures, grain weight, armed bodies, and a
pressure refuge too small for the movement assigned to it. The operating steward
closed the configuration.

The Ju'onai counter-route disappeared. So did the opening the Terjamna champion
had been ordered to take. Grain stopped between wards. Crews were divided by
the closure, and lower stores began issuing smaller shares while messengers
carried new questions to the houses.

Neither army had been ordered to withdraw. The champion remained able to punish
a reopening. Ju'onai forces still held the gate. The steward could keep the
machinery shut and could not settle the war outside it.

## Smaller loads

The load moved only after the parties accepted smaller transfers through more
than one route. Sunwall stewards now fasten an operating leaf to every forecast
that reaches the gate; house comparers attach what each sponsor means to do
after hearing it.

In one Ju'onai teaching version, students calculate the refuge before drawing
the counter-route. One Terjamna account leaves the Sunwall champion at the
closed gallery, waiting while smaller grain carts pass through another ward.

On the steward copy, the pressure refuge remains too small. Present Sunwall
workers who cite the case begin with the bodies a configuration can actually
hold.
