# The Three Orders at Sunwall

At a Sunwall cargo gate, two military orders depended on movement through the same galleries. The steward responsible for operating the machinery gave a third order: close it. That decision stopped both military plans and the grain passing between wards.

Grain and defenders had already moved around the gate when Ju'onai forces prepared to counterattack through the cargo galleries. Their readers had examined the weight carried by the wall, the intervals between lift movements, the stores, and the approaches. The forces were ordered to hold the gate and prepare the counter-movement.

Beyond the claimed line, a Terjamna champion waited for the defenders to move. His order was to seize the first opening they created. A prophetic house under his patron's protection had found possible futures in which such an opening appeared.

The two sponsored house reports agreed that armed movement would pass through the cargo galleries, but disagreed about what would happen afterward. Neither file contained a complete instruction from the Sunwall office responsible for how the machinery would be arranged and operated.

## The gate closes

The wall record lists the loaded structures, the grain's weight, and the armed people who would pass through. It also records a pressure refuge too small for the movement assigned to it. The operating steward shut down that arrangement of the machinery.

With the galleries closed, the Ju'onai could no longer use their planned counter-route. Their movement could no longer create the opening the Terjamna champion had been told to seize. The closure also stranded grain between wards and separated crews. Lower stores began issuing smaller shares while messengers took new questions to the prophetic houses.

The shutdown did not end the military confrontation. Neither army had orders to withdraw. Ju'onai forces retained the gate, and the champion could still punish a reopening. The steward had authority to keep the machinery shut, but could not settle the war outside it.

## Smaller loads

Grain began moving again only when the parties accepted smaller transfers spread across more than one route. Sunwall stewards now attach an operating leaf to every forecast that reaches the gate. House comparers attach a record of what each sponsor intends to do after hearing the forecast.

Different accounts carry the incident into teaching. In one Ju'onai version, students must calculate the refuge's capacity before planning the counter-route. One Terjamna account keeps its attention on the champion: he waits at the closed gallery while smaller grain carts pass through another ward.

The steward's copy still records the undersized pressure refuge. Present Sunwall workers who cite the case begin by asking how many bodies the proposed arrangement of the machinery can actually hold.
