# The Missing Relief Cohort

Later Papsenai accounts call this the Shield Levy Winter. The name returns in
arguments over what an emergency may take from the people maintaining
Ti'asantatca.

## The grain left first

War had reduced field labor and transport while the gulf storm was worsening.
An unnamed campaign authority demanded food from communities that supported the
stormshield. Local authorities released grain before their account separated
household subsistence and seed from anything available for negotiation.

Store workers could show what the collectors took. They had no paired account
of the protection or return promised for it. The campaign authority maintained
that refusing supplies would abandon other communities to the war.

## The empty relief watch

The same campaign tried to take trained shield workers for another target. A
relief cohort and its attendants were assigned away before competent
replacements completed the handoff. Circle workers kept the assignment beside
warnings from the active watch.

Those workers remained at the shield without the missing relief or provisions.
When the storm pressure rose, one protected section yielded. Wind and water
reached dwellings, stores, and shield works. Grain left after the collection
was lost there. Workers were injured or disabled; the surviving papers do not
give their number.

## The claims met after the breach

The store marks established extraction, but they could not show whether the
shield could bear it. Circle records showed the absent cohort, the broken
overlap, and warnings made before the section yielded. Households testified to
the reserve they lacked and the exposure that followed.

The campaign authority kept the wider war before the review. Store, Circle,
and household evidence placed both demands inside the same failure. Collection
had weakened the households and stores supporting the shield while diversion
removed the people meant to relieve its active workers. The Circles retained
technical judgment over the barrier.

The papers never name the campaign, its authorities, or the communities that
supplied Ti'asantatca, and the word *winter* fixes no shared calendar date.

## What later claimants had to carry

The first store marks stayed in later reviews beside demands for consumed seed,
damaged dwellings, and support for disabled workers. The surviving papers give
no disposition for those demands.

Later local assessments identified subsistence and seed before declaring a
surplus available for negotiation. An emergency collector had to leave a
receipt with the taking. It named the source and recognizable amount, the
authority and recipient, and what protection or return could later be reviewed.

Shield workers no longer fell within an ordinary military levy. A worker could
volunteer for another target after the Circle recorded consent and a competent
replacement accepted the handoff. An operational lead could refuse a diversion
that left no credible overlap or recovery body. Emergency collection and
volunteering remained possible.
