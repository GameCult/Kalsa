# The Burial of Offices

The Divided City can sentence a public office to death. The sentence is used
when removing a holder would leave the charter, seal, archive, patronage, and
subordinate machinery able to reproduce the same injury through a successor.
It ends an authority rather than pretending that a different hand on the seal
has repaired it.

Office burial is civic law within the divided sovereignty described in
[[Spoilers/Polities/Prophecy - The Divided City|the Divided City]]. It is not a
continent-wide practice, and it does not mean that an office has a Projection
or can die as a person does. The city borrowed mortuary forms because
[[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|death inventories]] already know
how to keep obligations, property, testimony, succession, and disputed voices
separate when one apparent owner has ceased to answer.

## The continuance petition

An affected person opens the proceeding with a **continuance petition**. The
petition names the office, the act or omission causing harm, the part of the
charter or record chain that keeps producing it, the holders through whom it
continued if more than one is known, and the remedy that appointment of another
holder would still fail to supply. Repeated injury across a succession is
strong evidence. It is not required when the office's own charter makes redress
impossible or assigns the same officer to act, keep the only record, and hear
the appeal.

Any ward clerk must enter the petition even when the accused office controls
the ordinary filing route. One copy goes to the City Mixed Bench; another goes
to a ward archive across the nearest imperial sector boundary. The second copy
does not give an occupier the case. It makes quiet erasure expensive in a city
where no claimant controls every road and archive.

A tenant, ration holder, worker, hosted provider, market user, or dependant
need not identify the hidden patron or prove that successive holders shared an
intent. They must show the public authority that touched them and the mechanism
by which the injury survives replacement. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast evidence]] may expose a planned intervention or a
captured horizon, but a predicted abuse is not enough to execute an office.

## The wake

If the petition shows a plausible structural claim, the bench may place the
office **under wake**. A wake is an inventory interval, not a verdict. New
appointments, destruction or sealing of records, transfer of the seal, and
irreversible discretionary orders pause. Existing ration floors, wages,
custodial care, appeal stays, and ordinary service continue through the people
and material owners already responsible for them.

The wake cannot command a Sunwall keeper to run an unsafe lift, a cult to
interpret its god differently, or an imperial garrison to surrender a claim.
[[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|Technical and relic custody]] remains with
its competent owner. The wake prevents the challenged civic office from using
urgency to enlarge itself while those other owners keep people alive.

Wake clerks assemble the office's charter, seal impressions, appointment and
removal record, budgets, stores, staff commissions, delegated orders, closed
petitions, adverse findings, contracts, cult covenants, machine credentials,
and every remedy still unpaid. The current holder answers as an officeholder
and witness. They are not allowed to dissolve the office first and present the
empty desk as compliance.

The hearing asks whether the office can perform its named public duty while
remaining answerable for its consequences. Concealed patron control, a charter
that makes appeal return to the challenged decision, liabilities repeatedly
lost at succession, and a seal that activates powers nobody can audit all
support burial. Bribery or incompetence that can be removed with the holder
belongs to ordinary discipline instead.

## The office funeral

When burial is ordered, the charter is closed and the seal is killed in public.
A clerk takes its final impression on the **office inventory**, cuts the seal
through that impression, and records where the unusable pieces go. The act is
evidence that this authority ended; it is not a spell and does not erase copies
already carried by a patron, god-cult, machine, or forgery shop.

The inventory names:

- every live order, delegation, levy, stay, appointment, and secret grant;
- every petition, debt, wage, dependant, restoration, and judgment still owed;
- every archive, store, threshold, key, formula, and machine credential the
  office controlled;
- each cult, house, imperial claimant, ward, work crew, and subordinate office
  that acted through it;
- the duties that must continue for immediate life and who can lawfully carry
  them during the gap;
- the records and liabilities proposed for transfer to any future charter.

A buried office has no successor. Its necessary duties may be divided among
existing authorities or established again under a new charter, but staff are
recommissioned individually and powers do not pass by possession of the old
archive. Stores first preserve the people and services already dependent on
the office and then answer recorded redress. The appointing civic authority
owns any duty left without a lawful carrier; it cannot make the burden vanish
by declaring the dead office empty.

This is the point of the funeral. Under ordinary succession, prestige, access,
and revenue climb into the new seat while unanswered injuries remain attached
to the predecessor. Burial makes the attractive powers and the ugly remainder
travel on the same inventory.

## Unquiet acts

An order, collection, detention, appointment, archive closure, or access claim
issued after the funeral under the dead charter is an **unquiet act**. It may
come from a holdover clerk, a copied seal, an imperial sponsor, an inherited
ritual, an Ark interface that still recognizes the credential, or a god-cult
whose covenant names the extinct office. The name describes surviving lower-
world machinery. It does not settle whether any divine or dead person spoke.

Any person burdened by an unquiet act may present the burial entry or the dead
seal's impression to a ward clerk or order officer. Civic enforcement pauses
except for the narrow act required against immediate danger. The actor seeking
enforcement must then name a living charter, its jurisdiction, its record of
adopting the act, and the owner who accepts the resulting petition and debt.
The commoner does not have to discover which conspiracy animated the corpse
before the hand leaves their door.

A machine may still open. A god may still answer. A garrison may still use
force. Office burial does not pretend the civic court commands those powers; it
denies them the dead office's public authority. [[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|Covenant procedure]] owns living cult enforcement, while the
court records the claimed divine continuation without treating it as an
appointment.

## Skinning the office

The common evasion is to establish a new office with the old staff, records,
credential, patrons, territory, and practical power while denying the dead
office's liabilities. City clerks call this **skinning the office**. A new name
does not prove continuity, but neither does it defeat a continuance petition.

A replacement charter therefore publishes which duties, records, people,
stores, credentials, pending cases, and debts it accepts; which authority owns
what it refuses; and what changed in the arrangement that produced the burial.
The office may be narrower, slower, or more expensive than its predecessor.
That is the price of making a different institution rather than fitting a clean
seal over the same machinery.

Ju'onai [[Spoilers/Institutions/Measured Offices|Measured Offices]] dislike the
doctrine because a civic bench can kill the local portfolio through which an
imperial model acted while leaving the imperial claim unresolved. Prophetic
Houses dislike losing service and archive contracts. Terjamna officers call it
paper secession. All three have also used office burial against rivals. The
practice survives because each claimant occasionally needs a way to destroy a
captured civic lever without granting the other two its succession.

## Cost and abuse

An office funeral consumes clerks, duplicate custody, hearings, interim pay,
public copies, guards for archives and stores, and scarce people able to keep
essential work moving without exercising the dead discretion. Experienced
staff lose standing even when they caused no harm. Petitioners can wait through
a wake while rent, hunger, or stigma continues. A hostile bench can bury a
competent office to break a rival patron, scatter a worker organization, or
seize an archive under the language of inventory.

For ordinary residents, the procedure offers something narrower than good
government: a claim can survive the official who denied it, and a successor
cannot inherit the road, grain, seal, or levy while calling the debt an extinct
person's mistake. Its strength is also its threat. Once people learn that an
office can die, every succession must show whether the institution continues
or merely its appetite.
