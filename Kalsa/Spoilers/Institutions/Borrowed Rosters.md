# Borrowed Rosters

Some Kalsan conspiracies publish their membership before most of the people on
the list have joined. A **borrowed roster** mixes sworn participants with
indispensable outsiders, then relies on rival institutions to make the false
membership materially true.

The method works because a roster is rarely judged by one authority. A cult can
withdraw relief, a patron can cancel protection, an archive can narrow access,
a branch holder can demand submission, a house can suspend an assignment, and
an office can divide a portfolio while no court has found treason. Each actor
calls its measure temporary and prudent. Together they can take a named person’s
work, lodging, witnesses, route access, dependants’ support, and standing before
the accusation is answerable.

A conspiracy that prepared the only surviving alternative did not need the
borrowed person’s prior consent. It needed the other powers to behave as
themselves.

## Hands, borrowed names, and after-names

Roster makers distinguish three positions. These translated terms are used
across several political traditions; no common code or continent-wide order
owns them.

- **Hands** know enough of the undertaking to promise a bounded act. A hand may
  be a clerk who releases one copy, a shrine custodian who keeps one household
  on the relief roll, a route keeper who admits one messenger, or a champion
  who delays one seizure. Hands do not need the whole plot.
- **Borrowed names** have not agreed to join. They are selected because their
  office, skill, archive access, kin, clients, or constituency makes every
  response costly. The roster authors expect accusation to isolate them and
  prepare an offer before publication.
- **After-names** are likely successors, alternate custodians, deputies, heirs,
  or adjacent authorities who can inherit the relevant lever if a listed
  holder is removed. They may also be innocent. Naming them makes a purge look
  like confirmation that the conspiracy had already reached the succession.

The circulated roster normally gives each person’s living name, the office or
relationship being claimed, and enough genuine detail to make inquiry
unavoidable. It does not mark which kind of name each entry is. Role keys are
kept by separate cell stewards, divided among witnesses, or never written at
all. A roster with only invented names is cheap slander. A borrowed roster
spends real secrets and exposes some real hands so that its false entries
cannot be dismissed without work.

This is conspiracy by constituency as much as by person. A useful borrowed
name carries people or systems that cannot be suspended cheaply: patients of a
clinic, users of a road, a ward waiting on grain, a dead person’s claimants, a
technical crew, a prophetic house’s dependants, or households beneath a branch
protector. The roster threatens that body with interruption and offers to
restore it through another patron.

## Publication is the recruiting act

A mature roster is provisioned before it is leaked. Its makers arrange safe
lodging, food, counsel, copied credentials, witness access, passage, work, and,
where a divine faction participates, a place on another cult’s relief or
protection rolls. These **harbors** are held by cutouts who may believe they are
supporting an appellant, refugee, dismissed worker, or endangered dependant.
The harbor need not know the plot to make the offer real.

Publication then uses channels that hostile institutions must preserve: a
sealed denunciation, an intercepted courier copy, an adverse forecast packet,
a mortuary attachment, a branch challenge, or a conflict declaration filed
against an office. Several recipients receive independently traceable copies.
Destroying one therefore looks like concealment rather than correction.

The roster’s authors watch three outcomes.

1. If the borrowed name is retained, distrust slows the office, forces divided
   custody, exposes its allies, and makes every later error politically useful.
2. If the borrowed name is suspended or expelled, the prepared harbor offers
   the named person and their dependants continuity in exchange for a message,
   introduction, copied record, future favor, or actual allegiance.
3. If the borrowed name is removed, an after-name or ordinary successor meets
   the same suspicion while the constituency demands that the vacant function
   continue.

Roster makers call a list immature if they profit only when an innocent person
accepts recruitment. The durable form gains a lever from retention, isolation,
or succession. Refusal remains possible; it is simply made expensive.

## Divine factions and mortal machinery

Gods do not need to invent this method, comprehend every cell, or agree with
the mortals acting in their name. A divine faction may include clergy,
potential stewards, champions, bound intermediaries, household custodians, and
patrons who share an account of what their God requires. A manifestation can
confirm one instruction or name one enemy without revealing whether the God
saw the complete roster. [[God-Cult Covenants]] preserves the difference
between cult office, lower-world allegiance, and actual binding.

Borrowed rosters are attractive to divine factions because direct conquest and
great miracles spend potential and advertise the route. A roster makes rival
institutions perform much of the coercion with ordinary stores, appointments,
closures, audits, and guards. The sponsor spends most heavily on the harbor:
reliable relief after another cult withdraws it, protection after a patron
abandons a household, or continued burial and witness support after an office
is declared compromised. [[The Potential Economy]] explains why those material
promises can outlast a weak grant or a silent God.

Different hierarchies favor different levers. A Ju’onai divine faction can use
conflict records, certification review, and portfolio division to turn a
borrowed name into an administrative problem. A Jamnai branch can withdraw
protection and invite the named person to submit elsewhere. A Divided City
faction can place one roster across house, civic, and imperial archives so that
no claimant can clear it alone. These are political uses of the institutions
owned by [[Measured Offices]], [[Jamnai Submission Hierarchy]], and
[[Prophetic Houses]], not proof that every such institution serves one hidden
God.

The method also exploits divine limitation. A God Beast knows through the
relations and evidence available to its hierarchy, not from outside the causal
field. A rival God that withdraws protection from a listed dependant may be
responding sensibly to partial evidence while completing another faction’s
recruitment. When enough offices, archives, welfare routes, and patrons react
through one design, the roster becomes an attempt at the local capture
described in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle Ecology#Timeline capture|Oracle
Ecology]].

## Exposure does not spend the leverage

Borrowed rosters are widely denounced where they are recognized. Knowing the
method does not tell a recipient which names are hands, which are borrowed,
which evidence was stolen from a real plot, or what a listed person has done
since publication. A forged membership claim can be disproved while the lost
job, frightened patron, copied archive, endangered family, and harbor offer
remain facts.

Nor can a responsible office simply ignore every list. Some rosters contain
real plans against grain, roads, shrines, garrisons, archives, or divine routes.
The trap is that individually defensible precautions can do the recruiting.
[[Forecast Evidence and Courts]] supplies one resistance: accusation and
forecast can justify investigation or a reversible safety measure but not
irreversible guilt. Its jurisdiction does not automatically preserve wages,
housing, cult relief, kin safety, or foreign passage while every other patron
waits.

Institutions that have survived the tactic practice **supported division**.
They separate only the dangerous authority, keep the named person and
dependants supplied, preserve independent copies and counsel, publish an expiry,
and investigate the alleged act rather than membership as a moral condition.
Supported division is expensive. It requires another qualified worker,
duplicate custody, guarded access, continued pay, and a patron willing to be
accused of sheltering treason. An enemy can keep filing plausible rosters until
that capacity is exhausted.

Clearing a borrowed name therefore requires restoration at the layer where the
roster bit: office, work, archive, welfare, protection, passage, or public
record. A finding that the list lied does not itself return any of them. A
person who accepts a harbor to survive is not retroactively guilty of the
original conspiracy; they may nevertheless have entered a new one whose price
is now enforceable.

## Costs, failures, and counterplots

Each borrowed name consumes shelter, food, escorts, replacement work, copied
credentials, and a credible patron. Overlong rosters bankrupt their harbors and
teach enemies where the real support network lives. A roster aimed at a person
whose constituency can keep them whole merely exposes the hands used to
publish it. A target with several independent affiliations can refuse the
offered allegiance and carry evidence between them.

The cruelest cost falls on dependants selected as leverage without appearing
on any list. Children lose schooling, patients lose a familiar healer,
households lose burial standing, workers lose an advocate, and travelers lose
a guide because an institution moved against one name. Conspirators describe
these losses as proof that the old patrons never deserved loyalty. The people
paying them may see two factions collaborating through mutual hostility.

Counterplots publish competing role keys, offer false harbors, insert a rival’s
true hand as an after-name, or keep a borrowed person publicly in office while
quietly moving the lever elsewhere. A divine faction can discover that the
mortal cell used its welfare and manifestations to recruit for a different God.
Exposure begins a contest over continuity; it does not end the conspiracy.

## Open pressures

- What happens when two divine factions borrow the same officeholder and offer
  incompatible harbors, each depending on the other faction to make refusal
  impossible?
- Can an after-name refuse succession when a clinic, road, ward, or archive will
  fail without someone taking the compromised seal?
- Which constituency can finance supported division through repeated false
  rosters when the accusing faction controls the nearest granary, shrine, or
  safe road?
