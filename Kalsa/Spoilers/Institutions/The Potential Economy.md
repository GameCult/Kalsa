# The Potential Economy

Magical potential is finite routing capacity, not a second form of coin. Bound
Projections and Beasts make potential available upward through a divine
hierarchy; a [[Spoilers/Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores#God Beasts|God Beast]] can delegate it downward to direct children and through them
to mortal action. Use depletes what a hierarchy can presently commit, rival
bindings can divert it, and concentrated flow leaks to thaumavores.

The metaphysical nature of potential belongs to [[Spoilers/Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores|Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores]]. The
**potential economy** is the set of social procedures by which cults, rulers,
households, champions, and claimants estimate that changing capacity and decide
whose need will receive it.

## No common measure

No adopted canon establishes a universal unit or instrument for magical
potential. Cult stewards nevertheless build practical accounts from observed
miracle strength, recovery time, the number and quality of known bindings,
failed petitions, rival interference, prophetic occlusion, and thaumavore
activity. Their ledgers record commitments and outcomes, not a transparent
substance held in jars.

This uncertainty is politically useful. A temple can attribute a failed cure
to weak faith, hidden leakage, insufficient material preparation, divine
judgment, or an overdrawn hierarchy. Auditors can compare promises with results
and expose repeated failure, but they cannot make the unseen reserve perfectly
legible.

## Sources, routes, and claims

Cult institutions distinguish several positions in a potential route:

- **Contributors** are bound people, Projections, or Beasts whose relation feeds
  capacity upward. Contribution does not prove voluntary consent.
- **Intermediaries** are direct children or subordinate divine agencies through
  which a God Beast can extend potential. A mortal office title alone does not
  make someone an intermediary.
- **Delegates** are clergy, champions, magi, or petitioners entrusted with a
  bounded use of potential. Their miracle still needs the causal levers listed
  in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos#Hard constraints|Kaos]].
- **Beneficiaries** receive protection, healing, harvest stability, safe
  passage, judgment, or public works without necessarily controlling the
  route.
- **Claimants** possess a covenant, office, lineage, emergency right, or
  political promise recognized by the allocating institution. Recognition does
  not guarantee the god will honor it.

[[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|God-Cult Covenants]] determine many of these
claims. The potential ledger makes them comparable enough to ration, while
preserving arguments over the origin and legitimacy of each binding.

## Steward formation and support

Potential stewardship is learned administration under metaphysical uncertainty. Trainees begin by recording petitions, material inputs, promised intervals, observed outcomes, and the difference between a failed miracle and a missing delivery. They learn the covenant categories governing a claimant without being authorized to rewrite them. They also learn when a question belongs to a grower, healer, weather worker, champion, mortuary office, or technical custodian rather than to the miracle ledger.

A steward qualifies through witnessed work across ordinary allocation, shortage, suspected leakage, and a case in which the correct act is to refuse a confident estimate. Training may occur in temples, shrine networks, ruler-supported offices, household federations, or beneath an experienced intermediary. Divine favor is not a substitute for the ability to preserve adverse outcomes. A person able to receive a delegation may remain unfit to ration it among others.

Stewards, petition clerks, auditors, site wardens, couriers, and local delegates require material support. They may receive temple maintenance, wages from a ruler, shares from estates or workshops, lodging along miracle routes, or service contributions from participating households. Their compensation must be recorded separately from the potential they allocate. Paying an allocator with preferred miracle access makes every rejected petition part of their private income.

Appointment identifies the ledger, territory or hierarchy, expected reserve, reporting route, material stores in scope, and authority to pause or redirect a grant. Succession transfers that office and its open commitments. It does not transfer divine delegation automatically. If no successor can observe the same route, the old estimate narrows rather than becoming inherited certainty.

## Allocation and reserve

Potential stewards divide expected capacity among recurring obligations:

- a reserve for defense against rival gods and catastrophic weather;
- grants to champions and high clergy;
- protection of shrines, archives, routes, and food stores;
- household petitions for illness, birth, burial, missing kin, and crop loss;
- seasonal rites and demonstrations of legitimacy;
- debt to subordinate Beasts or allied cults;
- containment and recovery after thaumavore leakage.

The order of these claims reveals the institution more clearly than its
theology. A cult may preach equal belonging while reserving reliable miracles
for officials who keep the hierarchy intact. Another may preserve a strict
household minimum because widespread abandonment would weaken its bindings and
its labor base. Emergency draws can save a city and leave villages without
protection for a season.

Stewards also choose where and when potential moves. Dispersed, brief grants
are harder for thaumavores to feed upon than a continuous spectacular miracle.
This rewards networks of small shrines and trained local delegates, but it also
lets the center blame distant failures on incompetent intermediaries.

## Material leverage

Potential opens reachable futures; it does not supply their matter, energy, or
labor. A harvest blessing needs seed, soil, water, workers, and a weather system
that can still be steered. A champion needs food, weapons, intelligence,
transport, and a conflict whose outcome remains open. A healing rite needs a
body with a survivable branch and practitioners able to reach it.

Temples therefore accumulate mundane resources around divine access. Granaries,
hostels, workshops, archives, training houses, roads, burial grounds, and
trusted suppliers are part of the miracle economy. Control of these inputs can
outlast a weak god. It can also conceal that a celebrated miracle was largely
the work of people whose names never entered the potential ledger.

## Concentrated-working docket

A steward opens a planned concentration after its docket names the intended
result and beneficiary, source matter and energy, prepared site, material
custodian, delegate, withdrawals from other work, known leakage sites,
interruption authority, expiry, and recovery reserve. Unanswered fields remain
marked, including burial or shrine routes that nobody inspected.

Each contributing record keeps its own signature and custody. The material
custodian signs for the inspected system. A death witness or shrine keeper
attaches a presence list. A household preserves the withdrawal of its
protection. The steward joins those records to the allocation without taking
over their claims.

After the working, a **concentration return** records what arrived, what was
diverted or retracted, stores consumed, machinery and bodies harmed, sites that
manifested, recognized presences that went silent, grants awaiting restoration,
containment still active, labor performed, and claims sent elsewhere. Material
support orders follow the return. Divine intent and posthumous identity stay on
their covenant and mortuary evidence paths.

## The household bargain

Ordinary households rarely bargain in abstract potential. They offer work,
produce, ritual attendance, information, hospitality, military service, or a
child's apprenticeship. In return they expect predictable access to relief,
mediation, burial, safe shelter, and protection from rivals or the unquiet dead.

This creates a welfare floor where the institution is strong and a trap where
alternatives are weak. Leaving a covenant can mean losing not only a god's
favor but a grain allotment, burial plot, training relationship, and the witness
needed to contest an inherited attachment. A nominally voluntary economy can
therefore reproduce coercion without any champion drawing a weapon.

Households resist by splitting obligations among several shrines, hiding
surplus, maintaining secular mutual-aid stores, supporting independent
witnesses, or making collective petitions that are harder to punish one by
one. Cults answer with exclusive covenants, inspections, preferred access, and
claims that divided alignment leaks potential.

## Organized allocation factions

Allocation disputes produce durable coalitions rather than isolated complaints.

- **Reserve stewards and defense patrons** control strategic forecasts, guarded sanctums, champion access, and arguments from catastrophic risk. They act by withholding long-horizon reserve, narrowing public ledgers, and funding delegates who can answer an attack. Their real strength is continuity under siege; their recurrent abuse is making every elite priority part of the emergency.
- **Household-minimum coalitions** combine beneficiary lists, neighborhood shrines, mutual-aid stores, healers, burial workers, and collective petitions. They act by demanding a protected floor, comparing failed promises across districts, and sustaining households through appeal. Their records can expose favoritism; they can also defend registered households while excluding migrants and the unlisted dead.
- **Champion and campaign blocs** possess armed followers, transport, patron money, and direct access to high-risk delegations. They argue that a hierarchy unable to defeat its enemies will soon fund no welfare at all. They act quickly and can convert temporary defense priority into a permanent claim on the reserve.
- **Dispersal and leakage wardens** control observation posts, minor shrines, route closures, and records of thaumavore loss. They favor brief local grants and interruption of spectacular rites. Their caution can protect the whole hierarchy while moving reliable aid away from poor districts that cannot travel.
- **Contributor and severance networks** organize bound households, apostates, defeated branches, and advocates who dispute the premise that capacity may be allocated without contributor consent. They preserve covenant origins, coordinate refusal, and threaten to withdraw labor, worship, or cooperation even where metaphysical withdrawal remains uncertain.

These are recurring organized positions, not universal named parties. A temple, polity, or sect may contain several at once. Their resources explain why allocation cannot be repaired by instructing one steward to be fair.

## Brokerage, tribute, and conquest

Potential itself cannot simply be handed over like grain. Access can still be
brokered. A ruler may fund a temple in exchange for priority on campaign
miracles. A subordinate cult may send offerings, trained delegates, or
recognized bindings upward in return for protection. A conquering hierarchy
may seize covenant archives and declare their listed households attached to a
new branch.

Whether a binding can be transferred without the bound person's participation
is unresolved. Institutions act on competing answers because property,
tribute, and military plans cannot wait for metaphysical certainty. Records of
forced transfer are a common origin of later schism and resistance.

Prophets are especially sought because their scale of prescience can enlarge a
god's strategic reach. Their recruitment can become patronage, hostage-taking,
or annexation depending on who controls refusal. Sorcerous conquest makes the
same economy explicit: submission subordinates a defeated Beast's remaining
potential, but completing the submission also drains the victor. Victory can
therefore produce a larger hierarchy and an immediate shortage at once.

Strategic hierarchies also seek a mixture of oracular shapes. Prophets compare
distant branches; opened readers expose local systems; Sorcerers force focused
departures; shamans maintain long commitments; Channelers control crossings;
Luck may bend survival beyond any steward's account. These capacities cannot be
substituted unit for unit. A ledger that counts “three seers” without recording
their operation is political fiction.

Controlling several shapes can bring a hierarchy closer to
[[Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle Ecology#Timeline capture|local timeline capture]]:
the same patron learns the horizon, owns the lever, intercepts rival influence,
and directs the response. It also creates internal competition. Bound agents
may receive different evidence, a compartment may hide an essential response
from the Prophet meant to assess it, and a Luck relation can preserve the God
while ruining the campaign its steward thought survival required.

## Leakage and magical fountains

Thaumavores consume potential passing near them. Stewards map recurrent losses
through failed rites, unclaimed manifestations, altered voices, ancestral
interventions, and places where a god retracts sooner than expected. They close
or rotate miracle sites, negotiate with recognized ancestors, and send
champions against entities classified as intruders.

Classification distributes loss. A registered ancestor may receive a ritual
share. An unregistered war dead may be hunted for stealing the same flow. When
a God Beast dies or a hierarchy loses control of a great concentration, the
reserve can become a magical fountain that supports many dead identities and
sleeping powers. At that point no steward controls allocation. Every claimant
has an incentive to keep the fountain open, close it, or redirect it before the
others can feed.

## Tally ghosts

A **tally ghost** is a known thaumavore deliberately kept at a prepared fork in
a divine route because its feeding response can witness traffic that the God,
intermediary, or potential steward has not entered in the allocation docket.
The practice appears where a hierarchy declares its reserve empty while a
champion, patron, or high shrine still appeared to receive power. Cults that
admit the practice call the presence a leakage sentinel. Household auditors and
severance networks call it the dead witness at the books.

The ghost is not a measure of potential and cannot read the hierarchy's mind.
When a previously recognized sign manifests during a bounded quiet interval,
it establishes only that an accessible flow crossed the prepared fork. It does
not establish quantity, source, intended beneficiary, divine intent, or even
that the answering presence is unchanged. A usable tally therefore joins the
manifestation record kept under [[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|mortuary law]]
to the route custody, the steward's docket, the material work claimed by the
beneficiary, and the withdrawals recorded by affected households. Courts can
preserve those pieces and order an audit; they cannot turn the ghost's hunger
into a verdict.

An intentional test, called **giving the tally its mouth**, opens a small named
grant across the fork while independent witnesses watch the route and the
presence. The test answers whether this witness can still encounter this route
under these conditions. Every answer consumes capacity that could have gone to
healing, burial, shelter, or defense, and every repeated answer helps organize
the thaumavore's [[Spoilers/Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores#Acquired potential bodies|acquired potential body]].
The only way to strengthen or repeat that response is to feed the danger being
used as an auditor. A long investigation can give the tally ghost durable
agency and open an ascent route before it establishes who was lying.

Tally ghosts produce a politics of controlled error. A steward can point to a
licensed feeder when a promised miracle fails, converting deliberate refusal,
an off-ledger grant, or bad material preparation into plausible leakage. A
hierarchy that knows the fork can route around it, starve it into silence, or
destroy its present body; a rival can feed it from another accessible route to
manufacture an accusation. Some tally presences are recorded as accepting the
office in exchange for recognition, offerings, protection of descendants, or a
hearing for an erased submission. Others remain attached to an unremedied
grievance while mortal containment keeps their present body available; nobody
can establish that either condition prevents voluntary release.
[[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|Covenant]] offices dispute whether
any such bargain is voluntary when the witness must remain hungry to remain
useful.

Disclosure is therefore a route, not a revelation. The mortuary witness carries
the answer roll, a material custodian identifies the fork, household copies show
which grants were denied, and a clerk outside the cult preserves the comparison.
No carrier should possess the complete route map before the hearing. Each is a
target for the champion or steward exposed by the joined record; feeding the
ghost to repeat its answer also names a living claimant whose protection will
be withdrawn. Destroying the acquired body can silence both threat and evidence
without proving release. Publishing the fork can expose the lie while teaching
every hierarchy how to bypass its only independent witness.

Tally practice remains local and often illicit. It does not make potential
countable, establish that every unexplained loss is divine fraud, or grant a
thaumavore authority over the living allocation. It gives one narrow external
check on the machinery of [[Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle Ecology#Timeline capture|timeline capture]]:
a hierarchy may own the promise, route, and explanation, yet still leave a
hungry witness where its secret passed.

### Hooks

- When a tally ghost voluntarily releases after years of service, does its
  silence clear the hierarchy, invalidate old audits, or expose an office whose
  solvency depended on keeping one dead person from leaving?
- Can a tally ghost bargain for its descendants' place in the household minimum
  without making every later answer purchased testimony?
- If undocumented grants give a tally ghost independent agency, who has standing
  to close the route, and which suppressed claims become the first obligations
  of a possible new god?

## Failure and succession

The economy fails through depletion, leakage, false accounting, severed routes,
lost material inputs, rebellion by contributors, or promises larger than any
reachable future. Because accounts are inferential, collapse often appears
first as a dispute over blame.

Succession freezes no ledger automatically. A new high priest may control the
archives but not the god's chosen intermediary. A victorious champion may
command the reserve while lacking legal authority over granaries and household
obligations. Rival factions then ration aid to prove which route still works,
turning ordinary welfare into a plebiscite conducted under hunger.

See [[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]] for the special
crisis created by a dead anchor, bound contributor, or disputed ancestor.

## Audit, discipline, and appeal

An allocation audit compares promises, material inputs, observed interventions, failures, beneficiary order, leakage reports, and the private interests of the people who controlled each gate. It cannot count the unseen reserve directly. Its strongest finding is often narrower: the temple claimed shortage while grain, healers, or delegates were diverted elsewhere; one district repeatedly absorbed failure; a champion's “emergency” never expired; or an ancestor classified as leakage received no hearing.

Auditors need copies outside the steward's archive, access to petitioners and local delegates, and protection from the patron whose priority they may expose. Household coalitions, rival cult branches, polity treasuries, and independent shrine networks can commission audits. Each has interests that must remain on the record.

Discipline can remove or suspend a steward, narrow an allocation portfolio, require restitution of material goods, revoke control of a shrine route, reopen rejected petitions, or refer theft and violence to local law. It cannot declare that a god refused someone, cancel a binding, or manufacture the capacity promised in a false ledger. A removed steward hands over records, current commitments, keys, material stores, and known leakage sites under duplicate witness.

A claimant may appeal concealed priority, inconsistent treatment, an expired emergency, falsified material inputs, denial of the household minimum recognized by that institution, or classification as hostile leakage without a hearing. Appeal must be possible before hunger or untreated injury makes victory meaningless. Emergency aid can proceed during review without conceding the final claim.

## The Three Ledgers consequence

The settlement of [[Spoilers/Events/The Three Ledgers Schism|The Three Ledgers Schism]] left the potential ledger institutionally separate from covenant and mortuary records. In institutions that inherit its lesson, material stores and miracle commitments are audited together, and open household obligations travel with a steward's succession rather than disappearing into the new office.

Reserve factions remember the schism as proof that public ledgers can expose a hierarchy during weakness. Household coalitions remember that secrecy had already converted their contributions into elite continuity. The event owns those facts; this note owns the allocative lesson, not the cult succession or mortuary finding.

## The Empty Bridge consequence

Stewards citing [[Spoilers/Events/The Empty Bridge Vigil|the Empty Bridge
Vigil]] put withdrawn shelter capacity on the same line as a concentrated
bridge grant. The entry includes ordinary stores and fuel placed at risk,
workers diverted to drainage and repair, and claims carried by households that
could no longer reach the funded site. The ledger records those costs without
acquiring jurisdiction over the upstream Channeler or divine intent.

## The Ash-Halo consequence

Stewards citing [[Spoilers/Events/The Ash-Halo Armament|the Ash-Halo
Armament]] require source racks, furnace draw, withdrawn shrines, answer-roll
custody, leakage perimeter, interruption owner, and recovery reserve to appear
before a comparable grant opens. Their returns keep forge loss, bodily injury,
blocked work, outer protection, manifestations, and later silences on separate
lines.

## Dungeon pressure

Dungeons form where the potential economy broke but its routes, records, or
material stores remain. A sealed shrine may still honor an obsolete allocation
schedule. A dead steward's ledger may identify households feeding a hostile
god. A buried granary may be protected by a champion whose grant never expired.
A magical fountain may sustain mutually hostile ghosts, each offering the party
a different account of who stole the reserve.

Adventurers can change the economy by restoring a route, destroying an archive,
moving a miracle site, exposing a false shortage, or closing a fountain. Every
choice has beneficiaries. Returning potential to a god may extinguish the dead
who survived by feeding on it; cutting the route may also end the only
protection keeping a living settlement viable.

## Unresolved

- Whether potential has a conserved quantity or only a context-dependent limit
  on reachable intervention.
- Whether contribution scales with worship, magical style, binding depth,
  individual Projection, or several of these.
- Whether stewards can distinguish depletion from deliberate divine refusal.
- Whether potential access, covenant claims, or inherited attachments can be
  transferred without the contributor's participation.
- How quickly different divine hierarchies recover after expenditure.
- Which containment practices reduce thaumavore leakage without harming bound
  contributors or the dead.
