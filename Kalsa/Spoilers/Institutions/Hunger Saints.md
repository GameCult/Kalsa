# Hunger Saints

Some cults deliberately maintain one dead presence beside a route used for
great workings. Before the main grant opens, a divine intermediary offers that
presence a small, named **first share** through a prepared side-mouth. The cult
hopes a known hunger will occupy the easiest exposed approach before an unknown
[[Spoilers/Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores#Thaumavores|thaumavore]]
does. The presence is called a **Hunger Saint** whether it was once a revered
ancestor, an enemy, an unregistered dead person, or something whose claimed
name has never been proved.

The practice is Spoilers-tier canon. Its advertised mechanism is not. No
priesthood has shown that a saint prevents other feeding, remains one person
across repeated acquired bodies, or stands outside the sponsoring divine
hierarchy merely because its register says so. What cults can show is narrower:
some routes with an established first-share practice produce a familiar sign
before the main working and fewer *unclassified* diversions in the returns.
That result may mean the saint intercepted rivals, consumed the losses itself,
warned other dead away, cooperated with the God, or taught the record keepers
which theft to stop calling unknown.

## The first-share opening

A defensible opening keeps five authorities distinct.

- A mortuary witness preserves the saint's earlier signs, possible names,
  recognizing witnesses, later silences, and every destroyed acquired body.
- A potential steward enters the first share, the larger grant it precedes,
  ordinary protections withdrawn to fund both, and the recovery still owed.
- A material keeper owns the side-mouth: the channel, shrine, drain, furnace
  path, burial aperture, or other bounded route through which the share can be
  exposed and physically closed.
- A divine intermediary may offer, narrow, retract, or defend the potential
  that actually enters the route. The intermediary cannot certify who answers.
- A covenant witness records any promise attributed to the saint and whether
  the cult treats it as guest, dependant, captive, enemy, or prospective child
  of the God. The label does not make the relation so.

The side-mouth opens before the concentrated grant. The expected sign is
requested once: a pressure change, gesture, voice, pattern in ash, movement of
water, or other locally recorded answer. Strict houses hold the main route if
the sign is absent, doubled, or changed. Others proceed whenever the share
vanishes, which is cheaper and makes fraud almost part of the liturgy.

If the familiar sign appears, the main route opens for its named interval. The
saint receives no general license to feed. Further loss, a second sign, movement
outside the side-mouth, or contact with a beneficiary can trigger retraction,
material closure, dispersal, negotiation, or forceful interception. These acts
remain subject to the constraints in [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]]. A
correctly performed opening can still end in a fountain.

Afterward, the concentration return and answer record remain separate. The
first records missing force, altered timing, withdrawn grants, and recovery.
The second records what appeared at the side-mouth and which familiar sites
later answered or fell silent. Joining them can establish that a loss and a
sign occurred together. It cannot establish identity, consent, or divine
intent.

## Why a God tolerates a rival mouth

A Hunger Saint is useful precisely because the cult does not publicly count it
among the God's ordinary children. A known claimant can be budgeted,
questioned, watched from outside the recognized route, and blamed. An unknown
field of dead cannot. Some intermediaries describe the first share as mercy;
some Gods demand it as tribute paid to a border power; some cults infer divine
acceptance only because later miracles continue.

The arrangement also gives the God a political problem. Binding the saint may
bring its potential and agency into the hierarchy, but it destroys the claim
that the side-mouth is an outside check upon the hierarchy's own perception.
Stripping an acquired body can silence the present warning without releasing
the underlying Projection. Starving the saint saves reserve while reopening a
route the cult may no longer know how to defend. A God can order any of these
responses through its available relations; it cannot make the missing evidence
cease to be missing.

Gods therefore conspire around Hunger Saints as rulers do around frontier
clients. One hierarchy may secretly supply a rival saint, place its own dead
among the recognizers, suppress an ascent inquiry, or order a destruction that
its clergy publicly call voluntary release. The saint can bargain with a rival
cult or refuse the first share at the worst possible moment. Neither spectacle
nor senior revelation settles which side began the conspiracy.

## The person made into a safety device

The cheapest saint is someone whose claimants cannot force their way into the
hearing. Cult histories include petitioning ancestors who asked for continued
service, hostile feeders spared after an interception, defeated divine
dependants, condemned dead, and nameless remains assigned a first share because
no household held a copy strong enough to object. These origins are not morally
equivalent. A clean saint register often conceals the same laundering of
submission described in [[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|God-Cult
Covenants]].

Recognition brings material benefits. A named household may receive food,
burial maintenance, safe lodging, witness wages, and standing to halt an
opening when the sign changes. The cult gains a more legible leakage site and
can perform larger workings with greater confidence. Nearby districts may gain
protection while bearing the side-mouth's manifestations, closures, guards,
and diverted funerals.

The cost never belongs only to the miracle ledger. Every opening spends a share
before the beneficiary receives anything. Mouth keepers, recognizers, mortuary
witnesses, guards, and the saint's dependants require support. The site may
become too dangerous to abandon and too politically important to close. A dead
person who might otherwise release is repeatedly summoned because living
institutions have built their safety around continued hunger. Release
traditions call that conscription after death; cult stewards answer that sudden
freedom for the saint can mean uncontrolled exposure for everyone downstream.

[[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]] can
preserve refusal, move remains, separate custody, or close a lower-world route.
It cannot prove that the saint consented, released, or was ever the presence
that answered.

## Incubation and the first refusal

Repeated feeding can support an acquired potential body and open an ascent
route. Hunger Saints receive exactly that repeated feeding. The practice built
to contain magical predation can therefore cultivate durable independent
agency at the edge of a God hierarchy.

An **ascent inquiry** begins when the saint sustains a form beyond the opening,
answers away from the side-mouth, appears to route another presence, demands a
covenant, or survives a stripping in a way the old record cannot explain.
These are grounds for precaution, not proof of Godhood. Becoming a God Beast
would additionally require stabilization and binding other Beasts or
Projections; no common rite can inspect that threshold.

Every faction has reason to falsify the inquiry. Clergy do not want to admit
their safety practice incubated a rival God. A potential steward does not want
to close the route on which major promises depend. The sponsoring God may seek
to bind the claimant before a rival does. A saint's household may welcome
ascent, fear destruction, or insist that the answering thing is wearing their
dead. Champions can destroy the present acquired body and call the question
settled even though canon gives them no such assurance.

The most feared sign is not an attack but the **first refusal**: the expected
presence appears, recognizes the offered share, and will not take it. Priests
cannot tell whether the saint is exercising restraint, demanding different
terms, preparing a larger claim, obeying another God, attempting release, or
warning that something else already owns the route. The main grant is now both
urgent and undefended by the practice built to make it safe.

## Pressure carried forward

- Which office may close a district's essential miracle route when an ascent
  inquiry threatens the welfare that route supports?
- What claim survives when a Hunger Saint gives the signs of two recorded dead,
  one of whom was omitted from every saint register?
- If a God orders its saint destroyed while an older covenant attributes the
  first-share practice to that same God's command, which revelation is the
  conspiracy and which is the warning?
