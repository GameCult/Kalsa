# Courts of Returned Seals

> [!note] Canon tier
> Everything adopted in this note is Spoilers-tier author canon. No Public-tier note currently names these courts, and no continent-wide court or universal procedure is implied.

In several Kalsan jurisdictions, the third properly recorded refusal of the same grievance seats a **Court of Returned Seals**. The petitioner does not need a ruler to found it. The three authorities that returned the grievance have supplied its mandate by agreeing, in durable form, that the harm is real enough to redirect and belongs somewhere other than with them.

The courts exist for **orphaned acts**: public consequences carried through recognized offices when no admissible author will own them. A sealed closure may have no acknowledged issuer. A miracle allocation may pass through several cult hands while each claims the god chose it. A valid machine sequence may be submitted under an office seal after the named officer was removed. A forecast may cause guards, employers, and storekeepers to act even though no court entered an order. The material harm remains when every explanation points to another chair.

A returned-seal court does not discover the hidden hand. It makes the offices whose seals, routes, stores, guards, archives, or recognized testimony gave the act public force answer for the consequence before those instruments can be used again without review.

## The three returns

A person begins with any office that has a plausible relation to the harm: the body that issued or enforced the order, held the relevant record, controlled the route or store, maintained the machine, allocated the miracle, or benefited from the act. The petitioner must identify a consequence they suffered or still face. They do not have to supply the complete causal model, name a magician, or know which jurisdiction will eventually own the remedy.

An office may accept the grievance, transfer it to an authority that affirmatively accepts it, or return it. A proper return records:

- the act and material consequence the office understood the petitioner to challenge;
- the boundary that the office says excludes the case;
- the evidence, object, person, or service still in its custody;
- any immediate aid, stay, or preservation measure it took;
- the next authority it says can answer;
- the officer, seal, witness, and date or local reckoning of the refusal.

An oral, tactile, or vernacular complaint can enter the procedure through a clerk or witness; elite High Lojban is not a condition of redress. The notice and translation duties in [[Spoilers/Institutions/High Lojban and Descendant Speech|High Lojban and Descendant Speech]] still apply. An office cannot prevent a return by calling a consequential complaint an inquiry and giving no traceable answer. Where the charter is functioning, silence past its named response interval can be witnessed as a refusal, though proving silence is harder than carrying a seal.

The first return redirects the claim. The second shows a jurisdictional conflict and normally opens access to an advocate, copied records, and subsistence support during further filing. The third return must come from a distinct authority rather than another desk beneath the same owner. It converts the three refusals into a temporary public mandate called **the Third Return**.

The number is deliberately procedural rather than sacred. Two offices can pass an unwanted petitioner back and forth forever. A third distinct refusal demonstrates that the failure belongs to the arrangement among authorities, even if it proves nothing about the secret actor.

## Seating the court

Each refusing authority must send a **binder** able to commit only that authority within the boundary stated in its return. A binder may be a judge, clerk, covenant witness, custodian, steward, household speaker, or another officer recognized locally. Rank outside the returned boundary adds no vote and no command.

A **keeper of returns** opens the docket, compares the three records, preserves copies outside any one refuser's archive, records attendance and nonattendance, and publishes the usable substance of the dispute. The keeper owns the docket, not the judgment. The petitioner may bring a **burden witness** who can state what delay, closure, lost work, injury, hunger, displacement, or exposure is costing. The burden witness does not need to explain the hidden cause and does not become a judge by suffering it.

The court's maximum authority is assembled from powers the three offices already possessed and then refused to exercise. It may:

- preserve and copy records, objects, and testimony already held by a refuser;
- stay an irreversible punishment, transfer, disposal, expulsion, or succession act resting on the disputed authority;
- continue baseline food, shelter, wages, passage, burial support, medical care, or safe service while the owners argue;
- assign temporary custody without deciding title, metaphysical identity, or permanent office;
- require a refuser to state which of its seal, archive, guard, store, witness, or technical route gave the orphaned act effect;
- allocate lower-world answering liability among offices that enabled, preserved, enforced, or materially benefited from the act;
- quiet a disputed seal or route until an office accepts the record and outstanding remedy.

It cannot enlarge technical competence, operate a relic, command a God Beast, identify Luck, decide whether a forecast was fate, create a covenant, settle the identity of the dead, or take authority outside the three returns. Those matters remain with [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|Relic Custody]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast Evidence and Courts]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|God-Cult Covenants]], and [[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]].

## Answering without authorship

The court separates guilt from **answering liability**. If an act could burden the public only because an office supplied a seal, roster, guarded threshold, miracle route, archive entry, store order, or recognized witness, that office may owe restoration even when no individual author is proved. Criminal punishment, cult discipline, technical fault, fraud, and divine interpretation continue on their own evidence paths.

This distinction catches conspiracies built from individually deniable acts. A Prophetic House can truthfully say its report was not an order; a civic office can truthfully say it issued no closure; guards can truthfully say a recognized warning changed their patrol; employers can truthfully say the guards made access impossible. The court asks which institutions gave that unsigned closure force and which benefited while the worker lost wages. It does not turn the forecast into a verdict.

The same rule reaches divine and technical evasion. A cult may deny directing a miracle while its steward selected the beneficiary, its champion cleared the site, and its stores supplied the working. A polity may deny ordering a dangerous activation while its seal admitted the crew and its allocation office took the output. [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Potential Economy|Potential stewards]] and machine custodians retain their bounded evidence. The offices that made the event govern people retain the bill.

## Relief shares and quieted authority

Jurisdictions recognizing the Third Return require consequential offices to maintain an **answer share**. It is not a common currency or fixed tax. It may be grain, shelter, treatment, repair labor, guarded passage, wage support, burial provision, access to a public store, or a claim upon later allocation. Each return identifies what share that office can actually supply. When the third return seats the court, an immediate portion opens before final allocation so survival does not depend on winning the jurisdiction argument.

An office that cannot supply relief must expose the absence rather than promise fictitious abundance. The court can assign later repair, return of goods, restored access, record correction, or a deduction from a benefit the office would otherwise receive. A petitioner is not made whole merely because three clerks finally agree which ledger was wrong.

If no office accepts the orphaned act, the court may **quiet** the instrument that gave it force. A quieted seal, roster, guard route, archive formula, or allocation channel cannot create a new public burden, transfer property, seat a successor, or make an irreversible disposal within the disputed scope. Existing physical and magical effects do not vanish. Quieting is a refusal of further public recognition, not an incantation against reality.

Essential service creates the cruel exception. When quieting the only heat, water, shield, burial, or containment authority would expose dependants, the court may appoint a **continuity hand** for minimum safe service. The hand receives the live record, tools, stores, and stop conditions necessary to preserve life. It may not change policy, settle succession, expand the service boundary, or use survival as proof that its provisional authority should become permanent. This narrow office makes the old authority replaceable without pretending competence can be voted into existence.

## Succession and audit

A returned-seal court does not appoint the permanent holders of the authorities before it. Each refuser selects and may replace its own binder under its existing charter. Replacement transfers the return, commitments, evidence holds, and unpaid relief; it does not restart the case or erase the predecessor's admissions.

When a refusing office is vacant, its current seal keeper, archive custodian, store steward, or acting service holder must either bind the office only for the returned matter or surrender the relevant instrument to quieting. Acting as binder does not make that person the permanent successor. If nobody will carry the office's record, the absence becomes evidence that the authority is already being exercised without an answerable seat.

Audit compares the three refusals with the actual route of consequence: who could issue, authenticate, interpret, enforce, interrupt, preserve, profit, and repair. It also records who was excluded from notice and how delay redistributed loss. A clean chain of individually lawful acts can still produce answering liability when the chain was the means by which every participant avoided owning the whole.

Final outcomes are narrow. One office may accept the case; several may divide remedy; the act may lose future public effect; an instrument may remain quieted pending ordinary succession; or the court may find that none of the returned offices has enough authority to supply the requested remedy. That last finding preserves the grievance and the failed redress path. It does not manufacture a sovereign above Kalsa's polities.

## Commoner use and institutional resistance

For an ordinary petitioner, the practical power is not a guaranteed victory. It is the ability to turn repeated jurisdictional rejection into standing, copied evidence, interim support, and a clock the offices do not wholly control. Petition carriers, market interpreters, sanctuary advocates, mortuary witnesses, work crews, and household networks often help people survive long enough to obtain the third return.

Powerful bodies have learned to defend themselves. A friendly office may accept a grievance and leave it unanswered so no third refusal forms. Clerks may return it without a seal, direct it to a dead office, split one harm into unrelated files, or claim that the petitioner's speech never became a legal complaint. Rivals may forge returns, flood an office with coordinated claims, or engineer an orphaned act to quiet the instrument they want during succession.

Gods and their cults have another option: pass intention through enough intermediaries that every mortal participant can deny authorship without lying. The court cannot punish the god for that conspiracy. It can force the mortal offices that made divine ambiguity govern food, force, burial, movement, or work to keep the harmed alive and expose the routes they used. A god wishing to remain officially innocent may discover that innocence has become expensive.

The institution is feared for the same reason it is useful. It does not need to defeat a conspiracy's account of itself. It makes deniability carry maintenance costs.

## Limits of recognition

No common Kalsan authority recognizes every returned-seal court. The practice travels through clerks, interpreters, courts, cult advocates, delving compacts, maintenance lineages, and households whose grievances crossed several owners. A jurisdiction may recognize third returns only among offices that adopted reciprocal answer shares. Another may honor the evidence but refuse the quieting remedy. An empire may treat the court as sedition; a local ruler may use it to make foreign cults and lineages pay for authority they exercise without admitting jurisdiction.

The institution does not cure unequal access. Poor petitioners struggle to travel, preserve copies, wait through response intervals, and distinguish three authorities from three masks worn by one patron. Offices with private guards can ignore quieting longer than offices dependent on recognition at markets, borders, shrines, or public works. The court creates leverage where relationships still matter. It is paper theater where no one capable of enforcement needs anyone else's seal.
