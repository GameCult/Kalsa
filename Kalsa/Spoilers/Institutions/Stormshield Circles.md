# Stormshield Circles

The Stormshield Circles keep Ti'asantatca habitable beside the southern gulf storm front. The seed describes a circle of the most powerful papsenai shamans continuously weaving a barrier until its members collapse from exhaustion and are replaced. This institution owns the labor and governance around that fact. [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]] owns how coordinated shamanism steers a reachable weather system, and [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|World]] owns the storm and geography.

“Circles” names the whole working institution: the active barrier circle, relief cohorts, trainees, tenders, observers, and the tribal bodies that provision and replace them. It does not establish an in-world title or prove that there is more than one active barrier at a time. The seed's use of “druids” may name these shamans, a specialist office, or obsolete vocabulary; the distinction remains unresolved.

## What the shield requires

The barrier does not create calm from nothing. Active shamans enter coordinated trance, bond with bounded parts of the threatened city or storm system, and steer available energy toward a survivable future. They require knowledge of the weather's present behavior, agreement about the target, an uninterrupted ritual space, and enough magical potential to continue affecting the branch. Rival magic, inaccessible causal levers, exhaustion, and thaumavore pressure can all narrow the result.

The material base is larger than the trance. The shield consumes food, clean water, dry and protected rest space, clothing, medicine, attendants, signal work, observation posts, safe paths between quarters and stations, and enough stored surplus to outlast periods when transport fails. Ritual callers, singers, or dancers may help preserve timing where local practice uses them; their exact offices are not fixed. Growers, cooks, carriers, cleaners, healers, builders, and family caregivers are shield workers even when they never enter trance.

Tools or relics may aid observation, shelter, signaling, or maintenance, but no relic support is currently established. A [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriesthood]] can receive operational custody of a named device without commanding the barrier. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|Relic Custody]] remains separate from Circle office.

## Admission and training

Talent is necessary but not sufficient for active service. Candidates begin with bounded bonds whose failure will not expose the city: a signal marker, sheltered wall, practice field, watercourse, or short forecast interval. They learn to enter and leave trance, describe pressure without treating every image as fact, recognize another worker's target, hand off an intervention, and stop before disorientation becomes collapse.

Training then moves through witnessed participation. A candidate shadows an active cohort, joins an overlap while a rested shaman still holds the target, and demonstrates that they can release a bond as well as seize one. Teachers record failed readings, panic, fixation, recovery time, and the candidate's own account. Power without a reliable exit is a hazard, not mastery.

Tribes nominate or sponsor many candidates because they bear the cost of training and lose the candidate's labor elsewhere. Kinship with prior shield workers can supply knowledge and status, but no blood claim proves fitness. Outsiders, migrants, and adopted members may be eligible where a tribe and the Circle accept responsibility for them; the current canon does not settle common membership rules, age thresholds, or rank names.

## Shifts, handoff, and exhaustion

Continuous protection is produced by overlapping shifts, not heroic permanence. An outgoing shaman reports the target, sensed pressures, interventions attempted, rival influence, bodily condition, and what they do not understand. The incoming shaman begins contact before the outgoing worker releases it. Observers watch the barrier and the workers during the overlap. If the handoff disagrees with the observed storm, the Circle names the uncertainty rather than smoothing it into a confident chant.

Rest is part of the shield. After a shift, workers need food, sleep, examination, quiet, and time away from targets that may continue to dominate dreams and attention. Repeated service can produce tremor, confusion, fixation, loss of ordinary time, impaired bonding, or collapse; the exact medical pattern is not yet canon. A worker who cannot safely return remains entitled to care even when the Circle needs a replacement.

Exhaustion debt accumulates when emergency shifts consume future capacity. Records therefore track more than who served. They track missed relief, shortened recovery, households deprived of a worker, and cohorts approaching unsafe load. A stable barrier with an exhausted relief body is a delayed failure, not a success.

## Consent, duty, and refusal

The shield protects lives, but that fact does not make every act of service voluntary. A defensible admission records what the candidate understands, who requested service, what term or review point applies, what support their household will receive, and how they may report harm or refuse a target. Continuing consent is checked before a new shift, not presumed from the first oath.

A shaman may refuse because the target feels inaccessible, another influence is present, their body has not recovered, their household obligation is urgent, or the proposed intervention would transfer danger toward people excluded from the decision. The refusal produces a warning and a need for replacement; it does not prove sabotage.

Emergency authority may call trained workers when delay threatens immediate mass death. It can prioritize contact and shelter, but it cannot manufacture competence or turn an unconscious body into consent. Any compulsory call must name who issued it, the danger invoked, the shifts performed, the support promised, and the point at which emergency authority expires into review. Papsenai law has not yet settled when refusal can be punished.

Household and tribal pressure often lives below formal compulsion. A family may fear losing honor, food priority, or promised external protection if a gifted member refuses. The Circle must preserve that fact rather than recording every arrival as a volunteer. Otherwise its consent ledger is propaganda with better handwriting.

## Authority and responsibility

Operational leads assign targets, approve handoffs, halt unsafe work, and issue shield warnings. They do not decide land use, evacuate a district by themselves, or command tribal harvests. Tenders and observers can remove a visibly failing worker from duty and preserve the observed state. Trainers control admission to the tasks they can witness, not hereditary status.

Tribal authorities provision cohorts, nominate candidates, represent affected households, and decide how their own obligations are met. Ti'asantatca's political authorities decide civic responses to a warning: closing routes, opening shelters, suspending work, or evacuation. A named external compact may supply food, medicine, escorts, observers, or technical service. The source-promised [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Alliance|Alliance]] remains a candidate model, not evidence that such a compact currently exists.

Responsibility follows the failed authority. A shaman answers for concealing a known limit or abandoning a held target without warning when warning was possible. An operational lead answers for unsafe scheduling or ignored observations. A tribe answers for promised relief or provisions withheld. City authorities answer for delayed evacuation and unequal shelter. Any actual external partner answers only for its recorded commitment. Collapse after honestly reported exhaustion is evidence of system failure before it is evidence of personal fault.

## Failure protocol

When the barrier becomes uncertain, the Circle should:

1. name the affected target and observed change;
2. warn city authorities and exposed districts without waiting for certainty theater;
3. call a rested relief cohort and begin an overlap if possible;
4. reduce nonessential interventions competing for the same workers or potential;
5. move attendants and records to the next protected station;
6. open the civic shelter or evacuation response appropriate to the warning;
7. preserve the shift, provisioning, and decision record for later review.

If a section must be yielded, operational leads identify which lives and structures will bear the transfer. “Save the city” is not a sufficient target when one district is quietly being sacrificed for another. Immediate decisions may remain brutal, but their ownership must be visible.

Several honest targets can also become the failure. A field holder can pull
rain away from seed, a shield cohort can press wind toward an outer section,
and a Channeler boundary can turn active force aside. If each responds only to
the changed pressure it receives, their corrections can recurse faster than
messengers can establish the whole field.

During an immediate cascade, a lead may issue a **narrowing order** for one
named watch. The order identifies the observations admitted to the central
signal, the targets to be released, the cohorts and stores reassigned, the
boundaries other workers must not change independently, and the exact condition
that ends the authority. This concentrates enough information, levers, and
response to keep one shield configuration coherent. It can also decide which
district, road, field, or outside group no longer has an independent future
inside the barrier.

[[Spoilers/Events/The Narrow Shield|The Narrow Shield]] made this emergency
power durable and suspect. Its central refuges survived. Outer land and people
bore the path the order abandoned. Later leads cite the rescue when they demand
early control; households cite the same record when they demand an expiry,
copies outside the Circle, and standing in the review.

A failed shield does not automatically prove bad magic. Possible causes include a storm outside the reachable branch, rival intervention, thaumavore leakage, false observation, incompatible targets, poor handoff, exhausted workers, missing supplies, political delay, or sabotage. The review must test each layer against its own evidence.

## Succession

Succession is a maintained relief body, not the naming of one prodigy after a funeral. Trainers preserve target descriptions, handoff practices, failure records, and the embodied knowledge of workers who survived long service. Multiple tribes must be able to supply and witness the work so that one lineage's death, defection, or bargaining crisis cannot make the barrier illegible.

An exhausted veteran may remain valuable as trainer, observer, advocate, or keeper of a particular storm history without returning to active trance. Families of dead or disabled workers retain claims to promised care and records of service. Erasing them once a replacement arrives converts succession into consumption.

## Historical consequence: the shield levy winter

[[Spoilers/Events/The Shield Levy Winter|The Shield Levy Winter]] owns the adopted wartime episode, damage, and settlement. The Circles own its operational consequences. Shield workers are outside ordinary military levy; voluntary service on another target begins only after a competent replacement accepts the handoff; and an emergency claimant must record the cohort, shift capacity, household support, and relief depth removed from the living barrier.

Circle records treat withheld food and diverted attendants as shield failures before they become individual failures in trance. A city or ally may still request campaign assistance, but the operational lead can refuse a demand that leaves no credible overlap or relief. The event fixes no universal staffing ratio, shift length, exemption form, or account of how the barrier works.

## Historical consequence: the Narrow Shield

After [[Spoilers/Events/The Narrow Shield|the Narrow Shield]], a Circle's
narrowing order names admitted signals, released targets, controlled
boundaries, stores, expiry, and later review. Outer households watch the scope:
officials have used the old one-watch rescue to ask for longer command.

## Hostile cases

- A gifted adolescent is “nominated” by a tribe that has already promised their service in exchange for favorable collection terms.
- An operational lead conceals exhaustion to avoid an evacuation that would damage urban stores and political prestige.
- A wealthy district receives the earliest warning while storm-exposed labor quarters are told to keep the roads clear.
- A shaman senses that holding the city target will redirect destruction toward nonmember settlements outside the shield.
- A Techpriest insists a relic must be shut down during the storm while Circle workers believe the interruption will break their handoff.
- A failed cohort comes from one tribe, allowing rivals to treat a material provisioning failure as proof of hereditary weakness.

Each case tests a different owner. None is repaired by commanding the active shaman to try harder.

## Dungeon consequences

Below Ti'asantatca, old stations can contain abandoned observation routes, sealed relief quarters, broken signals, shift records, and relic systems later generations mistakenly incorporated into ritual. A dungeon breach may alter airflow, expose a hidden causal lever, free a thaumavore feeding on repeated trance, or reveal that one protected district was built over an older sacrifice zone.

Useful expeditions include recovering a missing handoff record, reaching an isolated cohort, reopening an evacuation path, testing whether a buried machine supports or threatens the barrier, or finding the point where an apparent storm pressure is actually rival magic. The Circle owns safe integration with the living shield. Site authority, recovery claim, relic custody, and possible divine claims remain separate.

## Unresolved

- The Circle's in-world names, ranks, shift lengths, training ages, and number of active stations.
- Whether “druid” is a synonym, specialist office, translation error, or distinct practice.
- The barrier's exact spatial form and what observable signs mark each level of failure.
- Common law for compulsory emergency service, disability support, household compensation, and refusal.
- Whether any relic, God Beast, or Techpriesthood currently assists the shield.
- How far a shield intervention can redirect danger outside Ti'asantatca.
