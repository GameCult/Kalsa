# Stormshield Circles

Ti'asantatca remains habitable beside the southern gulf storm front because papsenai shamans maintain a continuous magical barrier. The Stormshield Circles organize that work: training shamans, replacing them during service, supplying their stations, caring for exhausted workers, and warning the city when protection becomes uncertain.

The seed describes the most powerful papsenai shamans weaving the barrier until they collapse and others replace them. Keeping that protection continuous requires far more people than those currently in trance. “Circles” includes the active barrier circle, relief cohorts, trainees, tenders, observers, and the tribal bodies that supply and replace them. The term does not establish what inhabitants call the institution or imply that several barriers operate at once. The seed also calls the workers “druids”; whether that means shamans generally, a specialist office, or obsolete vocabulary remains unresolved.

This note owns the institution's labor and governance. [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]] owns the rules by which coordinated shamanism can steer weather toward a reachable outcome. [[Spoilers/Foundations/World|World]] owns the storm and geography.

## How the barrier is maintained

Active shamans enter a coordinated trance and bond with bounded parts of the threatened city or storm system. Through those bonds, they steer energy already present toward conditions people can survive. They cannot create calm from nothing.

To work together, they need to know what the weather is doing now and agree on what they are trying to protect or change—their **target**. They also need an uninterrupted ritual space and enough magical potential to keep the intended outcome reachable. Rival magic, exhaustion, thaumavore pressure, or an inability to reach the parts of the system they need to affect can reduce what they can achieve.

The workers in trance depend on a much larger supply effort. Stations need food, clean water, clothing, medicine, attendants, observation posts, signals, and safe paths between quarters and duty. Workers need dry, protected places to rest. Stored supplies must last through periods when transport fails. Where local ritual uses callers, singers, or dancers, they may help workers keep time together; their exact offices are not fixed.

Growers, cooks, carriers, cleaners, healers, builders, and family caregivers therefore do shield work even if they never enter trance. A shortage among them can eventually become a failure at the barrier.

Tools or relics might help with observation, shelter, signaling, or maintenance, but no relic is currently established as supporting the shield. A [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriesthood]] may receive operational custody of a named device without gaining command of the barrier. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|Relic Custody]] and Circle office remain separate authorities.

## Learning to serve

Magical talent alone does not qualify someone for active service. Candidates first practice on bonds whose failure will not expose the city: a signal marker, sheltered wall, practice field, watercourse, or short forecast interval.

They must learn how to enter and leave trance, recognize another worker's target, and pass an intervention to someone else. They also learn to describe the pressures they sense without presenting every image as fact, and to stop before disorientation becomes collapse.

Training progresses to work witnessed by experienced shamans. A candidate shadows an active cohort, then joins an overlap while a rested shaman still holds the target. They must demonstrate that they can release a bond as well as take hold of one. Teachers record failed readings, panic, fixation, recovery time, and the candidate's own account. A powerful worker who cannot reliably leave trance can endanger the work.

Many candidates are nominated or sponsored by tribes. Those tribes pay for training and lose the labor the candidate would otherwise provide. Being related to earlier shield workers can bring knowledge and status, but ancestry does not demonstrate fitness for service.

Outsiders, migrants, and adopted members may be eligible when a tribe and the Circle accept responsibility for them. Common membership rules, age thresholds, and rank names remain unsettled in canon.

## Passing the work and recovering from it

The barrier stays active through overlapping shifts. Before leaving, a shaman reports:

- the target they have been holding;
- the pressures they sense;
- the interventions they have attempted;
- any rival influence;
- their bodily condition; and
- what they still do not understand.

The incoming worker begins contact before the outgoing worker releases the bond. Observers watch both the barrier and the shamans throughout the overlap. If the handoff account does not match the observed storm, the Circle must record the uncertainty instead of chanting through it as though everyone agrees.

Recovery makes the next shift possible. Workers need food, sleep, examination, quiet, and time away from targets that may continue to occupy their dreams and attention. Repeated service can produce tremor, confusion, fixation, loss of ordinary time, impaired bonding, or collapse. The exact medical pattern is not yet established. Someone who cannot safely return to duty remains entitled to care even when a replacement is urgently needed.

Emergency shifts can keep the barrier standing by using up the rest and reserve labor needed later. The Circle calls this **exhaustion debt**. Its records must therefore track missed relief, shortened recovery, households left without a worker, and cohorts nearing an unsafe workload, as well as completed shifts. The barrier may look stable after its capacity to withstand the next demand has already been spent.

## Entering service, refusing it, and being compelled

Because the shield protects lives, people face strong pressure to serve. That does not make their service voluntary.

A defensible admission records what the candidate understands, who requested their service, how long the commitment lasts or when it will be reviewed, and what support their household will receive. It also records how they can report harm or refuse a target. Consent must be checked again before each new shift; the first oath does not settle every later demand.

A shaman may refuse because:

- the target feels inaccessible;
- another influence is present;
- they have not physically recovered;
- an urgent household obligation requires them elsewhere; or
- the intervention would move danger toward people who had no part in deciding it.

Such a refusal warns the Circle of a problem and creates a need for replacement. It does not, by itself, establish sabotage.

When delay threatens immediate mass death, emergency authority may call trained workers. It can prioritize contact and shelter. It cannot make an unqualified worker competent or make an unconscious worker consent. A compulsory call must record who issued it, the danger they invoked, the shifts performed, the support promised, and when the emergency power ends and becomes subject to review. Papsenai law has not settled when refusal may be punished.

Pressure also comes from households and tribes without a formal order. A family may fear losing honor, food priority, or promised external protection if a gifted member refuses. Circle records must acknowledge that pressure. Recording every person who arrives as a volunteer would conceal how the institution actually recruits.

## Who decides what

Operational leads assign targets, approve handoffs, stop unsafe work, and issue shield warnings. Their authority does not extend to deciding land use, ordering a district's evacuation on their own, or commanding tribal harvests.

Tenders and observers can remove a visibly failing worker from duty and preserve a record of what they observed. Trainers decide admission to tasks they can witness. They do not confer hereditary status.

Tribal authorities provide for cohorts, nominate candidates, represent affected households, and decide how to meet their own obligations. Ti'asantatca's political authorities decide what the city does after a warning: close routes, open shelters, suspend work, or evacuate.

A named external compact could supply food, medicine, escorts, observers, or technical service. The source-promised [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Alliance|Alliance]] is still a candidate model; its mention does not establish that such a compact currently exists.

Responsibility must be traced to the decision or obligation that failed:

- A shaman answers for concealing a known limit or abandoning a held target without warning when warning was possible.
- An operational lead answers for unsafe scheduling or ignored observations.
- A tribe answers for withholding promised relief or provisions.
- City authorities answer for delayed evacuation and unequal shelter.
- An actual external partner answers for its recorded commitment, and no more.

If a worker honestly reported exhaustion and then collapsed, review should first examine why the system kept depending on them.

## When protection becomes uncertain

The Circle should respond before uncertainty becomes a confirmed breach:

1. Identify the affected target and the observed change.
2. Warn city authorities and exposed districts without waiting for certainty.
3. Call a rested relief cohort and begin an overlap if possible.
4. Reduce nonessential interventions that compete for the same workers or magical potential.
5. Move attendants and records to the next protected station.
6. Have the responsible civic authorities begin the shelter or evacuation response appropriate to the warning.
7. Preserve shift, provisioning, and decision records for review.

If a section must be yielded, operational leads must identify the people and structures that will receive the danger. “Save the city” does not adequately describe a decision to expose one district so another can survive. Immediate choices may still be brutal. The record must show who made them and who bore them.

### When separate protections interfere

A failure can begin with several workers each pursuing a defensible target. A field holder pulls rain away from seed. A shield cohort presses wind toward an outer section. A Channeler boundary turns active force aside. Each intervention changes the pressure someone else receives.

If every group then corrects only what it feels locally, the corrections can repeatedly trigger one another. Conditions may change faster than messengers can establish what is happening across the whole system.

During such an immediate cascade, a lead may issue a **narrowing order** for one named watch. The order specifies:

- which observations enter the central signal;
- which targets must be released;
- which cohorts and stores are reassigned;
- which boundaries other workers may no longer change independently; and
- the exact condition that ends the authority.

The order gives one command enough control over information, interventions, and response to hold a single shield configuration together. It can also remove a district, road, field, or outside group's ability to maintain its own protection within that configuration.

[[Spoilers/Events/The Narrow Shield|The Narrow Shield]] established why this power survives and why people distrust it. The central refuges survived; outer land and people bore the route of danger the order left unprotected. Later leads cite that rescue when seeking control earlier. Households cite the same record when demanding an expiry, copies held outside the Circle, and standing in the review.

### Finding the cause

A failed shield does not establish that the magic itself was performed badly. Review must distinguish among a storm beyond the workers' reachable outcomes, rival intervention, thaumavore leakage, false observation, incompatible targets, poor handoff, exhausted workers, missing supplies, political delay, and sabotage. Each possibility requires evidence from the part of the work it concerns.

## Keeping enough people able to serve

Succession requires trained relief workers already able to take over. Naming one prodigy after a veteran's funeral does not provide that capacity.

Trainers preserve target descriptions, handoff practices, failure records, and the practical knowledge carried by people who survived long service. Several tribes must be able to supply and witness the work. Otherwise a single lineage's death, defection, or bargaining crisis could leave everyone else unable to understand or continue the barrier's operation.

Veterans too exhausted for active trance may still serve as trainers, observers, advocates, or keepers of particular storm histories. Families of dead or disabled workers retain their claims to promised care and records of service. Those obligations do not end when the Circle finds a replacement.

## What the shield levy winter changed

[[Spoilers/Events/The Shield Levy Winter|The Shield Levy Winter]] owns the adopted wartime episode, its damage, and its settlement. Its consequences for Circle operations are specific.

Shield workers are exempt from ordinary military levy. A worker's voluntary service on another target can begin only after a competent replacement accepts the handoff. An emergency claimant seeking workers must record the cohort, shift capacity, household support, and depth of available relief being removed from the living barrier.

Circle records count withheld food and diverted attendants as failures in shield support before they cause an individual to fail in trance. A city or ally may still request campaign assistance, but the operational lead can refuse a demand that leaves no credible overlap or relief.

The event establishes no universal staffing ratio, shift length, exemption form, or explanation of the barrier's mechanics.

## What the Narrow Shield changed

After [[Spoilers/Events/The Narrow Shield|the Narrow Shield]], a narrowing order must name the signals it admits, targets it releases, boundaries it controls, stores it commands, its expiry, and its later review.

Outer households scrutinize how far that authority extends. Officials have used the earlier one-watch rescue to justify requests for longer command.

## Cases that test the institution

These cases put different decisions and obligations under pressure:

- A gifted adolescent is “nominated” by a tribe that has already promised their service in exchange for favorable collection terms.
- An operational lead hides exhaustion to avoid an evacuation that would damage urban stores and political prestige.
- A wealthy district gets the earliest warning while storm-exposed labor quarters are told to keep the roads clear.
- A shaman senses that holding the city target will redirect destruction toward nonmember settlements outside the shield.
- A Techpriest insists that a relic must be shut down during the storm, while Circle workers believe the interruption will break their handoff.
- A failed cohort comes from one tribe, giving rivals an opportunity to portray a provisioning failure as hereditary weakness.

Each requires scrutiny of a different authority. Ordering the active shaman to work harder resolves none of them.

## Dungeon consequences

Old stations beneath Ti'asantatca may preserve abandoned observation routes, sealed relief quarters, broken signals, and shift records. They may also contain relic systems that later generations mistakenly incorporated into ritual.

A dungeon breach can change the living shield's conditions. It might alter airflow, expose a hidden point through which the storm can be influenced, release a thaumavore feeding on repeated trance, or reveal that a protected district stands over an older sacrifice zone.

Expeditions can therefore matter directly to current operations. Parties might recover a missing handoff record, reach an isolated cohort, reopen an evacuation route, test whether a buried machine supports or threatens the barrier, or locate the point where what seems to be storm pressure is actually rival magic.

The Circle decides how discoveries and interventions can be safely integrated with the living shield. That responsibility does not settle authority over the site, recovery claims, relic custody, or possible divine claims.

## Unresolved

- The Circle's in-world names, ranks, shift lengths, training ages, and number of active stations.
- Whether “druid” is a synonym, specialist office, translation error, or distinct practice.
- The barrier's exact spatial form and what observable signs mark each level of failure.
- Common law for compulsory emergency service, disability support, household compensation, and refusal.
- Whether any relic, God Beast, or Techpriesthood currently assists the shield.
- How far a shield intervention can redirect danger outside Ti'asantatca.
