# Road Widowhood

On several Kalsan roads, a household may declare itself **widowed from a living member's service** while remaining that person's family. The declaration withdraws the hearth as the unpaid guarantor of one named outside obligation. It does not declare the worker dead, end a marriage, expel a relative, cancel the worker's consent, or sever a divine binding.

“Road widowhood” is a translated umbrella for related practices, not a universal code or settled native term. *Road* can mean a literal expedition or courier route, but it also means the threshold crossed by an itinerant [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriest apprentice]], a hosted prophet, a campaign worker, a cult champion, or a shaman assigned away from the [[Spoilers/Institutions/Stormshield Circles|Stormshield Circles]]. Public accounts already show households and cohorts paying for roads, levies, and dangerous service. This Spoilers-tier note establishes the refusal those costs produced.

## What the hearth refuses

Institutions usually contract with the person whose skill they want. The worker's household then replaces their ordinary labor, raises children and tends elders through the absence, sends food and clothing, services recruitment debt, receives frightened messages, nurses the returned body, supplies witnesses, and pays for searches or burial. An office can call all of that loyalty because none of it appears on its own account.

A widow declaration names:

- the worker and the office, lineage, cohort, cult, or [[Spoilers/Institutions/Delving Compacts|delving compact]] claiming the service;
- the assignment, road, threshold, term, or missed return review that brought the declaration into force;
- household labor, stores, debts, care, correspondence, rescue, convalescence, and mortuary costs previously supplied or expected;
- dependants whose claims continue regardless of the dispute;
- personal goods and earnings that remain the worker's;
- the witness or copy by which the institution can answer; and
- the condition, if any, under which household sponsorship can be offered again.

The notice may be made before departure, after an assignment changes, or when a promised return review passes without an accounting. Where the practice is recognized, the institution must either release the worker from its claim, accept a named guarantor for the unsupported costs, or record that the person continues without household sponsorship. It may not keep describing the hearth's withheld food, replacement labor, or sickbed as a contribution already owed.

## The person is not widowed

Road widowhood changes sponsorship, not ownership of a life. The worker may continue serving. The household cannot use the notice as a command over the worker's body, movement, wages, private correspondence, worship, or learned competence. The institution cannot treat it as the worker's resignation, confession of desertion, or consent to permanent residence elsewhere.

A spouse or partner remains the owner of their intimate commitment. A child retains claims upon parents and caregivers. A household may redirect common stores and work after naming the effect on dependants, but it cannot make a child's maintenance disappear by putting the absent parent's name on the road. Some partners remain married, share a home when the worker returns, and still keep the service widowed because affection is not a blank draft on the household purse.

The distinction also holds at the divine boundary. A living widow declaration can end a cult office's claim that household support accompanies service. It cannot prove that an inherited attachment or covenant ended. It creates no death inventory and says nothing about the worker's Projection. If a missing worker later dies or manifests, [[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]] receives the bodily and binding claims; the widow notice remains evidence of what the living household had already refused.

## The guarantor after the threshold

An institution retaining a road-widowed worker must identify the body that will answer for the absence. Depending on the service, that guarantee can include food, lodging, wages, equipment, messages, return passage, rescue, recovery time, disability support, dependant support, burial, and delivery of records or remains. “The cohort will manage” is not a guarantor unless the cohort holds stores, authority, and a way to survive the same failure that strands its members.

The practice does not make every journey affordable. It makes refusal legible when the real price was being hidden at home. A shield lead can call a trained worker under bounded emergency authority; the call does not silently conscript the household's seed, childcare, or future care. A delving sponsor can accept a worker after their hearth withdraws; the compact must then place that worker's return and dependant claims upon an actual guarantor. A Techpriesthood can keep an apprentice on an itinerant route; lineage support replaces the household labor it no longer owns. A [[Spoilers/Institutions/Prophetic Houses|Prophetic House]] hosting a provider cannot count a distant family's continued maintenance as proof that the residence remains voluntary.

## Evasion and class

Road widowhood exposes the full cost of prestigious service, so offices learn to evade it. Recruiters prefer orphans, refugees, debtors, estranged adults, and people already called householdless. Apprenticeship terms require families to waive future widow notices. Assignments are divided into short nominal terms so no missed-return threshold ever arrives. A patron gives one elder food and calls the whole household sponsored. A cult keeps children on its beneficiary roll only while the absent worker's relatives perform public loyalty.

These evasions create a new class of dangerous worker: the recruit selected because nobody outside the institution is expected to send a refusal. Dormitories, barracks, training houses, and expedition camps can become real homes of mutual care. They can also let the institution provide every meal, witness, debt, route, and bed, leaving no independent hearth able to say that service has consumed the person.

Poor households use widowhood most urgently and pay most heavily for using it. A wealthy family can absorb an absence and have the sacrifice praised as devotion. A poor one must choose between keeping the worker supplied and keeping everyone else fed, then hears its declaration described as disloyalty. In Jamnai territory, a high-branch [[Spoilers/Institutions/Soiru'i Sects|Soiru'i sect]] may feed a branch widow's children because the dead remain legible as loyal. The same store may punish a living widow whose household refuses to subsidize the next service.

The power can also run inward. A household may threaten widowhood to stop a member taking work, punish an unwanted marriage, seize an apprentice's earnings, or force loyalty to a local patron. The notice gains legitimacy only over resources and obligations the declaring household actually supplies. It never becomes evidence that the worker owes obedience at home.

## Return, repair, and belonging

Return does not erase the declaration. The worker brings their body, wages, records, injuries, new obligations, and possibly relationships formed on the road. Existing property, parental, marital, covenant, and household claims remain with their own owners. Household sponsorship resumes only through a new agreement or the declared condition; the office cannot discharge an injured worker at the threshold and call the old hearth restored.

Repair therefore has several parties. The institution accounts for promised support and the costs it exported. The household accounts for the worker's goods, messages, dependants, and any support it actually continued. The worker decides which service and relationships to continue. Neighbours and sects may supply food or witness, but assistance does not make them owners of reconciliation.

Road widowhood is recognized through particular compacts, household records, sect copies, work associations, and local forums. No continent-wide registry enforces it. Its practical force comes from households refusing together, guarantors needing their labor, and institutions discovering that a road cannot be staffed forever by people whose homes have stopped pretending the absence is free.
