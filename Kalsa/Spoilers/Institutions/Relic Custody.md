# Relic Custody

A relic is something inherited from the [[Spoilers/Foundations/Ark and Fall|Ark]], the interstellar vessel whose fallen Rings became the Crown Cities, or from the systems and works descended from it. It may be an object, component, interface, structure, or surviving process. Some can be carried away. Others are rooms, conduits, foundations, environmental works, or systems that cross several present borders. They are scarce because they are old, and many still provide services people need.

**Relic custody** determines who may preserve, enter, move, service, or hand over a particular relic, and under what conditions. For most people, it appears as a queue, a closed door, service dues, a delayed harvest, a guarded workshop, or an argument over which district gets the working part.

Several people can hold legitimate claims to the same relic. A polity governs the ground. A community needs its output. A household holds a component, a temple tends its shrine, a discoverer has risked their life to recover it, and a [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriesthood]] knows how to operate it. Custody gives these claims defined limits. Kalsa has no universal property law that resolves them all.

## Who can decide what?

The first task in a custody dispute is to separate the questions being argued:

- **Possession:** Who physically holds the relic or controls access to it now?
- **Site authority:** Who governs the land, district, ruin, or threshold where it sits?
- **Operational custody:** Who may inspect, isolate, service, restart, or refuse unsafe work on its particular arrangement of parts and connections?
- **Output claim:** Who is entitled to the water, heat, movement, shelter, information, or production it provides?
- **Service dependence:** Whose life or livelihood will suffer if it stops or moves?
- **Sacred claim:** Who tends the worship, burial, covenant, or ancestral obligations attached to it?
- **Recovery claim:** Who did the work and faced the danger of finding or extracting it?
- **Title:** What lasting ownership does the relevant polity or community recognize?

Holding a relic proves only possession. Repairing it does not make the repairer its owner. Governing the site does not make a ruler competent to operate the machinery. Religious obligations cannot settle whether an activation is safe, and a discovery does not cancel the claims of people already dependent on what was found.

A ruler may adjudicate these claims within their jurisdiction. That ruling is a political decision; the machine itself supplies no such verdict.

These distinctions matter in ordinary disputes. A town may block removal of a pump claimed by a distant polity. Workers may refuse to extract a component from a system they maintain. A temple may permit inspection but forbid dismantling. A ruler may seize a relic and find that possession cannot make it work. Rival Techpriesthoods may disagree while each holds part of the record needed to understand it.

There need not be one court to settle the whole dispute. Local law, negotiated shares, sureties, exchanges of hostages or witnesses, reciprocal access, temporary service priority, or a continuing division of authority may provide a settlement. Which arrangements dominate in each polity remains unresolved.

## Keeping a record people can use

A custody record should let someone establish what the relic is, what has happened to it, and what the current custodian is allowed to do. A defensible record identifies:

- the relic, its location, and its current arrangement;
- its visible condition and known function;
- the people currently dependent on it and everyone claiming rights over it;
- known hazards and limits on access;
- the work presently authorized;
- parts removed or substituted;
- witnesses and failed attempts;
- when the custody decision must be reviewed.

For a portable relic, the record should travel with it. For a fixed system, copies should be available at more than one access point where possible.

No single medium is required. The record may be written, marked on a component, remembered through ritual, copied into diagrams, or witnessed by several parties. What matters is whether it connects actions to their consequences. An impressive pedigree is poor evidence if it leaves out the cracked seal or the disputed claimant.

A custodian may withhold a route, interface, or procedure when disclosure would invite immediate harm. The exception must identify the danger it addresses. Keeping one dangerous threshold secret does not justify concealing every failure, fee, claimant, or dependency associated with the relic.

Good records make it possible to trace a removed component, establish who performed a repair, and require an emergency custodian to return authority. They can also leave ordinary households dependent on documents and specialists they cannot inspect for themselves. Recordkeeping does not remove that imbalance.

## Learning the work

A prospective custodian first learns kinds of work, then receives an appointment to a particular relic configuration: the actual arrangement they will be responsible for operating.

Training may come from a [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriest lineage]], a local maintenance body, apprenticeship with a previous custodian, recovered records, or a combination of these. A lineage name alone proves no competence.

A defensible formation record shows what the candidate has actually learned and done:

- the systems and configurations studied;
- inspections, stops, repairs, restarts, and failures experienced under supervision;
- tools, measures, languages, formulae, and interfaces used in practice;
- hazards explained to workers and dependants in language they could act on;
- limits the candidate recognized and work they correctly refused;
- workers, local keepers, translators, and prior custodians who can witness the record;
- circumstances in which the candidate still needs supervision or further competent comparison.

This training grants no universal license. Someone trained on a Crown lift does not thereby own a terraforming intake. Analytical insight cannot replace practical experience. An inherited formula may survive even when the meaning of its units has changed. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Measures, Formulae, and Machine Speech|Measures, Formulae, and Machine Speech]] addresses that problem.

## Appointing a custodian

An appointment gives a qualified person or team operational responsibility for a named configuration and its dependants. Who makes the appointment depends on the site. The parties may include the ground authority, communities receiving the output, a prior custodian, a competent lineage witness, work crews, a temple, or the household possessing the threshold. There is no universal appointing court.

The appointment must make the job's boundaries legible. It records:

- the relic, threshold, configuration, and known extent of the system;
- which inspections, isolations, services, substitutions, restarts, and refusals are authorized;
- the outputs involved and the people dependent on them;
- who controls access and the conditions for admission;
- the records, tools, keys, formulae, and stores being transferred;
- assistants, translators, workers, and relief coverage;
- support owed to the custodian and crew;
- duties concerning reporting, secrecy, conflict, and review;
- the term of office, emergency authority, succession plan, and process for removal.

Within that boundary, the custodian makes operational decisions. Appointment does not transfer land, rights to allocate output, title, cult authority, salvage rights, or authority to decide the identity of a possible person within a relic.

A settlement can appoint the person who maintains its water supply without gaining the competence to override a stop based on a physical hazard. A lineage can attest that a custodian is competent without acquiring the settlement's water.

## Paying for the work

A custodian needs other people's labor and resources to do the job. Naming someone responsible while withholding food, shelter, tools, assistants, access, replacement material, record copies, relief, or inspection time leaves them answerable for work they cannot perform.

Support terms should say who supplies:

- ordinary livelihood and compensation for dangerous work;
- crews for cleaning, carrying, guarding, and observing local conditions;
- tools, measures, formula copies, and access to calibration;
- interpreters for technical work and public notices;
- medical care, rescue, and support for injured workers or dependants;
- substitutes and rest during prolonged failures;
- secure duplicate records outside the custodian's sole control.

Dependants may make support conditional on service. They cannot require the custodian to certify an unsafe act. The custodian, in turn, may not shut a public output merely to improve personal terms.

When promised support fails, the custodian must record what is missing, how that limits the work, who is exposed to harm, and what must be supplied or changed before work can safely continue.

## Entering the site and doing the work

Before anyone begins, the acting parties should establish which relic and configuration the work concerns, what prompted access, who may enter, what may be changed, which observations must be preserved, and who decides whether service can resume.

A Techpriest may stop work within an unsafe operational boundary. Once output is safely restored, that technical judgment does not determine who receives it.

Entry itself may require permission from more than one authority. At [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]], Low Sere controls access to the drained cistern threshold. The appointed custodian controls the pressure seal and technical admission. A lawful descent requires both; either key by itself is insufficient.

## When danger cannot wait

An immediate danger can justify acting outside ordinary permission. Emergency work may include opening a sealed path, moving a relic away from fire or flood, interrupting a service, removing a failing part, or entering a restricted chamber to rescue people.

Emergency custody lasts only for the named danger and the work needed to stabilize it. Once the danger is contained, the actor must preserve a record of the changes, identify losses, notify affected claimants, and either return the relic or bring any continuing dispute before the authorities that actually exist. Saving a relic establishes service and risk. It does not grant every right over it.

Sometimes the necessary act is refusal. A custodian may decline a ruler's activation order if it exceeds known limits or places danger on people excluded from the decision. The ruler may punish that refusal as sacrilege, theft, insubordination, or sabotage. Whether the work was unsafe and whether the refusal was politically punishable remain separate questions, even when the same person must answer for both.

## Complaints, suspension, and removal

Custodians are accountable for what they do within their authority. Grounds for discipline can include:

- concealing an intervention or falsifying a record;
- undertaking work beyond their training;
- selling access or diverting output;
- refusing to preserve evidence;
- abandoning the role without emergency notice;
- repeatedly failing to communicate hazards;
- becoming unable to continue operating safely.

A complaint is a reason to inquire, not proof of failure. The inquiry must distinguish what the custodian could know and do at the time from what became clear afterward. It examines separately:

- the operational claim made and the evidence then available;
- the support, workers, formulae, and access actually supplied;
- the public instructions given and whether affected people could understand them;
- political orders and overrides;
- the physical outcome and knowledge gained later;
- acts within the appointment and acts outside it.

Immediate suspension is legitimate if continued access presents a named danger. It transfers only the control over thresholds, tools, and records needed to stabilize the site. It establishes neither guilt nor permanent title.

Removal requires more than stopping an unsafe intervention. The site's state must be preserved, an inventory made, and the work subjected to competent comparison. Dependants must receive notice. The custodian and workers must have a chance to answer, and there must be a handoff plan.

Review belongs to a local body: the appointing parties or their successors, with an independent competent witness where one can be obtained. A lineage can testify about training without automatically deciding who holds the settlement's office. Dependants can testify about harm without making a vote proof of a technical claim.

The inquiry may lead to a warning, narrower authority, supervised practice, additional support, restitution, suspension, removal, or referral of theft or violence to local law. An unsupported complaint may end in restoration. Removal alone does not strip a custodian of wages, property, testimony, appeal, or credit for completed work; any such loss requires a separate decision by the relevant authority.

## Replacing a custodian

A handoff transfers only the rights it names. Sending a component to a workshop may give the workshop possession and permission to repair it while leaving title and authority to reinstall it elsewhere. A loaned key does not transfer the chamber. Emergency entry does not establish a permanent route.

Records and tools are necessary, but receiving them does not demonstrate that a successor understands the system as it now exists.

Custodians train relief because sudden incapacity can be expected, even when its date cannot. A proper handoff should include a witnessed walk through the live system's boundary and an account of:

- the current configuration and known substitutions;
- active hazards, unresolved claims, and recent failures;
- differences in formulae and units;
- worker contacts and notices to dependants;
- work the successor is not yet qualified to perform.

If the custodian cannot continue, dependants need warning. An apprentice may receive provisional authority to isolate the system and perform ordinary service while a competent comparison is sought. That provisional appointment must expire or narrow at a named review. It cannot become permanent simply because people have grown accustomed to it.

An outgoing custodian may surrender the threshold and public record while refusing to give a dangerous formula to an unqualified successor. Such a refusal requires competent review and safe escrow where available. Withholding every record to force reinstatement breaches the handoff obligations. So does demanding every key before naming a qualified successor.

## Where appeals go

An appeal goes to the authority responsible for the disputed matter. There is no universal relic tribunal.

- **Formation or technical findings:** a custodian seeks an independent competent comparison accepted locally.
- **Support, output closure, secrecy, or misuse:** dependants appeal to the authority that appointed and sustains the office.
- **Notice, wages, injury, or changed work:** workers use their compact or local forum.
- **Kin and cult claims:** follow [[Spoilers/Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]].
- **Site entry, recovery, and hazards:** follow [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic and Ruin Law|Relic and Ruin Law]].
- **Delving crew claims:** follow the crew's [[Spoilers/Institutions/Delving Compacts|Delving Compact]].

An appeal cannot compel an unsafe restart. A stop remains in force until the named hazard changes or another qualified custodian records a different, explicitly bounded operational claim. At the same time, withholding access to review must not allow an ousted custodian to retain a monopoly over a settlement's service.

## What the Grey Scald requires reviewers to separate

[[Spoilers/Events/The Grey Scald|The Grey Scald]] is the sole owner of the adopted facts of that event. Its consequence for custody is a requirement to review emergency suspension, discipline, technical comparison, mortuary claims, handoff, and succession separately. Deciding one must not silently decide the rest.

The present [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|Ashen Intake]] descent can recover the missing body and evidence. That recovery makes review possible. It does not settle Teren's appeal or Sera's appointment.

## Finding relics in ruins

The buried Crown Cities contain parts detached during the Fall, abandoned after later failures, cached during wars, or still connected to systems whose extent can no longer be seen. A recovery party gains possession under uncertain conditions. It does not begin with clear title.

Before moving an object, the party should record where it sat, what remained connected to it, and any signs of continued service, warning, burial, habitation, or restraint. It should also record what changes when the object moves.

Apparently inert parts can still matter. One may be keeping a seal closed. A closed door may secure treasure, maintain quarantine, imprison a claimant, or serve several of these purposes at once. [[Spoilers/Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]] does not remove the need to inspect how the system works. No style reveals every distant dependency.

Recovery claims give weight to labor and danger that might otherwise go unrecognized. Those claims weaken when extraction destroys evidence needed to understand the relic, disables a public work, violates an acknowledged burial, or ignores a living claimant.

There is no universal canonical division of shares among discoverers, carriers, sponsors, local rulers, priesthoods, and dependent communities.

## When a relic seems to respond

A relic may speak, refuse an action, make a prediction, show an apparent preference, or become associated with repeated coincidences. These observations deserve preservation and a limited, careful inquiry. They do not identify their own cause.

Ark survivals may include imprisoned Posthumans, Promethean processes, adaptive systems, and ritual projections. Any particular relic may contain none of them. [[Spoilers/Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores|Thaumavores]] can also manifest where magical potential concentrates.

Control of a container therefore does not settle anyone's authority over a possible person within it. Operators should record the behavior, competing explanations, and any proposed act that would erase the only available evidence. Immediate danger may still require isolation or shutdown. Neither reverence nor skepticism gives an operator permission to destroy the subject of the dispute.

Present canon supplies no universal personhood test for relic minds, ghosts, gods, or simulations. It also does not establish that any particular Crown relic contains one.

## What custody makes possible in a dungeon expedition

An expedition may be hired to map the extent of a service, restore the records connecting past interventions, witness a handoff, retrieve a named part, recover a prior crew, isolate a spreading failure, or carry evidence between claimants who cannot safely meet. Bringing home a valuable object is only one kind of result.

Even a successful recovery leaves distinct questions to resolve. The party may possess the relic while a Techpriesthood recognizes its configuration, a site authority permits its movement, a village claims its output, a temple demands rites, or a possible inhabitant refuses activation. None of those outcomes entails the others. The return journey can matter as much as the descent.

No universal right of salvage, right of conquest, priestly veto, or polity monopoly has been adopted. Until a polity note establishes otherwise, dungeon finds remain subject to claims concerning site, service, hazard, sacred obligations, recovery, and possible persons.

[[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]] is the canonical direct-entry test of these competing claims. The settlement needs the output. A custodian controls technical entry. A former custodian contests removal. Kin claim remains along the route. The delving party can recover and possess the missing calibration object without gaining permission to sell or install it.

## Unresolved

- Whether any Crown City keeps a durable relic registry.
- Which kinds of relic may be privately held, exported, dismantled, or used in war.
- How custodians recognize configuration identity after centuries of substitution.
- What evidence is required before anomalous behavior is treated as a possible person or divine presence.
- How claims survive conquest, migration, abandonment, or the extinction of a dependent community.
- Regional formation standards, appointment terms, support baselines, discipline, removal, and custodian appeals outside Low Sere.
