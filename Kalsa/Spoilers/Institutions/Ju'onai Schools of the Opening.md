# Ju'onai Schools of the Opening

The **Study of Openings** is the translated common name for Ju'onai traditions
that investigate sudden comprehension, causal records, and the limits of
intervention. An **opening** is the experience of a bounded system becoming
newly legible: its joins, tensions, dependencies, and possible hinges seem to
stand exposed. The author crosswalk calls the underlying Gift **Analysis** and
compares it through the **Clarity** axis. No Ju'onai school possesses that
backstage taxonomy.

The field includes people who experience openings and people who never have.
An **opened reader** gives a firsthand account of such an episode. A **learned
reader** reconstructs, tests, criticizes, teaches, or applies causal knowledge
without claiming revelation. Both may be called readers. The distinction
matters because elite institutions often treat firsthand experience as
authorship while reducing the observations of workers and learned readers to
raw material.

These names are English translations, not reconstructed descendant Lojban.
Ju'onai legal, temple, vernacular, client, and scholarly registers use different
words whose exact forms remain unresolved.

Scholarship makes evidence transmissible. It does not acquire every authority
that may later act upon that evidence.

## Working vocabulary

- A **frame** is the system treated as the subject of an account.
- A **join** is a dependency inside that frame.
- A **hinge** is a place where an accessible intervention may alter what follows.
- A **carry** is understanding retained after an opening and made available for
  later work. A carry can be useful and still be transferred beyond its proper
  frame.
- An **aftermark** is evidence that an outside magical intervention changed an
  active system. It does not by itself identify the actor, Gift, intention, or
  legal responsibility.
- A **named edge** states what a frame excludes: people, flows, places, source
  records, and the time at which the claim must be reviewed.
- **Confidence marks** record that a proposition is affirmed, held open, or
  ruled out within an account. They report the reader's position, not reality's
  obedience.

No school uses all of these terms identically. Imperial forms make them appear
more settled than classroom, temple, field, and client practice actually are.

## Scholarly lineage

A Ju'onai scholarly lineage is a chain of teachers and students carrying
methods, contradictory cases, commentary rights, and archive duties. It is not
a bloodline. Families can capture lineages by controlling tutors, rooms,
stipends, archives, temple appointments, and access to consequential cases.
That gives descendants preparation and patronage, not inherited truth.

Succession transfers responsibility for an archive, unfinished students,
disputes, teaching rooms, instruments, and material trusts. It does not transfer
an opening, certificate, administrative portfolio, divine relation, or right to
operate a relic. A lineage divides when teachers and records separate. One
reader may study in several and owe duties to each.

## Concordance Temples

Concordance teachers say an opening occurs when a mind, the idea embodied by a
system, and a concept-and-feeling god briefly align. The veil lifts because
divine order becomes momentarily legible. Their best evidence is experiential:
openings during service, repeated associations between particular gods and
kinds of insight, and successful work by temple-supported readers.

Temple estates, offerings, welfare stores, patron households, diagram halls,
and clerks make long contemplation and comparison possible. Teacher-initiation
and temple appointment carry the tradition forward. A successor may inherit a
school and archive without inheriting the predecessor's divine favor.

The model takes the felt force of revelation seriously and explains why ideas,
emotion, worship, and political standing affect Ju'onai scholarship. It also
makes patronage look like evidence. Independent openings can be dismissed as
theft or unnamed favor; failure inside a god's supposed domain becomes impurity
or insufficient concordance. A god able to perform certainty can make its own
blind spot into curriculum.

## Trial Boards

Board traditions say a system becomes knowable when placed under bounded
pressure. An opening reveals the moves by which a reader can prevent loss,
reach a stated result, or expose a hidden rule. They prize repeatable
intervention, unfamiliar cases, perturbation, and records showing which
prediction survived an adversarial change.

Their material body is expensive: test rooms, scale mechanisms, route tables,
game boards, candidate lodging, witnesses, clerks, adverse-case archives, and
occasionally dangerous Crown interfaces kept by Techpriests. Board teachers
pass on case archives only after apprentices demonstrate test design and
preservation of failed results. The formal power to issue a certificate belongs
to commissioned [[Spoilers/Institutions/Houses of Attestation|Houses of Attestation]], not to
every scholarly board or teacher using the method.

The tradition excels where a frame can be stressed honestly. Its central fraud
is to forget that the sponsor chose the board, goal, pieces, and stopping point.
A candidate can learn the trial rather than the world. A community can become
an adversary in somebody else's exercise. Ancient machinery may still reward a
Promethean purpose no present teacher recognizes.

## Four-Because Chairs

Four-Because teachers separate four questions that patrons often collapse:

1. What physically brought the result about?
2. What moved an actor to choose or refuse an act?
3. What reason is offered to justify it?
4. What follows under the explicit rule being invoked?

They teach that an opening may illuminate the first question and sometimes the
consequences relevant to the fourth. It does not read motive, manufacture
justification, or choose which law governs. Their chairs grow around court and
administrative archives, parallel translations, disputed cases, scribes,
interpreters, and public commentary. Funding comes from courts, cults,
portfolios, schools, and litigants who rarely lack preferred conclusions.

A chair passes through apprenticeship, commentary on contradictory authorities,
and custody of source copies rather than descent. In practice, families can
capture appointment by controlling stipends and texts. The school's precision
keeps explanation separate from verdict, but inherited grammar can masquerade
as the natural structure of thought. Fine distinctions cannot replace a
missing field witness, and elite speech can make vernacular evidence sound
conceptually defective.

## Named-Edge Circles

Named-Edge Circles are client, worker, clerk, and low-expression reform
lineages. They teach that no opening arrives without a frame, and no defensible
claim leaves its edge unnamed. A full account records excluded dependencies,
source witnesses, intended intervention, expected consequence, and the time at
which material results must be reviewed. Domain workers enter as contributors,
not anonymous inputs beneath a prestigious synthesis.

Circles survive through copied petition rolls, client archives, travel funds,
field schools, survey materials, translation networks, food shares for worker-
teachers, and mutual aid for people challenging powerful sponsors. Rotating
archive stewards recognize apprentices through fieldwork and preservation of
adverse testimony. There is no central Circle able to license the rest.

The method catches elegant claims that fail just outside a certified boundary.
It can also demand a frame so broad that nobody can act, treat every omission as
bad faith, or discount genuine sudden insight because its source is difficult
to audit. Naming an edge does not make the edge honest.

## Teaching, publication, and class

Students enter through temple schools, family tutors, public colleges, client
academies, office apprenticeships, work crews, and private commentary circles.
Training consumes food, lodging, copied cases, safe mechanisms, teacher time,
travel, witnesses, and years away from ordinary earning. Patrons finance that
cost in exchange for service, ideological loyalty, future office, or control of
publication.

A useful scholarly account names its frame, sources, confidence, missing
observations, proposed hinge, intended use, and review horizon. Schools preserve
failed cases because success without adverse comparison becomes lineage
advertising. Publication ranges from public wall copies and teaching cases to
sealed temple commentaries and dangerous relic reports. Custody can protect a
person or system; it can also ensure that only a patron's descendants learn why
the patron was wrong.

**Four Readings of a Broken Sluice** is a common teaching-dialogue family rather
than one fixed text. Copies let representatives of the four lineages dispute a
failed waterwork while a keeper challenges their authority, attribution, and
ability to secure repair. Client schools often preserve the keeper's final
question; prestigious central copies are more likely to end when the student
selects a method.

Opened readers dominate prestige because their experience is dramatic and
difficult to counterfeit. Learned readers, clerks, translators, maintainers,
and domain workers make the experience comparable across time. The field
depends upon their records while repeatedly denying them authorship. Public
schools and Named-Edge Circles challenge that extraction; established houses
answer that undisciplined testimony makes every model hostage to grievance.

## Historical stress: the Santabi'u Silt Appeal

[[Spoilers/Events/The Santabi'u Silt Appeal|The Santabi'u Silt Appeal]] owns the
event. Its prepared gates truly carried the stated flow. The valley did not,
because the commissioned frame excluded silting lateral channels reported by
local keepers.

Trial reformers use the case to require transfer tests and client-supplied
adversarial cases. Four-Because Chairs teach that a correct mechanism neither
described the whole valley, entailed increased capacity, justified the levy,
nor proved resistance. Named-Edge Circles treat the preserved petitions and
lateral marks as foundational evidence for contributor authorship and delayed
review. Concordance Temples split: reformers say bounded sight was converted
into cosmic warrant, while triumphalists blame corrupt alignment, client
resistance, or the wrong divine patron.

The appeal does not prove that any school already existed in its current form.
Each lineage uses the wound to claim antiquity and necessity. The valley's
keepers reply that an imperial school can cite their suffering for generations
without restoring what the levy consumed.

## Refusal and scholarly discipline

A reader may refuse to extend a carry beyond its frame, suppress an adverse
case, erase a contributing witness, or let a sponsor present confidence as
verdict. A teacher may refuse a student unsafe access while recording the
reason and a review path. A client school may reject an imperial translation
that changes the question it was asked.

Scholarly discipline includes correction, loss of commentary rights, removal
from archive custody, public adverse notes, withdrawal of patronage, and
referral of fraud to the authority harmed. A school can discipline scholarship;
it cannot privately revoke civil standing, office, certificate, cult membership,
or technical custody owned elsewhere.

## Play and dungeon seams

- Four schools hire the party to recover one old trial record, each because its
  missing adverse result changes a different doctrine.
- A keeper demands co-authorship before revealing the only route through a
  failing waterwork; the patron offers the party more to list her as a witness.
- A Crown interface opens for a learned reader whom a Trial Board refuses to
  certify, separating machine response from imperial status.
- A concept god orders its Concordance Temple to suppress a failure that may
  instead prove the god has misunderstood its own domain.
- A Named-Edge survey exposes a dependency so broad that immediate repair
  becomes impossible; people downstream cannot wait for epistemic purity.
- A Four-Because commentary proves why an accusation does not follow from an
  aftermark, while the actual intervention remains dangerous and unidentified.

## Unresolved

- Exact descendant-language names, pronunciations, and the relationship among
  high, vernacular, temple, client, and administrative terms.
- The etymology of Ju'onai and recurring `-nai` forms.
- Which lineage is oldest and which histories are retrospective claims.
- Whether any god causes openings, merely shapes them, or mistakes correlation
  for authorship.
- Whether aftermarks can ever identify a magical tradition rather than a local
  intervention.
- How an opening fails to observe its own operation.
- Whether any Trial Board preserves genuine Crown pedagogy rather than a
  post-Fall reconstruction.
- Regional and client models that reject the Study of Openings altogether.
