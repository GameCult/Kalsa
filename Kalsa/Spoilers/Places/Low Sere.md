# Low Sere

Low Sere is a coldward settlement whose heat and water come from [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]], a buried terraforming work. The relic's surviving filters process a mineral-laden underground flow, making its water and heat usable. Forty-one occupied household hearths stand in a dry basin below the intake slope. They can live here because its warm discharge makes the hollow less hostile than the surrounding plateau.

That supply is close to failure. Three of the intake's eight shutter indicators remain pale, and another closes with each pressure pulse. The settlement has commissioned a descent to recover missing equipment and records, inspect the machinery, and bring back evidence for two long-delayed custody hearings.

Low Sere governs its own work, daily life, claims to use the output, and local permission to enter the site. Dependence on the intake does not give the settlement authority to decide how it can safely operate. At the entrance, local admission and technical permission are held by different people.

## How heat and water reach the settlement

Low Sere has four working areas. Water passes through several of them, so a failure at the intake spreads through much of settlement life.

### Cistern House

The Cistern House stands over two settling basins. Warm grey water enters the east basin and leaves mineral ash behind as it settles, then passes into the covered lower cistern. The west basin can be drained for inspection. A stair behind its inner wall leads to the intake's pressure seal and the dungeon below.

Water marks, ration boards, sampling vessels, drain wheels, tools, and duplicate service notices fill the house. Maro Seln controls the settlement's drain wheel and iron access grate. Sera Venn holds the eight-tooth spindle that releases the pressure seal. Normal entry needs both of them.

### Warm Steps

Channels carry warm outflow from the Cistern House through the stone terraces where most households live. The upper steps get cleaner heat and drier rooms. The lower steps are cheaper and wetter, and ash entering the system reaches them first.

Jori Kes and Pell Am, the two workers killed in the Grey Scald, both came from lower-step households.

### Grey Beds

After drinking and washing needs are met, spent water warms beds of reed, greyroot, medicinal moss, and tough kitchen crops. The settlement also uses ash skimmed from the settling basin as ceramic temper, abrasive, and pigment, and exports a small amount.

A bad intake cycle therefore reaches far beyond the cistern. It damages the settlement's water supply, heating, food production, craft work, and trade together.

### Upper Shelf

The dry shelf above the basin holds guest sheds, pack animals, the traders' road marker, and salvage considered too suspect to bring among the hearths.

Outsiders stay here until a household, work crew, or the basin table accepts responsibility for their water use and exit route.

## Who decides

The **basin table** is an agreement among household water witnesses, Grey Bed workers, Cistern House maintainers, and whoever currently speaks for the intake appointment. It governs the water schedule, shared stores, work obligations, local admission to the site, and emergency action affecting the basin.

Its authority has limits. The table cannot appoint household successors, settle every debt, decide cult doctrine, or overrule a stop imposed because of unsafe physical conditions. It is not a council with authority over every part of village life.

The table accounts for water and work through marked vessels and work-watches rather than a single currency. According to their use and capacity, households owe cleaning, hauling, bed work, pipe watching, or stores. An injured household member's inability to work may qualify the household for support.

Recognition matters as much as contribution. A household without a recognized water witness can labor for years and still be among the first to lose water in a shortage. Large hearths bring more workers and stores to the table. Lower-step renters may have to speak through a patron whose own dry room is less affected by the decision.

## Daily work and unequal costs

Every work-watch starts with observations at the Cistern House: temperature, taste, ash, and sound. Lower-step households report wet walls; Grey Bed workers report flow and crop stress. Maintainers scrape screens and compare the deposits. The basin table uses these reports to adjust water for washing, beds, craft, and trade, preserving drinking water and minimum heat first.

Children learn safe paths around hot channels before they learn the inherited service marks. Ash workers cover their mouths and oil their cracked skin. Bedding dries on shared rails. Traders buy fired greyware and complain that every delay becomes a water argument. Funeral cloths and repair cloths hang from different beams: the mist erases color before it erases texture.

The intake supplies the heat that cooks soup, loosens joints, grows crops, makes saleable pots possible, and keeps the lower rooms habitable. When it falters, people first spend more time carrying water and have less time to choose their own work.

Households have different means of enduring a shortage. Upper-step residents keep private vessels and can withstand a short ration. Lower-step renters lose warmth and work at the same time. Grey Bed laborers receive water through the crop compact, which leaves them vulnerable to being declared nonessential when the table panics. Ash workers suffer skin and lung damage that seldom appears in technical records.

Sera Venn's custodial household receives guaranteed heat because a sick or exhausted operator puts everyone at risk. The guarantee is necessary, and people resent it. Teren Vey's household lost its appointment share when he was removed; his dependants bore that loss before he had an appeal. The Ash Names' support pot has kept two lower-step families from leaving, but it cannot replace their lost workers.

## The Grey Scald's unfinished consequences

During the Grey Scald, custodian Teren Vey opened the intake's auxiliary relief route. His inherited warning and the interval understood by the workers did not match. Jori Kes and Pell Am died, and three other workers were injured. Teren was suspended and later removed without a competent technical review.

[[Spoilers/Events/The Grey Scald|The Grey Scald]] owns the adopted account of the event. Its consequences remain part of Low Sere's daily life. Two lower-step households lost workers. Three injured people and their dependants still need support. The Ash Names gained a mortuary claim below the settlement. The basin table still has an unresolved disciplinary failure to address, while every hearth depends on an office that Sera's household holds only provisionally.

## People at the threshold

### Sera Venn — provisional intake custodian

Sera apprenticed under Teren and kept the intake running after his removal. Her appointment allows ordinary inspection, isolation, and reduced service at the Cistern House boundary. It does not permit her to open the inner Intake Crown without the missing Ashen Measure or a competent comparison of the relevant practice.

Her office has remained “provisional” for years, and she is exhausted. She wants the Ashen Measure calibration comb and the last handoff folio recovered, followed by a review of her training. That review should give her either a full appointment with clear limits or an honest replacement. She will stop a descent that treats the calibration object as salvage.

### Maro Seln — cistern reeve

Maro organizes water marks and represents the basin table at the entrance. He holds the west-basin drain wheel and controls the iron grate, but he cannot release the pressure seal. He sponsored the current delving compact because only three intake shutters remain before failure.

Maro supported Teren's emergency suspension after the Grey Scald. He now knows that removing Teren without completing the necessary review and handoff helped create the present danger. Keeping Low Sere functioning matters more to him than Teren's reputation. He fears a review that closes the threshold while everyone argues.

### Teren Vey — removed custodian

Teren no longer controls the entrance. He admits putting the Ashen Measure and last handoff folio inside the intake so an unqualified successor could not use them. He calls this bounded refusal. Low Sere calls it withholding a public survival tool.

He knows a bypass through the Relief Throat and will offer it if his appeal is formally reopened. His disputed intervention during the Grey Scald and his later withholding both remain matters to be examined; recovering the objects will not itself settle them.

### Tavi Kes — speaker for the Ash Names

Tavi is Jori Kes's sibling. They speak for the **Ash Names**, the kin and mortuary fellowship that tends Jori, Pell, and the injured Grey Scald workers. The fellowship keeps name cloths, death testimony, a support pot for affected households, and a seal on the Memorial Sump.

Tavi's permission for a descent has four conditions: the compact must include a kin witness, every disturbance must be recorded, the two bone-name tags must be recovered if reachable, and no concentrated miracle work may be used in the sump.

Tavi believes the knocks below are the dead asking not to be made part of another repair. The vault leaves that belief unresolved.

### Ressa Orr — Eighth Baffle witness

Ressa is an itinerant Techpriest whose lineage recognizes part of the intake's filter and pressure practice. She can compare Sera's training and Teren's disputed intervention against that practice. She has no appointment at Low Sere.

She will accompany the party only if the basin table supplies assistants, copies of the formulae, and authority for her to stop work at the Intake Crown. Her presence makes a competent review possible, but it also gives her lineage leverage. Low Sere fears trading one custodian whose practice it cannot adequately inspect for a distant order that can collect the record and leave.

## The commissioned descent

Three shutter indicators remain pale. Each pressure pulse closes another. When none remain, untreated hot slurry will enter the relief route beneath the lower cistern.

Low Sere can abandon the lower basin and save lives. Doing so would cost it warm water, the Grey Beds, and most of the conditions that let people live here without winter cold governing daily life.

The basin table has commissioned the crew to:

- Recover the Ashen Measure and last handoff folio.
- Recover any reachable bone-name tags.
- Inspect the Intake Crown.
- Return enough evidence for Sera's appointment and Teren's appeal to receive separate hearings.

The delving compact reserves fixed parts, records, remains, and the calibration object. Recovering them does not make them salvage the crew may sell.

The Cistern House is the only entrance for which admission is granted. Old maintenance cracks and hot vents do not become safe routes because a delver can squeeze through them. Smuggling out a component through one avoids the admission process while leaving Low Sere to live with the altered machinery.

## What the outcome changes

Repair and evacuation produce different futures, and technical success does not settle who should hold custody.

- **Full restoration** preserves Low Sere and forces the basin table to conduct the review it has postponed.
- **A bypass** buys time to evacuate but ends the settlement's present economy of warm water, heated crops, and related work.
- **Selling the Ashen Measure** can enrich the crew while condemning the intake to recurrent failure.
- **Giving custody to the Eighth Baffle lineage** may stabilize operation while shifting leverage away from Low Sere.
- **Honoring an Ash Names refusal** can protect the dead while requiring abandonment of the intake.
- **Letting the pressure clock reach failure** ruptures the lower cistern, displaces households, and makes the surface settlement part of the dungeon.

Play extends beyond the descent. Before entry, the crew can negotiate the compact, take part in ration decisions, help lower-step households, address the kin-witness requirement, or argue for terms under which Teren's appeal can proceed. Questions about who is qualified to hold custody and what may be taken as salvage remain active throughout. Even a technically successful return leaves restoration work and disputes above the threshold.
