# Low Sere

Low Sere is a coldward settlement built around the warm discharge of [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]], a buried terraforming work whose surviving filters turn a mineral-laden subsurface flow into usable heat and water. Forty-one occupied household hearths cling to a dry basin below the intake slope. The settlement exists because the relic makes this one hollow less hostile than the plateau around it.

Low Sere owns its present life, work, use claims, and local admission to the site. It does not own the intake's safe operation merely because it depends upon the output.

## Material body

The settlement has four working surfaces rather than decorative districts.

### Cistern House

The Cistern House sits over two settling basins. Warm grey water enters the east basin, drops mineral ash, and passes to the covered lower cistern. The west basin can be drained for inspection. Behind its inner wall, a stair reaches the intake's pressure seal.

The Cistern House contains water marks, ration boards, sampling vessels, drain wheels, tools, and duplicate service notices. It is also the threshold of the dungeon. Maro Seln controls the settlement's drain wheel and iron access grate. Sera Venn controls the eight-tooth spindle that releases the pressure seal. Normal entry requires both.

### Warm Steps

Most households occupy stone terraces warmed by channels carrying outflow from the Cistern House. Upper steps receive cleaner heat and drier rooms. Lower steps are cheaper, wetter, and first to suffer when ash enters the system. Jori Kes and Pell Am, the two workers killed in the Grey Scald, came from lower-step households.

### Grey Beds

Reed, greyroot, medicinal moss, and tough kitchen crops grow in beds warmed by spent water. The beds consume flow after drinking and washing claims are met. Ash skimmed from the settling basin becomes ceramic temper, abrasive, pigment, and a small export. A bad intake cycle therefore harms water, warmth, food, craft, and trade together.

### Upper Shelf

The dry shelf above the basin holds guest sheds, pack animals, salvage too suspect for the hearths, and the road marker used by traders. Outsiders stay here until a household, work crew, or the basin table accepts responsibility for their use of water and their exit route.

## The basin table

Low Sere has no universal village council. The **basin table** is the compact among household water witnesses, Grey Bed workers, Cistern House maintainers, and whoever currently speaks for the intake appointment. It owns water schedule, shared stores, work obligations, local threshold admission, and emergency action affecting the basin.

It does not appoint household successors, settle every debt, decide cult doctrine, or overrule a material stop. Participation is unequal. Large hearths bring more stores and workers. Lower-step renters may be represented through a patron whose dry room depends less on the decision.

The table records water by marked vessels and work-watches rather than a single currency. Households owe cleaning, hauling, bed work, pipe watching, or stores according to use and capacity. A household that cannot work after injury may draw support. A household without a recognized witness can perform labor for years while remaining first to lose water in a shortage.

## Current actors

### Sera Venn — provisional intake custodian

Sera apprenticed under Teren Vey and kept the intake alive after his removal. Her appointment grants ordinary inspection, isolation, and degraded service at the Cistern House boundary. It does not authorize opening the inner Intake Crown without the missing Ashen Measure or a competent comparison.

Sera is exhausted by an office that has remained “provisional” for years. She wants the calibration comb and folio recovered, her formation reviewed, and either a full bounded appointment or an honest replacement. She will stop a descent that treats the object as salvage.

### Maro Seln — cistern reeve

Maro holds the west-basin drain wheel, organizes water marks, and speaks for the basin table at the threshold. He does not control the pressure seal. He sponsored the current delving compact because Low Sere has three intake shutters left before failure.

Maro supported Teren's emergency suspension after the Grey Scald. He now knows the incomplete removal helped create the present danger. He will prioritize settlement continuity over Teren's reputation and fears any review that could leave the threshold closed during argument.

### Teren Vey — removed custodian

Teren opened the auxiliary relief route during the Grey Scald. His inherited warning and the workers' understood interval did not match. Two people died and three were injured. He was suspended, then removed without a competent technical review.

Teren admits placing the Ashen Measure and last handoff folio inside the intake where an unqualified successor could not use them. He calls it bounded refusal. Low Sere calls it withholding a public survival tool. He knows a bypass through the Relief Throat and will offer it if his appeal is formally reopened. He no longer controls the entrance.

### Tavi Kes — speaker for the Ash Names

Tavi is Jori Kes's sibling and speaks for the **Ash Names**, the kin and mortuary fellowship tending Jori, Pell, and the injured Grey Scald workers. The fellowship maintains name cloths, death testimony, a support pot for affected households, and a seal on the Memorial Sump.

Tavi permits a descent only if the compact carries a kin witness, records every disturbance, recovers the two bone-name tags if reachable, and does not use concentrated miracle work in the sump. Tavi believes the knocks below are the dead asking not to be made part of another repair. The vault leaves that claim unresolved.

### Ressa Orr — Eighth Baffle witness

Ressa is an itinerant Techpriest formed in a lineage that recognizes part of the intake's filter and pressure practice. She can compare Sera's formation and Teren's disputed intervention but holds no appointment at Low Sere. She will accompany the party only if the basin table provides assistants, formula copies, and authority to stop at the Intake Crown.

Ressa's presence makes review possible and gives her lineage leverage. Low Sere fears replacing one opaque custodian with a distant order that can leave after collecting the record.

## Ordinary life under relic dependence

Each work-watch begins with temperature, taste, ash, and sound observations at the Cistern House. Lower-step households report wet walls. Grey Bed workers report flow and crop stress. Maintainers scrape screens and compare deposits. The basin table adjusts washing, beds, craft, and trade after preserving drinking and minimum heat.

Children learn safe paths around hot channels before they learn the inherited service marks. Ash workers cover their mouths and oil cracked skin. Households dry bedding on shared rails. Traders buy fired greyware and complain that every delay becomes a water argument. Funeral cloths and repair cloths hang from different beams because mist erases color before it erases texture.

The intake is not mysterious scenery to these people. It is the reason soup cooks, joints loosen, crops grow, pots sell, and lower rooms remain habitable. Its failure is experienced first as more time carrying water and less time owning one's labor.

## Authored elaboration: the forty-second bowl

Low Sere calls the custodian's shared supper the **forty-second bowl**. The name
does not add another household to the basin. It names the covered meal that one
of the forty-one hearths carries to the Cistern House each evening work-watch,
along with that hearth's report of channel sound, wall damp, water taste, and
who has been pulled from ordinary labor to keep the service going.

The turn is marked beside the water schedule. Hearths may exchange it, but the
replacement and any food or labor debt are recorded before the next ration.
Two signals hang beside those marks: a broad bronze water-cut bell audible
down the terraces and a smaller fired-clay turn bell whose higher or lower
strike calls a replacement from the corresponding Steps. Neither signal says
what the intake is doing; it says what Low Sere's people must do next.
The carrier owes delivery, not obedience. They may remain for one short witness
interval if pressure interrupts the meal: long enough to preserve what Sera
observed, who was warned, and which work she stopped. A longer watch needs a
named substitute. The witness stands at a scored place on the dry basin lip,
outside the pressure grate and clear of the drain wheel. Otherwise a supper
custom becomes an unpriced way to recruit assistants from households already
paying in wet rooms and missed work.

The bowl supports the acting custodian without recognizing a permanent title.
Sera may eat from it, share it with an assistant, or send it unopened to a
household whose labor the Cistern House displaced. Maro records a full, short,
exchanged, refused, or returned turn. A short bowl is not dishonor when stores
are short; an unrecorded shortfall is. If the rotation cannot provide food or a
witness without consuming household minimums, that is a support failure at the
site. Sera may narrow service rather than let the basin table describe hunger
and solitary work as successful custody.

Bel Orra's lower-step hearth exposes the bargain. Its walls are wet, Bel's
eldest carries drinking water instead of working the Grey Beds, and the
household has already supplied food for the planned descent. Bel still wants
Sera fed and the intake watched. What she rejects is the claim that loyalty
makes the same meal, witness time, and lost bed work free whenever the office
needs them.

The rotation binds Low Sere because every recognized hearth eventually feeds
the person keeping heat beneath all of them. It also displays class. A large
dry hearth can exchange a turn or spare a carrier; a lower-step household may
choose between the common bowl and its own supper. When that choice is hidden,
belonging becomes a levy no one admits imposing. When it is recorded, the
bowl's return can force the basin table to find food, relief, or a safer level
of service before care itself breaks.

## Class and burden

Upper-step households keep private vessels and can endure a short ration. Lower-step renters lose warmth and work together. Grey Bed laborers receive water through the crop compact and can be reclassified as nonessential when the basin table panics. Ash workers bear skin and lung damage that rarely appears in technical records.

Sera's custodial household receives guaranteed heat because an exhausted or sick operator endangers everyone. The support is necessary and resented. Teren's household lost its appointment share after removal; his dependants paid before his appeal existed. The Ash Names support pot prevented two lower-step families from leaving but cannot replace lost workers.

## Historical consequence: the Grey Scald

[[Spoilers/Events/The Grey Scald|The Grey Scald]] alone owns the adopted event facts. Low Sere retains the settlement consequences: two lower-step households lost workers, three injured people and their dependants need continuing support, the Ash Names gained a mortuary claim below, the basin table inherited an unresolved disciplinary failure, and Sera's household carries a provisional office on which every hearth depends.

## Present crisis

Three of the intake's eight shutter indicators remain pale. At each pressure pulse another closes. When none remain, untreated hot slurry will enter the relief route beneath the lower cistern. Low Sere can abandon the lower basin and preserve lives, but will lose warm water, Grey Beds, and most winterless habitability.

The basin table has commissioned a direct descent to recover the Ashen Measure, last handoff folio, and any reachable bone-name tags; inspect the Intake Crown; and return enough evidence for Sera's appointment and Teren's appeal to be heard separately. The compact reserves fixed parts, records, remains, and the calibration object from salvage.

## Outcome pressure

- A full restoration preserves Low Sere and forces a review the basin table can no longer postpone.
- A bypass buys evacuation time but ends the settlement's present material economy.
- Removing the Ashen Measure for sale can enrich the crew and condemn the intake to recurrent failure.
- Giving custody to the Eighth Baffle lineage may stabilize operation while moving local leverage elsewhere.
- Honoring the Ash Names' refusal can protect the dead and require abandonment of the intake.
- A failed clock ruptures the lower cistern, displaces households, and turns the surface settlement into part of the dungeon.

## Dungeon seams beyond the entrance

Low Sere offers play before and after descent: compact negotiation, water ration, support for lower-step households, argument over Teren's appeal, kin witness, custody comparison, salvage pressure, and restoration after a technically successful return.

The Cistern House is the only admitted entrance. Old maintenance cracks and hot vents are not safe secret doors merely because a delver can fit through them. Smuggling a component out through one bypasses admission and leaves Low Sere bearing the altered system.
