# The Three Orders at Sunwall

The Three Orders at Sunwall was a confrontation in which a safety closure stopped two rival military plans without defeating either army. It occurred during the contest that divided the [[Spoilers/Polities/Prophecy - The Divided City|City of Prophets]], after Ju'onai tribute rule had been established. A Terjamna force was trying to turn military access into permanent control, while the Sunwall continued feeding wards claimed by both powers.

This note owns the episode's adopted factual sequence. Its exact date, cargo gate, participating houses, commanders, wards, and relation to any formal armistice remain unresolved.

## Two plans for the same gate

Ju'onai officers prepared to defend a cargo gate and its adjoining lift routes. Opened readers mapped the structures under load, intervals of movement, garrison supplies, and available assault approaches. The command repositioned grain and defenders until its officers believed they could answer every useful Terjamna approach.

Each side also received a Prophetic house report, based on a different briefing. The Ju'onai-sponsored team compared possible futures after the defensive repositioning. The house under Terjamna protection considered a seizure led by a champion whose Sorcery had not yet selected its point of attack. Both reports described heavily crowded futures, with other actors able to change what followed, and both would remain useful only briefly. Neither sponsor gave the civic Sunwall office its complete intended response.

The resulting orders depended on one another. Ju'onai forces were to hold the gate and then make a counter-movement through its cargo galleries. The Terjamna champion was to exploit the first opening that movement created. Carrying out the Ju'onai plan would therefore supply an opportunity the Terjamna plan needed; each side's intended success helped make the other's possible.

## The closure

Sunwall stewards saw a different problem. Loads had changed, grain had been concentrated, and armed forces were moving through galleries whose pressure refuge could not accommodate everyone assigned to them. Using their existing authority over safe operation, the stewards closed the cargo configuration.

Neither surviving sponsor briefing had proposed this closure. It removed both the Ju'onai counter-movement's route and the opening the Terjamna champion expected to attack.

The armies remained in place. Grain was stranded, work crews were split, and lower wards came under ration pressure. Civic order officers could serve the closure order, but they could not command either garrison to leave. House messengers carried revised questions across lines that were already shifting as soldiers and residents moved.

All three orders still had force. Ju'onai troops held their claimed defense. The Terjamna champion could punish a reopening. The Stewardship kept the unsafe configuration shut. None of these authorities could settle the whole confrontation.

## Moving grain again

The immediate settlement allowed movement in smaller loads along more than one route. House reports subsequently entering civic use traveled with their sponsor's intended response. Sunwall operating changes were recorded separately as actions that could alter events, rather than treated as fixed conditions within a military forecast.

Neither empire surrendered its claim. The houses remained independent of the civic offices, and the Stewardship acquired no authority over war or tribute. The episode became one reason the city keeps forecasting, judgment, material operation, and armed force under several owners.

Later accounts emphasize different evidence:

- Ju'onai teaching treats the closure as an operating boundary the original plan failed to include.
- Terjamna accounts credit the champion with forcing a larger enemy to abandon its prepared movement.
- House comparers point to how quickly the forecasts ceased to be useful.
- Steward copies begin with refuge capacity and record how long the grain remained stopped.

These traditions preserve different parts of the same event.

## What the episode establishes

A strategic forecast submitted for civic action must identify what its sponsor intends to do in response, and who controls the material systems whose operation could change the situation. A Sunwall operating order remains a separate kind of decision: it governs operation and cannot settle tribute or title to a sector.

The military plans show why even a prepared victory can give another oracle the opportunity it needs. The closure shows a different limit. Stopping both predicted actions does not prove that the stewards foresaw more than the prophets. They acted on a material system the sponsors had left out of their briefings.

Keeping these decisions in separate hands can prevent one claimant from taking control of the whole system. Residents still bear the costs: delay, shortage, and overlapping coercion from authorities acting against one another.

## Adventure consequences

The two house reports, three orders, load maps, messenger copies, and later settlement may survive under different custodians. Recovering a single copy can change a present dispute over whether an empire, a house, or the Stewardship deserves credit for the whole outcome.

Reopening the old route can restore capacity. It can also recreate the military crossing that the closure removed.
