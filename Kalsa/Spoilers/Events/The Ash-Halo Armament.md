# The Ash-Halo Armament

The Ash-Halo Armament was a Jamnai rite that assembled divine armour around a champion defending a Crown heat-trunk gate. During the fitting, other presences drew power from the working. The divine hierarchy struck back, breaking several of their manifested bodies. The armour closed, and the champion held the gate until relief arrived. Afterward, familiar signs of the dead stopped appearing at three shrines, while a violent presence remained beneath the forge.

Later mortuary copies gave the event its Ash-Halo name. High-branch cult accounts call it the armament at the furnace court. The event is adopted history, and this note is its sole canonical owner. The God, branch, champion, Crown district, siege, and exact date remain unresolved.

## The court before the light

A Jamnai branch prepared the armament while defending one of the Crown's heat-trunk gates. A bound Prophet supplied the interval during which the gate could be held. Forge custodians then spent three furnace shifts preparing the court: they staged iron scales, copper wire, ceramic splints, resin, charcoal, quench water, and lifting weights, and opened a path from a working heat trunk. Porters marked every rack and cleared the floor. The champion fasted under guard while healers monitored heat, breath, and skin.

To supply the rite, the potential steward withdrew grants from two outer branch shrines and concentrated that power in the armament. The written allocation named the champion, the gate, the prepared court, the source stores, the grant's expiry, and the remaining reserve.

The allocation said nothing about the mortuary gallery beneath the court. A Soiru'i sect had sent an answer roll—a record of dead presences previously recognized around that gallery. A branch registrar received it, but it remained unopened throughout the preparations.

## The armour closes

When the divine route opened, furnace draft swept ash and iron filings into a ring around the champion. Counterweights fell. Racks tipped in their marked order, resin softened, wire tightened, and iron scales closed over the champion's body faster than the forge crew could follow.

The working drew on the court's materials, stored heat, gravity, machinery, and the God hierarchy's potential. These prepared resources made the assembly possible, but the result was still divine raiment: no unaided crew could have selected and completed that chain of actions as the attack on the gate began.

Faces and hands flashed separately within the ring. Whenever one lasted long enough to turn toward the court, the champion's grant weakened. The God could sense the distortion through its route. It received neither the answer roll nor the forge workers' identifications of what they saw.

The hierarchy responded through the champion and the prepared court. Each counterstroke pulled diverted power back through the hot raiment. Several manifestations lost the bodies they had acquired from potential—the bodies that allowed them to act in the lower world. Their forms broke apart in the ash halo. One diversion escaped through the quench drain, leaving a presence able to act from the slag cistern beneath the gallery.

The armour finished closing. The champion held the heat-trunk gate until the branch relief arrived.

## What stopped answering

After the siege, the death witness opened the answer roll and compared it with household copies. Familiar signs recorded before the armament no longer returned at three shrines. One presence had warned furnace families of pressure faults. Another had appeared at a tributary burial meal. The third was a defeated champion whom a Soiru'i sect claimed as a protector.

Witnesses could establish that those signs had appeared before the rite, that flashes had been seen in the court, and that the shrines later fell silent. They could not determine what memory or personhood survived elsewhere in the corresponding Projections. The silence did not settle that question.

The presence in the slag cistern answered immediately and violently. Cleanup crews could no longer use the quench drain. Production slowed while workers built a new channel, removed contaminated slag under guard, and supported two people burned during the armament. The two outer shrines also remained without their ordinary protections until the divine hierarchy recovered enough to restore the withdrawn grants.

## The records diverge

The surviving records follow different consequences. The high-branch victory copy ends with the armoured champion holding the gate. The furnace return goes on to record damaged lifting gear, burns, the blocked drain, and delayed production. Mortuary copies preserve the unopened answer roll alongside the accounts of later shrine visits.

One surviving Soiru'i copy leaves four interpretations of the defeated champion's flash in the margin: hunger, warning, petition, and ascent. It chooses none of them.

## Descendant practice

The first lasting change was to the allocation dockets. They began recording which stores supplied a working, which shrine protections had been withdrawn to fund it, how far leakage might reach, who could interrupt it, and what reserve remained for recovery.

Mortuary offices later required answer rolls to be opened before a rite wherever recognized dead lay near its route. Soiru'i sects began carrying their own copies after a branch registrar left the original sealed.

These practices never became a shared Jamnai rule. Branches that refuse the records now stop their carriers at the court threshold. That dispute over admission continues at the present site.

## Present site

The furnace court and its remains can survive in the current Crown: the under-gallery, slag cistern, source racks, counterweight ways, branch copies, and mortuary route. [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ash-Halo Court|The Ash-Halo Court]] owns their return to service.

## Unresolved

- The God, branch, champion, date, district, siege opponent, and exact institutional descendants.
- Which familiar sign, if any, belonged to each flash in the halo.
- What survived after the acquired potential bodies were destroyed.
- Whether the slag-cistern presence was one of the recorded dead, a Beast remnant, or another feeder.
- Whether any participant was attempting ascent and what the God understood of the collision.
