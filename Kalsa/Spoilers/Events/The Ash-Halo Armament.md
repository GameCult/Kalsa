# The Ash-Halo Armament

The Ash-Halo Armament is an adopted Jamnai history of divine raiment, feeding,
interception, and mortuary loss. This note alone owns the event. The God,
branch, champion, Crown district, siege, and exact date remain unresolved. The
name comes from later mortuary copies; high-branch cult accounts call it the
armament at the furnace court.

## The court before the light

A Jamnai branch defending one of the Crown's heat-trunk gates prepared a divine
armament for its champion. A bound Prophet supplied the interval in which the
gate could be held. Forge custodians spent three furnace shifts staging iron
scales, copper wire, ceramic splints, resin, charcoal, quench water, lifting
weights, and an open path from a working heat trunk into the court. Porters
marked every rack and cleared the floor. The champion fasted under guard while
healers watched heat, breath, and skin.

The potential steward concentrated grants withdrawn from two outer branch
shrines. The written allocation named the champion, gate, prepared court,
source stores, expiry, and remaining reserve. It did not name the mortuary
gallery beneath the floor. A Soiru'i sect had sent an answer roll listing dead
presences previously recognized around that gallery. The roll reached a branch
registrar and remained unopened during preparation.

## Iron crossed the floor

When the route opened, furnace draft tore ash and filings into a ring around
the champion. Counterweights fell, racks tipped in their marked order, resin
softened, wire drew tight, and iron scales closed over the body faster than the
forge crew could follow. The working used the court's matter, stored heat,
gravity, machinery, and the God hierarchy's potential. The result was
nevertheless divine raiment: no unaided crew could have selected and completed
that chain while the gate attack began.

Faces and hands appeared in separate flashes within the ring. The champion's
grant weakened each time one held long enough to turn toward the court. The God
could perceive the deformation through its route but did not receive the
answer roll or the forge workers' identifications.

The hierarchy answered through the champion and the prepared court. Each
counterstroke pulled the diverted flow back through the hot raiment. Several
manifestations lost the acquired potential bodies supporting their lower-world
agency. Their forms broke apart in the halo. One diversion escaped through the
quench drain and left an active foothold in the slag cistern beneath the
gallery.

The armour closed. The champion held the heat-trunk gate until the branch
relief arrived.

## What stopped answering

After the siege, the death witness opened the answer roll beside household
copies. Familiar signs recorded before the armament no longer returned at
three shrines. One had warned furnace families of pressure faults. Another had
appeared at a tributary burial meal. The third was a defeated champion whom a
Soiru'i sect claimed as protector. The witnesses could establish prior signs,
the flashes seen in court, and later silence. They could not establish what
memory or personhood survived elsewhere in the corresponding Projections.

The slag-cistern presence answered immediately and violently. Cleanup crews
lost access to the quench drain. Forge production slowed while workers built a
new channel, removed contaminated slag under guard, and supported two people
burned during the armament. The outer shrines whose grants had been withdrawn
could not resume their ordinary protections until the hierarchy recovered.

## The records diverge

The high-branch victory copy ends when the armoured champion holds the gate.
The furnace return continues through the damaged lifting gear, burns, blocked
drain, and delayed production. Mortuary copies bind the unopened answer roll to
the later shrine visits. On one surviving Soiru'i copy, four possible readings
of the defeated champion's flash—hunger, warning, petition, ascent—share a
margin with no selected answer.

## Descendant practice

The first durable change appeared on allocation dockets: source stores,
withdrawn shrine work, leakage perimeter, interruption authority, and recovery
reserve. Mortuary offices later added a pre-rite opening rule where recognized
dead lay near the route. Soiru'i sects began carrying their own copies after a
branch registrar left the original sealed.

Branches refusing those records now stop their carriers at the court threshold.
The resulting admission dispute, rather than a shared Jamnai rule, survives
into the present site.

## Present site

The furnace court, under-gallery, slag cistern, source racks, counterweight
ways, branch copies, and mortuary route can survive in the current Crown. Their
return to service is owned by [[Spoilers/Dungeons/The Ash-Halo Court|The
Ash-Halo Court]].

## Unresolved

- The God, branch, champion, date, district, siege opponent, and exact
  institutional descendants.
- Which familiar sign, if any, belonged to each flash in the halo.
- What survived after the acquired potential bodies were destroyed.
- Whether the slag-cistern presence was one of the recorded dead, a Beast
  remnant, or another feeder.
- Whether any participant was attempting ascent and what the God understood of
  the collision.
