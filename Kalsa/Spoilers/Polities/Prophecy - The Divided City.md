# Prophecy — The Divided City

Prophecy, signature 011 and Sequence V, was developed from Analysis and Shamanism. Prometheus replaced direct divine competition with an economic selection regime: wealth and aesthetics rewarded people able to perceive long-range systems. The program sought broader fitness than the narrow virtual contest that produced Analysis, but still made reproduction a designed outcome of power.

Prophets consciously perceive multiple possible future states without needing to know every cause. A skilled prophet compares branches and works backward toward present decisions. Prophecy detects large-horizon deformation and occlusion in forecast space, including interference by other prophets, shamans, Gods, and inferred Luck. It does not directly identify a Luck user.

Prophets contribute exceptional potential to God hierarchies but do not normally found independent God Beasts.

## The sunset city

The Prophecy Crown landed near the center of the terminator. Sunlight reaches the tops of high structures while much of the city remains beneath twilight. Access to sun and a visible tan became signs of wealth and status.

The city rebuilt its Spokes into the **Sunwall**, an enormous sunward wall that shadows half the city. Vertical farms high on the wall can sustain its large population, extending above altitudes where humans breathe safely. Lifts, pressure refuges, farm galleries, and maintenance routes turn altitude into an inhabited hierarchy even where open air cannot support life.

The Sunwall is both granary and wound. Its sunward works catch the light that feeds the city while its bulk makes the lower shadow deeper. Upper terraces, illuminated courts, and scheduled access to sun became privileges; shade labor, crowding, and dependence on distributed harvests fell disproportionately on people without house or occupation patronage.

## The divided sovereignty

The [[Spoilers/Polities/Analysis - Ju'onai Hegemony|Ju'onai Hegemony]] first reduced the city to tributary status without fully assimilating it. The expanding Terjamna/Sorcery power later contested the same prize. The result is a practical condominium: two imperial claims overlap a surviving local civic authority, though the city's name, formal armistice, and exact sector lines remain unresolved.

No participant can cheaply abolish the others.

- Ju'onai rule claims precedence through first conquest, tribute, ideological hierarchy, and the administrative competence to classify prophetic service.
- Terjamna rule claims standing through successful force, protected submissions, and the capacity to break any arrangement that becomes exclusively Ju'onai.
- The city's tributary authority controls the daily systems neither occupier can replace without destroying the prize: local courts, collection, markets, neighborhood peace, Sunwall allocation, and coordination among [[Spoilers/Institutions/Prophetic Houses|Prophetic Houses]].

Each empire can compel tribute and strategic cooperation within its reach. Neither owns the whole city, every prophet, or the Sunwall. Full annexation would give one rival exclusive access to prophetic lineages and threaten the other's future before the conqueror could stabilize the farms. Local authorities survive because both empires need an intermediary capable of making grain, light, forecast labor, and ordinary law cross hostile boundaries.

The arrangement is not equality. Garrisons, collectors, divine patrons, and house clients exert different pressure by ward. The civic authority can bargain between empires but cannot pretend their force is absent. The empires can overrule local decisions in claimed strategic matters but depend upon local compliance to make an order real.

## Tributary government

The civic authority owns ordinary continuity rather than ultimate sovereignty. Its native name, constitutional composition, and selection rules remain unresolved, but its recurring functional offices are adopted:

- **Ward offices** keep residence, ration, market, use, and local-dues records; receive ordinary petitions; and coordinate neighborhood peace. A ward record does not supersede a house archive, imperial compact, or Sunwall operating decision outside its scope.
- **The Civic Allocation Table** schedules public grain, light courts, cargo priority, pressure refuge, and emergency reserves after receiving capacity reports from the Sunwall Stewardship. It allocates output; it does not operate the machinery.
- **The Sunwall Stewardship** coordinates the farm galleries, lifts, pressure refuges, maintenance routes, and their trained work crews. Stewards can stop an unsafe configuration and issue operational warnings. They do not own the harvest, the city, or the people who depend on either.
- **The Tribute and Service Office** assembles local dues, imperial tribute, and forecast-service quotas already authorized by civic and compact authority. It cannot invent a new house obligation or decide which forecast is true.
- **Ward and sector courts** hear ordinary civic cases. The **City Mixed Bench** hears forecast appeals and cross-claim disputes under the boundaries in [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast Evidence and Courts]].
- **Civic clerks and order officers** preserve public records, serve notice, carry orders, and enforce civic remedies. They do not command either imperial garrison or a house's internal discipline.

These are translated functional names rather than settled endonyms. The offices are separate because an allocator who could also conceal capacity, judge objections, and operate the lifts would own the city's dependence without having to admit it.

Imperial claims are paid in grain, access, intelligence, and scheduled prophetic labor. A demand large enough to cripple the city becomes self-defeating and gives the rival a reason to intervene. This makes every quota a negotiation disguised as obedience.

Young or strategically important house members may reside with an occupier as students, honored guests, service providers, or guarantees. The vocabulary changes with the speaker. Their practical function is hostage-provider politics: they make a house's forecast obligations credible while giving the host privileged access to future expertise.

## Light, food, and the Sunwall

The civic authority allocates the Sunwall's public outputs. Wall stewards operate farms, lifts, pressure refuges, and maintenance schedules; they own operation, not the city or the people dependent upon it. Houses and imperial sectors bargain for crop shares, cargo priority, terrace hours, and emergency reserves. Ordinary households hold use claims through ward, work, tenancy, or ration custom rather than owning a slice of sunlight itself.

Light allocation includes more than leisure. It determines which crops grow, who works at dangerous altitude, which children recover from illness in sun courts, whose ceremonies receive a bright hour, and which roofs remain beneath cultivated shadow. A tan is prestigious because access is organized, not because sunlight is rare in the abstract.

Forecasting cannot solve allocation cleanly. A forecast changes when officials announce it, when a house adjusts its bid, when a garrison threatens seizure, or when frightened residents hoard grain. The city's law therefore treats prophecy as evidence within a decision, never as the decision's owner. See [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast Evidence and Courts]].

## Houses and forecast labor

Prophetic noble houses preserved strong expression by restricting marriage with the wider control-descended population. The source associates highly expressed house lineages with Analyst-like “elfin” features, large heads, and large eyes. These are old visual descriptions requiring current viewpoint and range, not a complete biological caste. House language of purity is a political lineage claim, not proof of superior personhood.

Houses steward training traditions, lineage records, estates, and negotiated service obligations. They do not own future truth or the personhood of their members. In practice, inheritance, education, residence, debt, marriage approval, and access to imperial protection give them substantial coercive power. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Prophetic Houses|Prophetic Houses]] records both that machinery and the ways people evade it.

Forecast labor is the city's strategic export. Houses supply comparative forecasts for tribute, war, diplomacy, harvest, infrastructure, and litigation. Both empires prefer dependent providers to a free prophetic commons; both also fear that crushing the houses would scatter talent to the rival. Houses exploit that fear, trading service and hosted kin for room to govern themselves.

## Ordinary non-house life

Most city life occurs outside the noble compounds. Non-house residents farm the Sunwall, maintain lifts and pressure doors, keep ledgers, transport grain, work markets, serve garrisons, raise families, and live with decisions justified in the language of futures they may never be allowed to inspect. Some possess weak or unrecognized Prophecy. Others are Null, mixed-style, migrants, or descendants of tributary populations.

A household usually encounters prophecy as a schedule, quota, closure, premium, marriage rumor, delayed trial, or official warning—not as a sublime vision. Forecasts can preserve a harvest and can also make a landlord's preference sound inevitable. Civic evidentiary rules exist because the people most frequently acted upon are not the people speaking from the witness dais.

Cross-sector work and kinship make the imperial division porous. A resident may sleep under Ju'onai collection, work a Terjamna-protected warehouse, draw grain from a civic ward, and owe money to a house. The city persists through these ordinary crossings more than through proclamations of unity.

## Player and delving consequences

Adventurers encounter the city's divided authority at the job boundary. A house can commission recovery of an archive copy but cannot grant entry through an occupied ward. A ward court can authorize evidence recovery but cannot order a Sunwall crew to open an unsafe lift. The Sunwall Stewardship can admit a repair party to a named shaft without granting salvage title. An imperial officer can provide armed passage through its sector without deciding the civic case attached to what the party finds.

Useful work includes carrying a forecast record across a closed sector line, escorting a hosted provider who invokes return, inspecting a lift or pressure refuge under civic order, recovering ration or tenancy records from buried galleries, protecting an appeal witness, or tracing a diverted harvest without accepting either empire's account as the verdict. [[Spoilers/Dungeons/Dungeon Provenance|Dungeon provenance]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic and Ruin Law|ruin law]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|relic custody]], and the commissioning office retain separate claims.

Helping one organ changes the city unevenly. Restoring a lift expands food capacity and may strengthen the allocator who controls its schedule. Exposing a concealed forecast can release a defendant while destroying a house contract or an imperial plan. Opening a pressure route can save a lower ward and create a new military crossing. The player-facing choice is rarely whether prophecy is correct; it is which authority may act on incomplete knowledge, who bears the reversible precaution, and what remedy remains when the feared branch never arrives.

## Hostile and marginal readings

A lower-shadow resident may see the condominium as three authorities collecting from one kitchen. A Terjamna veteran may see civic procedure as Ju'onai delay dressed in local dignity. A Ju'onai assessor may see house secrecy as hereditary extortion. An unrecognized prophet may see the houses' purity doctrine as theft of a Gift they did not create.

These accusations are not errors to be smoothed away. They identify where the machine loads its costs.

## Historical stress: the Closed Lift Appeal

[[Spoilers/Events/The Closed Lift Appeal|The Closed Lift Appeal]] is the factual owner of the event. Its polity consequence was to make the city's divided body harder to collapse: the City Mixed Bench retained civic judgment, imperial parties retained only their scoped claims, and the Sunwall Stewardship retained operational responsibility. The precedent did not abolish occupation or make the civic authority sovereign.

## Unresolved

- City name, exact sector borders, and the text or even existence of a formal armistice.
- Native names, composition, selection, tenure, and succession of the tributary civic authority and its functional offices.
- Number, names, and internal constitutions of the Prophetic Houses.
- Precise shares of tribute, forecast labor, Sunwall harvest, and light access.
- Whether hosted house members possess a recognized right of refusal or return.
- Which present occupier or successor state owns each old Ju'onai and Terjamna claim.
