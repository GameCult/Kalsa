# Prophecy — The Divided City

The City of Prophets feeds a large population from farms built high on an enormous wall. Its prophetic houses supply forecasts to two rival empires, both of which claim authority over parts of the city. A surviving civic government keeps grain moving, hears ordinary disputes, and allocates access to light. None of these powers controls enough to govern alone.

For residents, that division reaches into food, work, housing, and travel. The empire collecting tribute from a household may differ from the one protecting its workplace. A civic office may assign its grain ration while a noble house holds its debts. Forecasts influence all these decisions, but the people affected may never be allowed to read them.

The city's settled name remains unresolved. “Prophecy” also names the Gift for which it is known: the ability to consciously perceive several possible futures.

## What prophets can perceive

A prophet can perceive possible future states without knowing every cause that could produce them. Skilled prophets compare those possibilities, then work backward to consider what present decisions might lead toward or away from them.

Their reach includes changes and obscured regions in distant forecasts. Other prophets, shamans, and Gods can interfere with what they perceive. Prophets can also infer the influence of Luck from distorted forecasts, but they cannot directly identify a Luck user.

In the author classification, Prophecy has signature 011 and belongs to Sequence V. Prometheus developed it from Analysis and Shamanism. Instead of direct divine competition, the development program used an economic selection regime: wealth and aesthetics rewarded people who could perceive systems over long periods. Prometheus sought a broader form of fitness than the narrow virtual contest that had produced Analysis. Success and power still determined reproduction by design.

Prophets can contribute exceptional potential to God hierarchies. They do not normally found independent God Beasts.

## A city built toward the sun

The Prophecy Crown landed near the center of the terminator, the boundary between day and night. Sunlight reaches the tops of high structures while much of the city lies in twilight. Height therefore offers something residents below cannot take for granted: time in the sun. Access to it, and a visible tan, became signs of wealth and status.

The city rebuilt its Spokes into the **Sunwall**, an enormous wall facing the sun. Vertical farms high on the wall can sustain the city's large population. Some extend above the altitude where humans can breathe safely. Lifts, pressure refuges, farm galleries, and maintenance routes make those heights usable even where people cannot survive in the open air.

The same structure that catches light for the farms shadows half the city and deepens the darkness below. Upper terraces, illuminated courts, and scheduled hours in sunlight became privileges. People without house or occupation patronage disproportionately bear the crowded housing, labor in the shade, and dependence on distributed harvests. The city's food supply and its unequal access to light are built into the same wall.

## Why two empires share the city

The [[Spoilers/Polities/Ju'onai Hegemony|Ju'onai Hegemony]] conquered first, reducing the city to tributary status without fully assimilating it. The expanding Terjamna/Sorcery power later contested Ju'onai control. The resulting arrangement is a practical **condominium**: two imperial claims overlap, while a local civic authority continues to govern daily life. The exact sector lines and the text—or even existence—of a formal armistice remain unresolved.

Each participant has something the others cannot cheaply replace:

- **Ju'onai** claims precedence through first conquest and tribute. It also relies on its ideological hierarchy and its administrative ability to classify prophetic service.
- **Terjamna** claims standing through successful force, submissions made under its protection, and its ability to break an arrangement that gives Ju'onai exclusive control.
- **The tributary civic authority** runs local courts, collection, markets, neighborhood peace, Sunwall allocation, and coordination among [[Spoilers/Institutions/Prophetic Houses|Prophetic Houses]]. Destroying those systems would damage the city both empires want.

Each empire can compel tribute and strategic cooperation where its power reaches. Neither controls the whole city, every prophet, or the Sunwall. Full annexation would give the victor exclusive access to prophetic lineages. That would threaten the rival's future before the conqueror could stabilize the farms. Both empires therefore need local authorities able to move grain, light access, forecast labor, and ordinary legal business across hostile boundaries.

This dependence does not make the parties equal. Garrisons, collectors, divine patrons, and house clients apply different pressures in different wards. Civic officials can bargain between the empires, but imperial force remains present. Either empire can overrule local decisions in matters it claims as strategic; it still needs local compliance to carry out the order.

## Why control of forecasts remains divided

An empire that controlled all the houses could brief most long-range observers, receive their reports first, and choose its responses before rivals began planning around them. If it also controlled the Sunwall, gates, and messengers, it would have practical means to make those forecasts come true. The struggle is therefore about more than receiving accurate predictions. Each claimant wants its own decisions to shape the futures everyone else must consider.

Neither has secured both kinds of control. Houses divide archives and allegiances across sectors. Civic clerks keep some reports beyond a sponsor's sole custody. Sunwall crews can halt an unsafe operation even when every patron's plan depends on it proceeding. Kin, grain, and messages continue to cross imperial lines. A prophet residing with a patron may know that patron's intentions and still know nothing of a ward closure already changing the possible futures.

House comparers call this situation, in translation, a **crowded horizon**. The term helps a report identify other actors who can respond during the period it covers. Houses can also abuse it: “crowding” may excuse a badly framed question or conceal which patron was warned first. [[Spoilers/Foundations/Oracle Ecology|Oracle Ecology]] owns the full account of these interactions; house doctrine supplies only its own understanding.

Several futures remain possible partly because several authorities retain the power to force a different outcome. That keeps conquest unfinished. It also means ordinary residents can face three precautionary orders, each issued against the others.

## The offices that keep daily life running

The civic government maintains ordinary life without possessing ultimate sovereignty. Its native name, constitutional composition, and selection rules remain unresolved. The following recurring offices are adopted, with translated functional names:

- **Ward offices** maintain records of residence, rations, markets, use claims, and local dues. They receive ordinary petitions and coordinate neighborhood peace. Their records have limited scope: they cannot override a house archive, imperial compact, or Sunwall operating decision on matters outside ward authority.
- **The Civic Allocation Table** receives capacity reports from the Sunwall Stewardship, then schedules public grain, light courts, cargo priority, pressure refuge, and emergency reserves. It decides how available output and access are distributed; it does not run the machinery.
- **The Sunwall Stewardship** coordinates farm galleries, lifts, pressure refuges, maintenance routes, and trained crews. Stewards issue operational warnings and can stop an unsafe configuration. That responsibility gives them no ownership of the harvest, the city, or its dependent population.
- **The Tribute and Service Office** assembles local dues, imperial tribute, and forecast-service quotas that civic and compact authorities have already authorized. It cannot create new house obligations or decide which forecast is true.
- **Ward and sector courts** hear ordinary civic cases. The **City Mixed Bench** hears forecast appeals and disputes crossing different authorities' claims, within the limits set out in [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast Evidence and Courts]].
- **Civic clerks and order officers** preserve public records, serve notice, carry orders, and enforce civic remedies. They command neither imperial garrisons nor the internal discipline of houses.

These responsibilities remain separate for a practical reason. An office that could assign grain and light, conceal the available capacity, judge objections, and operate the lifts would control the city's dependence with little room for challenge.

The empires take payment in grain, access, intelligence, and scheduled prophetic labor. A demand that cripples the city defeats its own purpose and gives the rival grounds to intervene. Even an apparently obedient quota therefore rests on negotiation.

Some young or strategically important house members live with an occupier. Depending on the speaker, they are students, honored guests, service providers, or guarantees. They function as both hostages and providers: their presence makes a house's forecast obligations more credible and gives the host privileged access to future expertise.

## Who receives food and light

The Civic Allocation Table distributes the Sunwall's public outputs using the capacity its operators report. Houses and imperial sectors bargain over crop shares, cargo priority, terrace hours, and emergency reserves. Ordinary households usually establish use claims through ward membership, work, tenancy, or ration custom. Those claims grant forms of access rather than ownership of a portion of sunlight.

Allocation reaches well beyond leisure. It affects which crops grow, who must work at dangerous altitude, which ill children can recover in sun courts, whose ceremonies receive a bright hour, and which roofs remain under the shadow of cultivation. A tan signals status because access to sunlight is organized and unequal.

Forecasts cannot settle these choices once and for all. Announcing a prediction can change what officials and residents do. A house may revise its bid, a garrison may threaten seizure, or frightened households may hoard grain. Those responses change the future being forecast. City law consequently admits prophecy as evidence for a decision while leaving responsibility for the decision with the relevant authority. See [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|Forecast Evidence and Courts]].

## Houses, inheritance, and forecast work

The prophetic noble houses preserve training traditions, lineage records, estates, and negotiated service obligations. They maintained strong expression of Prophecy by restricting marriage with the wider control-descended population.

The source associates highly expressed house lineages with Analyst-like “elfin” features, large heads, and large eyes. These old visual descriptions still require a current viewpoint and an account of their range. They do not define a complete biological caste. House claims of purity serve lineage politics; they do not establish superior personhood.

A house has no ownership of future truth or of its members' personhood. It nevertheless has considerable means to coerce them. Inheritance, education, residence, debt, marriage approval, and access to imperial protection all give house authorities ways to constrain a member's choices. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Prophetic Houses|Prophetic Houses]] describes those controls and how people evade them.

The work houses sell is the city's strategic export. They compare possible futures for tribute, war, diplomacy, harvests, infrastructure, and litigation. Both empires want providers who depend on them, rather than a free prophetic commons. Both also fear that crushing the houses would send their talent to the rival. Houses use that fear to bargain, exchanging service and hosted kin for room to govern themselves.

## Life outside the noble compounds

Most residents live outside the houses. They farm the Sunwall, maintain lifts and pressure doors, keep ledgers, move grain, work markets, serve garrisons, and raise families. Some have weak or unrecognized Prophecy. Others are Null, mixed-style, migrants, or descendants of tributary populations.

For these residents, prophecy usually arrives as something they must respond to: a schedule, quota, closure, premium, marriage rumor, delayed trial, or official warning. They may be denied access to the forecast used to justify it. A prediction can help preserve a harvest; it can also give a landlord's preference the appearance of inevitability. Civic rules for forecast evidence matter because the people most often subjected to these decisions are seldom the witnesses explaining them in court.

Work and kinship repeatedly cross the imperial division. One resident may sleep in a ward where Ju'onai collects, work in a Terjamna-protected warehouse, receive grain through a civic ward, and owe money to a house. These routine crossings do more to sustain the city than declarations of unity.

## Adventuring across divided authority

A commission grants only the permissions its issuer controls. A house can hire adventurers to recover an archive copy, but it cannot authorize passage into an occupied ward. A ward court can authorize evidence recovery, but it cannot require a Sunwall crew to open an unsafe lift. The Sunwall Stewardship can admit a repair party to a named shaft without granting salvage rights. An imperial officer can provide armed passage through its sector without deciding the civic case attached to the party's discoveries.

Jobs can involve carrying a forecast record across a closed sector line, escorting a hosted provider who invokes return, or inspecting a lift or pressure refuge under civic order. Parties may recover ration or tenancy records from buried galleries, protect an appeal witness, or trace a diverted harvest without treating either empire's account as the verdict. [[Spoilers/Dungeons/Dungeon Provenance|Dungeon provenance]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic and Ruin Law|ruin law]], [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|relic custody]], and the commissioning office retain separate claims.

A successful job can help residents while changing the balance of power. Restoring a lift increases food capacity and may strengthen the official who controls its schedule. Revealing a concealed forecast can free a defendant while ruining a house contract or imperial plan. Opening a pressure route can save a lower ward and create a military crossing.

The decision for players is rarely just whether to trust a prediction. It is who may act on incomplete knowledge, who must live under a precaution that can later be reversed, and what remedy remains if the feared future never occurs.

## Accounts from people under pressure

A lower-shadow resident may describe the condominium as three authorities collecting from one kitchen. A Terjamna veteran may regard civic procedure as Ju'onai delay dressed in local dignity. A Ju'onai assessor may regard house secrecy as hereditary extortion. An unrecognized prophet may see purity doctrine as the houses claiming a Gift they did not create.

Each accusation identifies a different burden imposed by the arrangement. Their disagreement should remain visible rather than being corrected into a single official account.

## What the Closed Lift Appeal preserved

[[Spoilers/Events/The Closed Lift Appeal|The Closed Lift Appeal]] owns the event's factual account. Its consequence for the city's government was to reinforce the division of responsibility: the City Mixed Bench retained civic judgment, imperial parties retained only the claims within their respective scope, and the Sunwall Stewardship retained operational responsibility.

The precedent made it harder for one participant to absorb the others' powers. Occupation continued, and the civic government did not become sovereign.

## Why workers cite the Three Orders at Sunwall

Sunwall stewards and house comparers cite [[Spoilers/Events/The Three Orders at Sunwall|the Three Orders at Sunwall]] when a strategic forecast is brought to civic machinery. The sponsor's intended response and the steward's operating change travel as separate leaves, so the plan and the material intervention remain separately recorded.

The event neither founded this practice nor settled the city's constitution. Present workers use it as a local reason to refuse a plan when its pressure refuge, load, or mechanism cannot support what the plan requires.

## Unresolved

- City name, exact sector borders, and the text or even existence of a formal armistice.
- Native names, composition, selection, tenure, and succession of the tributary civic authority and its functional offices.
- Number, names, and internal constitutions of the Prophetic Houses.
- Precise shares of tribute, forecast labor, Sunwall harvest, and light access.
- Whether hosted house members possess a recognized right of refusal or return.
- Which present occupier or successor state owns each old Ju'onai and Terjamna claim.
