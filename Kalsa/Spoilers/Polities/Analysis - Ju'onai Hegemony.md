# Analysis — Ju'onai Hegemony

Analysis, signature 010 and Sequence IV, came from a virtual evolutionary game. Prometheus gave its Ring biological interfaces, dangerous programmed environments, and contests of artificial intelligence. Reproductive rights depended on success. The narrow pressure produced conscious causal perception at substantial physical cost.

An Analyst experiences sudden understanding of a system's structure and exploitable relations. Learned understanding can remain useful after the magical perception passes. Analysts can interact unusually well with computers, Promethean nanites, and mechanical systems. They detect causal intervention in active systems but do not possess a universal view of magic, souls, truth, motive, or technical practice. Analysis reveals a bounded model. It does not choose the boundary honestly by itself.

## Place and empire

The Analysis Crown fell sunward, just beyond the shadow of the Santabi'u coastal mountain chain. The Ju'onai Hegemony grew into one of the setting's two principal expansionist powers. Its people are called junsenai in the seed. The Crown City's name and the exact location of Santabi'u remain unresolved.

Analysis can independently found God Beasts. Ju'onai gods embody both ideas and feelings. Their hierarchies expand through ideological allegiance: a person's public status depends partly on the god or concept they represent, and cultural conversion can transfer real divine power. Even farmers and other subordinated groups stand within named divine domains.

The source describes junsenai as dogmatic, insular, physically atrophied, “elfin,” politically tactical, and unwilling to enter battle without first securing victory. Physical effects of the old selection regime remain a possible inherited pressure moderated by post-Fall intermarriage. “Elfin” is an old narrator's visual shorthand, not a settled people-category. Dogmatism, insularity, and occupational subordination describe institutions or viewpoints, not every person.

## The examination state

Prometheus's contests left the Ju'onai with a durable political temptation: treat demonstrated analysis as the right to decide. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Ju'onai Examiners|Ju'onai Examiners]] turn bounded demonstrations into certificates and rank. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Analyst Administration|Analyst Administration]] uses those records to select officials, model tax capacity, route petitions, plan public works, and govern clients.

The two organs remain separate in current canon. Examiners certify a demonstrated analytical scope; administrators appoint, tax, allocate, and command. A certificate does not prove moral fitness, loyalty, technical competence, or universal insight. An office does not make its holder able to analyze every system encountered.

In practice, the distinction is strained. Strongly expressed lineages can afford tutors, preserved interfaces, patronage, and repeated examinations. Their success becomes evidence that their descendants deserve the same preparation. Low-expression people produce field observations, maintain systems, copy records, and carry petitions while appearing in official models as labor classes rather than knowers.

The Hegemony is useful because it can preserve and compare causal records across large territory. It can find a failing granary route, expose a false tax estimate, coordinate repair, and remember which intervention produced which consequence. It becomes oppressive when the measured surface is treated as the whole person or when the official who chose the metric calls the result natural order.

## Administration and territorial dominance

Ju'onai expansion prefers a territory already rendered legible. Analysts map routes, authorities, stores, bottlenecks, grievances, divine alignments, and likely resistance before soldiers or tributary terms arrive. Entering battle without already having won is an ideal, not proof that every conquest was effortless or correct.

Conquered and client polities may retain local rulers, courts, cults, and work offices while owing tribute, records, passage, ideological deference, and access to Hegemony examiners. The Hegemony establishes administrative portfolios over tax, roads, grain, defense, or relic works without necessarily replacing every local owner. This layered rule lowers occupation cost and makes local elites responsible for delivering the model imposed upon them.

An official model can improve a client's irrigation and also convert that improvement into a larger levy. A local ruler can use Ju'onai certification to defeat rivals. A district can resist by concealing production, keeping rival records, training low-expression clerks, disputing the system boundary, or making compliance cost more than the model anticipated.

The Hegemony's status gods sharpen these contests. A god associated with measurement, cultivated reason, obedience, harvest, or public order can gain power as the relevant office and vocabulary spread. That does not establish that each concept has one necessary divine owner. Clergy, officials, clients, and God Beasts contest the correspondence. [[Spoilers/Institutions/God-Cult Covenants|God-Cult Covenants]] owns binding procedure; administrative rank cannot prove a person's upper-dimensional allegiance.

## Office and public works

Administrative offices are bounded portfolios rather than a single ladder of cleverness. A holder receives a territory or system, records, staff, budget, obligations, and review path. Eligibility may require a relevant certificate. Appointment still depends on superior office, patronage, divine faction, lineage, and political trust.

Succession transfers the portfolio and its record, not the predecessor's personal insight. Acting custodians keep tax collection, petitions, stores, and safety work moving while the next holder is examined and appointed. A patron may preserve one family in office for generations by controlling preparation without ever declaring the office hereditary.

Public works expose the limits of magical Analysis. An Analyst may see a causal relation in a watercourse, wall, road, archive, or relic. [[Spoilers/Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriests]] and [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|relic custodians]] retain operational competence over entrusted configurations. Local workers know sounds, soil, pressure, wear, and exceptions absent from the administrative model. An office that ignores those faculties can be brilliantly wrong at imperial scale.

## Tax, allocation, and ordinary petitions

The administration estimates land, production, labor, trade, infrastructure use, and risk, then assigns money, goods, service, or transport obligations according to local practice. Exact currencies and rates remain unresolved. The central political fact is that a measurement changes what the Hegemony claims a place can bear.

Officials allocate revenue to garrisons, roads, archives, public works, relief, examiner schools, cult obligations, and superior tribute. Clients argue not only over the rate but over who chose the counted unit, which losses are recognized, and whether increased output belongs to the community that produced it.

An ordinary resident encounters the examination state through a petition: a tax roll that classifies the household incorrectly, a road intervention that flooded a field, a certificate denied, an official model that erased a local right, or a public work closed after an analytical warning. Clerks preserve the claim, route it to the responsible portfolio, gather material evidence, and record remedy or refusal. An Analyst report may support the decision. It does not own the verdict.

Appeal climbs to a superior administrative office. Examination procedure can be reviewed by a separate examiner; policy remains with the administrator. A client polity can invoke its tributary compact, a worker can file through an office or patron, and a relic custodian can refuse an unsafe intervention. These routes are useful and unequal. The institution being challenged often owns the language, records, and time required to be heard.

## Evidence and blind spots

Analysis can detect local causal intervention where the relevant system is active and observable. It does not identify a hidden actor merely because a causal scar exists. It does not see dormant Luck, read intent, certify prophecy, or make an examiner immune to false premises.

Administrative reports should name their boundary, inputs, missing observations, intervention, and expected review point. Fraud and error enter through staged examinations, patron-selected samples, concealed local changes, copied insights, counterfeit certificates, intimidated workers, and a model framed so that the sponsor's preferred answer is the only one inside it.

The Hegemony's great blind spot is not lack of intelligence. It is the class power to decide what counts as a system.

## Expansion strands

The Hegemony acquired tributary control over part of the [[Spoilers/Polities/Prophecy - The Divided City|City of Prophets]]. It also annexed the original Channeler Crown City early after the Fall, dispersing communities that later gathered at Saxfoldi. The exact order and current borders remain unresolved.

In the Divided City, Ju'onai assessors must confront forecasts that change when acted upon; [[Spoilers/Institutions/Forecast Evidence and Courts|forecast law]] refuses to let expertise become verdict. In the Channeler Crown, Sarxe memory preserves the violence hidden by a clean annexation model. The Hegemony may classify both as administratively necessary. Neither client account is required to accept the classification.

## Historical stress: the Santabi'u Silt Appeal

[[Spoilers/Events/The Santabi'u Silt Appeal|The Santabi'u Silt Appeal]] alone owns the factual event. Its polity consequence is the Hegemony's characteristic contradiction: bounded Analysis can be correct while imperial administration uses the boundary to make a client territory false. The appeal demonstrates real self-correction and the class power to delay correction until damage fits an official model.

Hegemony memory emphasizes preserved competence and narrowed authority. Valley memory emphasizes consumed seed, departure, and observations believed only after they became administratively legible. Neither interpretation changes the event record or makes examination identical to government.

## Resistance and dungeon seams

Resistance includes rival ledgers, local measurement schools, refusal to certify a patron's model, hiding production, preserving worker testimony, exploiting differences among divine offices, and appealing to a superior who wants evidence against a subordinate. Occupied people can learn the Hegemony's categories well enough to turn them against the office that imposed them.

The Analysis Crown's buried virtual arenas, interfaces, programmed adversaries, and examination archives form natural dungeons. An old test may still adapt to entrants while certifying an authority no living polity recognizes. A record vault can expose generations of fraudulent office. A nanite interface may answer an Analyst and still require a Techpriest to determine what changing it will do. Provincial dungeons can hide survey markers, tax stores, suppressed petitions, failed public works, or the material outside an imperial model.
