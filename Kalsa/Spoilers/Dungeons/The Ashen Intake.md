# The Ashen Intake

The Ashen Intake is an ancient, buried terraforming installation that supplies [[Spoilers/Places/Low Sere|Low Sere]] with warm water and heat. Forty-one occupied hearths depend on it, along with the settlement's Grey Beds, ash craft, and lower cistern. It is failing. Three of its eight shutter indicators remain pale.

This is Kalsa's canonical starting dungeon for a party ready to enter immediately. The entrance is available, and the settlement has offered a usable expedition agreement, though several people dispute its terms. The crew must recover a missing calibration tool and service record, then reach the controls and leave the Intake in a safe configuration. Saving the machinery will not settle who should be trusted to operate it.

Characters can examine the site's marks, formulae, machinery, records, and manifestations. None reveals simulation truth or gives them an omniscient explanation of Kalsa.

[[Spoilers/Events/The Grey Scald|The Grey Scald]] records what happened during the previous failure. This article covers the evidence left underground, the routes and worsening hazards, and the changes the party can make now.

## Begin at the Cistern House

The expedition starts at the drained west basin of Low Sere's Cistern House.

Warm ash mist hangs below the stone lip. Maro Seln has drained the basin and unlocked its iron grate. At the black pressure door, Sera Venn kneels with an eight-tooth spindle in both hands. As the party watches, a fourth pale mark on the indicator goes dark, leaving three.

Tavi Kes ties two name cloths to the lead recorder's pack. Ressa Orr asks who can order the crew to withdraw. Behind the household witnesses, former custodian Teren Vey waits to argue that the compact uses the wrong translation.

The party can descend once it accepts or contests the terms. If the discussion takes a full work interval, advance the pressure clock.

## What the expedition must do

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where is the entrance? | The black pressure door behind the drained west settling basin in Low Sere's Cistern House |
| Who admits the crew from the surface? | Maro Seln acts for the basin table and controls the drain wheel and iron grate |
| Who releases the pressure door? | Sera Venn, provisional custodian, holds the eight-tooth spindle and may refuse an unsafe opening |
| Who depends on the Intake? | Low Sere's forty-one occupied hearths, Grey Beds, ash craft, and lower cistern |
| How much time remains? | Three pale shutters at the start; each pressure pulse closes one |
| What is the main objective? | Recover the **Ashen Measure** calibration comb and the last handoff folio, then reach the Intake Crown and establish a safe configuration |
| What else may be recovered? | Jori Kes's and Pell Am's bone-name tags, if recovery will neither destroy remains nor flood the Memorial Sump |
| What cannot become salvage? | Intake components, the calibration object, records, and settlement output |
| What remains to be decided afterward? | Who has both the competence and the right to hold custody of the stabilized system |

Ordinary lawful entry needs both Maro's surface admission and Sera's technical release. Stealing a key gives someone the means to enter, not the right. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Relic Custody|Relic Custody]] may justify forced entry for emergency rescue, but those responsible must make a complete record afterward.

## Who wants what

### Low Sere's basin table

The basin table claims the right to admit people to the site and to secure its warm-water output, public records, and restoration. Its case rests on the households that maintain the basin and rely on it. The table does not claim the technical authority to restart the Intake.

Maro Seln represents that urgency at the door: he wants the relic working before a review can close it.

### Sera Venn

Sera's provisional appointment lets her operate the pressure seal and care for the degraded intake within limits. She may inspect, isolate, and stop the system. She cannot authorize final calibration without the missing Ashen Measure or competent review.

She wants the party to recover what is missing, obtain the necessary review, and preserve the site's condition and evidence.

### Teren Vey

Teren, the removed custodian, maintains that his emergency intervention during the Grey Scald stayed within technical limits. He also disputes the process that removed him.

He admits hiding the Ashen Measure and handoff folio underground to prevent an unqualified restart. In exchange for a compact that records recovery as grounds to reopen his appeal, he offers the Relief Crawl route and a safer explanation of the service formula.

### Ressa Orr and the Eighth Baffle lineage

Ressa can compare the Intake's operating practice, Sera's training, and Teren's intervention. Her lineage claims no current title to the installation. If its review saves Low Sere, however, it will seek a formal agreement to provide continuing support.

Ressa retains her own authority to stop work at the Intake Crown.

### Tavi Kes and the Ash Names

The kin of Jori Kes and Pell Am claim the Memorial Sump, identified remains, bone-name tags, and every record of the Grey Scald. Tavi requires a kin witness on the expedition. Fixed memorial objects must stay in place, and no concentrated miracle may be used in the sump.

These mortuary claims do not give the fellowship ownership of the rest of the Intake.

### The delving crew

The crew is entitled to wages, care, rescue, accurate warning of hazards, and its own expedition record. It also claims whatever movable salvage remains after prior claims have been met.

Doing the recovery work does not make the crew owner of the Measure, folio, fixed machinery, remains, or the water and heat supplied to Low Sere.

## The agreement at the door

The offered [[Spoilers/Institutions/Delving Compacts|Delving Compact]] sets out what the expedition may do and what Low Sere owes in return.

**Support and payment.** Low Sere supplies six work-watches of provisions, surface rescue, injury care, and witnessed compensation. Payment may take the form of water, heat, stores, or trade goods, according to what the settlement has available. Wages and care remain owed even if there is no salvage.

**Limits of entry.** The crew enters through the Cistern House and may travel through the seven recorded zones. Of the seals named in the objective, it may open only those of the Custodian Gallery and Intake Crown.

**Command and refusal.** The delving lead decides movement, defense, route, and withdrawal, subject to three bounded stops:

- Sera can stop technical work at the pressure seal and known intake boundary.
- Ressa can independently stop work at the Crown.
- Tavi can hold any disturbance of identified remains.

**Records.** The recorder makes three copies: one for Low Sere, one for custodian review, and one for the crew.

**Recovery and salvage.** Fixed components, the Ashen Measure, handoff folio, bone-name tags, remains, and custody records are reserved. Repair, mortuary, and public-restoration costs are met before the working crew divides any remaining movable salvage.

**Expiry.** The compact ends on return, clock failure, or withdrawal. It grants no right to enter again.

Several disputes are already pressing against these terms. Teren will share his private route only if the return record names his appeal. Tavi will block descent if the party refuses a kin witness. Ressa will not permit final actuation merely because someone treats the service phrase as sufficient proof that the procedure is safe.

Maro wants permission to override a stop when the last shutter is reached. The offered compact gives him no such power.

Much of the expedition's food came from poor lower-step households. If the crew takes treasure and retreats without meeting the objective, those households bear the loss.

## Running the pressure clock

Begin with **three pale shutters**. Close one after each pressure pulse.

A pulse occurs when any of the following happens:

- The party takes an extended rest underground.
- Someone attempts a service formula in the wrong order.
- A major gate is forced or broken.
- The Relief Throat discharges without control.
- Play stalls for roughly one work-watch without a stabilizing action.

### Three pale shutters

Low Sere still receives its ordinary minimum service, though the Intake coughs grey water. Steam makes active seams visible. The old walkways are hot enough to trouble the crew but do not yet scald.

### Two pale shutters

Ash reaches the lower cistern, and Low Sere cuts the flow to the Grey Beds. Mist reduces visibility in the Settling Walk.

More potential reaches the Memorial Sump. Its manifestations become clearer and more dangerous.

### One pale shutter

Relief valves hammer, and unshielded metal burns anyone who touches it. Low Sere evacuates the lower-step channels. Any forced actuation now risks causing an immediate pulse.

### No pale shutters

Untreated hot slurry enters the Relief Throat. The next pulse ruptures the lower cistern.

Before that final pulse, the party can still:

- open the full bypass, saving lives at the cost of Low Sere's warm-water economy;
- get a message to Maro to close the surface feed at the Cistern House, buying time to evacuate but making a later restart harder;
- install the Measure and complete the correct sequence at zero differential pressure;
- withdraw before the rupture seals or changes the routes.

If Low Sere receives warning, its people can survive the rupture. The lower basin, Warm Steps, and dungeon entrance become a scalded disaster site.

## Understanding the service formula

Four octal triplets appear on the Intake Crown:

`110 — 001 — 000 — 111`

Teren translates the inherited high service phrase as:

> Six feed. One bears witness. At zero, seven are released.

Low Sere's old public instruction gave it as “feed six; wait one watch; release at empty.” The Grey Scald followed that reading of “one” as a time interval.

The crew must establish what the words and numbers refer to in the machinery. Choosing the more prestigious translation is not enough.

| Term | Physical meaning and evidence |
|---|---|
| **110 / six** | Six feed baffles have matching grey abrasion. They must align with the Ashen Measure's six long teeth. |
| **001 / one** | A person must hold the seventh, relief baffle by hand. Its handle carries glove burns and a watcher mark. |
| **000 / zero** | The differential gauge must reach zero. An empty cistern does not satisfy this condition. |
| **111 / seven** | The six feed baffles and the held relief baffle must release together. The eighth shutter stays in place to isolate the system. |

The party can compare gauges on the Meter Stair, wear in the Settling Walk, testimony in the Memorial Sump, the Custodian Gallery's folio, and the Measure itself. [[Spoilers/Institutions/Measures, Formulae, and Machine Speech|Formula practice]] governs how that comparison is made and accounted for.

If the machine responds to a sequence, that establishes an operational result. It does not establish anyone's political legitimacy or prove that a language is pure.

## Routes through the Intake

```text
Cistern House
    |
1. Ash Door and Meter Stair
    |----------------------\
2. Settling Walk         4. Relief Crawl
    |          \             |       \
3. Eight Screens --- 5. Memorial Sump  |
    |                   |               |
6. Custodian Gallery ---+---------------+
    |
7. Intake Crown
```

The Settling Walk takes longer and lets the crew preserve more evidence. The Relief Crawl offers a faster, hotter route based on Teren's disputed knowledge. Either route gives access to the Memorial Sump.

Every zone is part of an inherited installation altered by later human use. None is a simulation.

## Zone 1 — Ash Door and Meter Stair

Maro must drain the basin and Sera must turn the spindle before the black door opens inward. A toothed track records exactly how it was opened. Forcing the door damages that record and advances the clock.

Beyond it, eight shallow channels follow the descending stair. Six carry warm flow. A seventh hammers intermittently. The eighth is dry and marked with a black isolation sign. Each has a triplet label. A differential gauge displays pressure on two opposed arcs.

These are physical clues to the six-one-zero-seven formula. Ressa or a careful observer can record the relationships. An Analyst can map how the local parts relate, but cannot certify operation while the calibration dimension is missing.

### Dangers

- Ash coughs can abruptly obscure the stair and make it slippery.
- The hammering seventh channel burns anyone who touches it without insulation.
- Opening the dry eighth channel bypasses the admission seal, creating an uncontrolled route directly to Low Sere.

### Ways onward

The broad stair descends to the Settling Walk. A maintenance hatch bearing Teren's marks opens into the Relief Crawl.

## Zone 2 — Settling Walk

A bridge made of grids crosses three ash chambers. Beneath it, mineral dust settles out of the warm flow. Low Sere's present basins reproduce this sequence on a smaller scale.

The crew can see six repeatedly used baffle housings. Scratches left by workers record maintenance rotations, including missed shifts before the Grey Scald. A removable brass hand tool may be claimed as residual salvage if its recovery is documented. The screen weights may not be cut out for salvage.

### Crossing the bridge

Corrosion has weakened the grids. Concentrated weight can break them, and at two pale shutters mist hides the missing sections. Anyone who falls into the slurry suffers burns, choking ash, and damage to gear. The flow carries them toward the Memorial Sump screen.

The crew may brace the bridge to make its return safer. This uses compact tools owed back to Low Sere. The work itself does not advance the clock unless it becomes an extended rest; the party can also choose to press on without bracing.

### Ways onward

The bridge leads to the Eight Screens. A side ladder descends to the Memorial Sump's upper lip.

## Zone 3 — Eight Screens

Eight upright filter frames divide the intake. Six are grey from use, the seventh is scorched, and the eighth is black and almost clean. Beside them is a rack for a calibration comb.

The Ashen Measure is needed to synchronize the frames. Temporary wedges can hold one baffle for a single pulse, giving the crew time to retreat or reach the Custodian Gallery. They cannot restore the system.

Six long wear lines correspond to the formula's first term. On the seventh handle, a witness mark matches the bone-name tag cord used by platform workers.

### Dangers and limits

Each screen has a paired flow that must be isolated before the screen is cleaned or moved. Skipping that step advances the clock.

Compacted ash can break loose as a mass large enough to strike like a moving wall. The filter mesh is valuable, but every piece cut away worsens the output reaching Low Sere.

### Ways onward

A service door opens into the Custodian Gallery. A passage through the lower screens reaches the Memorial Sump, but using it disturbs the sump's seal.

## Zone 4 — Relief Crawl

This narrow, hot bypass runs around the Settling Walk. Teren used it during the Grey Scald, and his knowledge of it remains disputed. White mineral scale shows where flow has passed recently.

Only one armored or burdened person can pass at a time. Pressure knocks give little warning before becoming a scalding jet.

A person can hold a manual brace to delay one pressure-clock pulse. The longer they hold it, the worse the heat becomes, and they cannot defend themselves effectively. Cutting the brace opens the full bypass, committing Low Sere toward evacuation.

### Evidence of the warning

Teren's route marks show that he expected the maintenance platform to be cleared before he opened the route. A later scratch, written in ordinary Low Sere speech, specifies the shorter interval.

The two marks preserve different instructions. Neither establishes who actually received which warning.

### Ways onward

A hatch opens above the Memorial Sump. The crawl also continues to an emergency port at the Intake Crown.

## Zone 5 — Memorial Sump

This was once a settling pocket no longer in use. Post-Fall workers converted it into a maintenance platform. The Grey Scald filled it with mineral ash. Later, the Ash Names sealed the upper grate and tied two name cloths above the flow.

Beyond the seal, Jori Kes's and Pell Am's bone-name tags lie near mineral-coated human remains. Under Tavi's compact, the party may identify the remains and recover the tags. Fixed remains must stay where they are unless immediate danger requires their removal.

### What manifests here

Some spoken names receive answering knocks. Where warm potential passes, ash briefly assumes a human posture.

The presences could be Jori and Pell, other dead, thaumavores, pressure effects understood through grief, or several of these at once. They offer no simulation message, and their testimony cannot be treated as unquestionable.

At two pale shutters, the manifestations can draw away heat, put out lamps, repeat fragments of warning, or block the way to the Custodian Gallery. Recognition, a correctly spoken name, reduced potential, or material repair may calm them.

Force can disperse a form and move attention elsewhere. It does not establish that the dead have been defeated.

### Dangers

Mineral crust conceals unstable depth. Removing remains without supporting them collapses ash into the lower screen. Concentrated miracles supply the manifestations with more potential and may attract additional presences.

### Evidence on the tags

One tag has a work-watch notch matching the longer interval. The other is tied to a platform-clearance token issued after the valve had already started turning.

Together, these support both Teren's account of the procedure he expected and the workers' account of receiving late notice.

### Ways onward

A sealed archive crawl leads into the Custodian Gallery. The Relief Crawl hatch provides a route upward.

## Zone 6 — Custodian Gallery

The gallery is a dry service room containing records from different periods, discarded tools, a wall diagram, and a locked calibration chest.

Teren made the chest respond to a mechanical condition: the crew must copy the current differential gauge reading onto three octal wheels. Correct settings open it.

### Inside the chest

- **The Ashen Measure:** a black ceramic comb with six long teeth, one hooked tooth, and one solid isolation edge.
- **The last handoff folio:** Teren's intervention record, Sera's incomplete formation list, formula variants, and records of support failures.
- **Two sealed maintenance notices:** neither was ever delivered to the lower-step households.
- **A small pouch of portable calibration beads:** these are disputed property, but review may release them as residual salvage.

The Measure and folio are reserved objects. If the crew takes the beads without recording them, later comparison of the formulae becomes entangled in a dispute over custody.

### Dangers

Wrong wheel settings release an ash purge and advance the clock. The room's dry air makes heat exhaustion harder to notice.

The chest can be broken open in desperation. Doing so damages the Measure and leaves only bypass outcomes available.

### Ways onward

The main service door leads to the Intake Crown. A narrow return reaches the Eight Screens, and the archive crawl leads back to the Memorial Sump.

## Zone 7 — Intake Crown

Seven operating baffles stand around a black isolation shutter. A central rack holds the Ashen Measure, and another differential gauge repeats the one on the Meter Stair. There is no voice to explain the machinery.

### Restoring the Intake

The correct procedure is:

1. Keep the black eighth shutter in place as the isolation boundary.
2. Insert the Ashen Measure, aligning its six long teeth with the six feed baffles.
3. Assign one person to hold the hooked seventh relief baffle.
4. Bleed pressure through the marked channel until the differential gauge reads zero.
5. Release all six feed baffles and the relief baffle together.
6. Record the machine's response. Competent custodians then decide whether to remove the Measure or leave it installed.

With the recovered folio, Sera can perform this sequence. Ressa can compare the procedure and stop it. Teren may advise remotely; he may accompany the crew only if the compact has been amended to permit it. A player character can carry out the physical steps, but that work does not confer custody.

### Other choices

**Full bypass.** Opening the Relief Throat sends slurry away from the cistern. Low Sere loses normal output but gains time to evacuate.

**Surface shutdown.** The crew signals Maro to close the settlement feed. One final pulse occurs before the clock pauses. Restart will require excavation and a new compact.

**Partial wedge.** Restoring one or two baffles buys several work-watches. The eventual failure will be more dangerous.

**Removal.** Taking the Measure without calibrating preserves a valuable object while abandoning the settlement's claim.

### Errors at the controls

Releasing the baffles while the differential gauge is above zero repeats the Grey Scald on a larger scale.

Releasing them with the eighth isolation shutter open sends the surge directly toward Low Sere.

Waiting “one watch,” instead of assigning someone to hold one baffle, advances the clock.

## What counts as recovery

Material success means the expedition returns with:

- the Ashen Measure intact, or installed under recorded custody;
- the last handoff folio and copies;
- a record of the configuration chosen at the Intake Crown and what happened;
- the crew alive and Low Sere notified;
- the bone-name tags, if they could be recovered within the mortuary hold.

There is no need to open every door or destroy a manifestation. Maximizing salvage can actively undermine the job: the machinery, records, and remains already have claims on them, and a settlement still needs the output.

## Returning to Low Sere

Reserved objects go through a witnessed inventory on return. The crew must surrender them for that process while retaining its own expedition record. Low Sere owes wages and care before anyone divides residual salvage.

The return also starts several distinct reviews. Sera and Teren receive separate hearings. Tavi receives the tags and an account of any disturbance. Ressa submits her comparison within its stated limits. Maro must disclose any override he ordered.

Working machinery alone will not secure trust. The crew can repair the Intake and still compromise the result by concealing context, allowing one claimant to take every copy of the record, or accepting payment that depends on a preferred verdict.

Conversely, a crew that cannot reach the objective can still give a defensible account of its conduct: warn Low Sere, respect the stops, and bring back evidence of what prevented completion.
