# The Ash-Halo Court

The Ash-Halo Court is a current Ghostlight situation in a reopened Jamnai
furnace court. The Ninth Furnace branch intends to arm Daro, its champion,
before an assault on the East Collar heat gate. The first face appears in the
quench water before the rite begins.

The cult hired the party to clear the old slag conduit and certify access to
the under-gallery. A mortuary witness has placed a sealed answer roll in their
custody. The first horn is sounding above them.

## The East Collar claim

The Ash Hook branch holds the East Collar gate above Ninth Furnace. It diverted
the return heat into a siege foundry after a collar plate fractured. The Ninth
Furnace hostel and a tributary bakehouse now receive cold water for part of each
shift. Ash Hook's custodian calls the diversion a safety boundary. Iresa, holder
of Ninth Furnace, calls it a conquest disguised as maintenance and intends to
retake the gate before the hostel stores spoil.

The assault will restore heat quickly if Ninth Furnace wins. A damaged collar
opened under combat could flood the lower galleries with steam. Nobody at the
court has Ash Hook's current inspection record; a courier carrying its claimed
fracture copy has not returned.

## People at the court

Iresa paid for the armament and ordered the horns. She accepts a delay for
Sedren's material stop or for one recognized-share petition filed before the
second horn. She treats any other delay as aid to Ash Hook. If the party opens
the answer roll against her order, she lets its carrier speak once, then marks
the party's sponsorship for review after the assault. If they conceal the roll
and she discovers it, she orders the registrar to seize their delving return.

Sedren owns the furnace configuration. Thin ceramic or an open path through the
slag cistern is enough for him to delay the second horn. He will stop the
weights after the grant opens if heat crosses his marked perimeter. Iresa can
remove him afterward, but no other qualified custodian is present to sign the
working. Sedren opens his rack copies, maintenance throat, and furnace return
to anyone who inspects beside him. He knows neither the feeder nor the divine
route.

Vael holds the grant docket. A signed custody receipt gives the party a view of
the champion's grant, expiry, reserve, and two withdrawn shrine entries. He
withholds the contributor names from a copy unless a shrine keeper
countersigns. Before the second horn he can spread the grant across fitting
intervals; afterward he can reserve or retract what remains. Evidence of a
second feeder makes him disperse. A warning without a route makes him preserve
the assault allocation.

Orsa speaks for the Iron Shelter inside the rite. Before the second horn she
can enter one recognized share if Melka presents a recorded sign, Taru
recognizes the claimant, and Taru names the requested act and accepts a
witnessed term. Once the route opens,
the Iron Shelter treats unrecognized diversion as attack and pulls the grant
inward through the raiment. A recognized claimant instead receives an offer of
service, departure, or binding. Orsa knows the offered terms and the hierarchy's
response, never the claimant's complete identity.

Melka owns the answer roll and will open it with one household recognizer and
the party present. She permits a full copy before carrying it below; Iresa's
registrar may challenge that copy at the court threshold but cannot erase the
household witness. Melka will carry the original into the gallery if the party
keeps a route back to the stair. Her evidence ends with recorded signs and
later appearances.

Taru carries a household copy for the defeated champion whose shrine went
silent after the first armament. He asks for warning or withdrawal if that
champion's sign returns and refuses a binding offered in its name. If Iresa
keeps his copy outside, he leaves before the second horn to warn the withdrawn
shrines; the party then loses its only present Soiru'i claimant.

Daro speaks privately while the anchor wires remain slack and a healer is
present. After the second horn he can answer only with agreed hand signs until
the fitting releases his jaw. He will ask Sedren to stop if divided movement
continues through two rack falls. At the third horn he may refuse the march,
losing his branch standing and leaving the East Collar assault without its
champion.

Nema, the branch registrar, gives the party a certified extract naming Iresa's
order, Orsa's authority, and the promised restoration of withdrawn grants. She
releases the complete branch copy only on Iresa's order or a superior appeal.
She records refusals exactly and interprets them in Ninth Furnace's favor.

## The prepared body

Forge workers have restored the racks and weights from copies made after
[[Spoilers/Events/The Ash-Halo Armament|the first armament]]. Several
substitutions are recent. One ceramic run is thinner than its return describes.
The quench drain crosses the old slag cistern before joining a newer channel.
Heat, ash, and fragments can therefore carry the working past the court's
marked perimeter.

The champion has been fasting and wears the anchor wires under padded cloth.
Healers can keep the body alive through an ordinary fitting. They have no
training for a grant that changes course through dead flesh or a Beast remnant.
The champion can report pressure, loss, and divided motion while the route is
open. He cannot identify what touches it.

Vael has withdrawn minor protection from a branch hostel and
a furnace-family shrine to fund the armament. Both entries are visible in the
ledger. The assault patron promised to restore them after victory. No reserve
has been assigned to keep the under-gallery open if manifestations block the
quench works.

## What the party can reach

The answer roll can be opened with Melka, kept sealed under the
branch order, copied, or carried into the gallery. It records gestures, voices,
sites, last appearances, and households able to recognize them. It proves
neither identity nor hostility.

The slag conduit reaches the cistern through a maintenance throat. The party
can clear it, dam it, divert it into an empty cooling bay, or leave the old
obstruction in place. Each choice changes where heat, quench water, debris, and
potential can travel when the grant opens.

The source racks can be spread across several fitting intervals. This weakens
the single visible armament and reduces the amount exposed at once. Removing
the thin ceramic run delays the third horn until Sedren inspects and signs a
replacement configuration. Dropping the weights aborts the rapid assembly after the route opens
and can trap hot material around the champion.

The branch recognizes a ritual share for named Soiru'i. Adding an answer-roll
claim requires Orsa, Melka, and Taru. The party can carry that
request, conceal it, or let the feeder enter an unrecognized route. Recognition
can open negotiation or binding; it can also give a dangerous claimant a
better foothold.

## Three horns

### First horn — stores

Workers open the racks and heat path. Sedren makes the last
material inspection. A face forms in the quench trough and copies a gesture
from the sealed roll. Orsa orders the water covered until the
champion is ready.

### Second horn — route

The divine grant enters the champion and prepared machinery. Nearby
manifestations can reach it through the quench path, source stores, recognized
shares, or another foothold the party left active. Diversion first appears as
lost force, mistimed rack motion, cold patches, a foreign voice, or divided
movement in the champion.

The hierarchy can retract, disperse, redirect, contain, negotiate, bind, or
strike along a route it can perceive. Each response changes the armour. The
party can alter physical paths, carry evidence and requests, protect witnesses,
support the champion, or demand the custodian use a stop. None of those acts
guarantees that the God complies.

### Third horn — closure

The assault begins if the champion can move. A concentrated grant may close
the raiment in one incandescent sequence. A dispersed grant may leave lighter
armour and more surviving outer protections. A successful feeder may remain in
the court as an independent claimant. Loss of control can turn the cistern into
a fountain. An abort leaves the heat gate without its promised champion and
the branch patron looking for the owner of the refusal.

## Reachable outcomes

- **Concentrated armament:** the armour closes and the champion marches.
  Forceful interceptions can strip manifestations along the route. The answer
  roll and later shrine visits determine which familiar presences have gone
  silent.
- **Dispersed fitting:** the champion receives partial raiment across several
  intervals. More hostel and shrine protection survives, while the assault
  begins with less force and more time for rivals to respond.
- **Recognized claimant:** an intermediary admits one manifestation to a
  negotiated share or binding attempt. It may supply evidence, demand terms,
  betray the route, or stabilize as a new independent power. This result does
  not establish Godhood or identity by itself.
- **Hidden feeding:** the armour appears successful while the cistern or another
  site accumulates enough diverted potential to answer later.
- **Fountain:** several dead presences and Beast remnants manifest from a route
  nobody controls. The party must choose whom to evacuate, what to contain, and
  whether closing the flow will extinguish the only available witnesses.
- **Aborted working:** stores, machinery, and some grants survive. The champion,
  branch patron, forge custodian, and exposed households carry incompatible
  accounts of why the assault lost its miracle.

## Receipts after the court

The forge return records material consumed, machinery damaged, work stopped,
injuries, and the state of the heat trunk. The potential return records grant,
withdrawals, detected loss, retraction, reserve, and recovery still owed. The
answer roll receives witnessed flashes, later appearances, and silences without
declaring what died. Covenant and branch records preserve orders, recognized
shares, refusals, promises, and the champion's use of the grant. The party's
delving return preserves the route changes and evidence it actually carried.

These records can support food, heat, treatment, repair, safe lodging,
containment, restored grants, discipline, or appeal. None proves divine intent
or resolves the identity of a manifestation.

## Continuing pressure

The heat-gate assault, hostel protection, shrine relations, forge work,
champion's body, possible new claimant, and party testimony continue after the
third horn. Ghostlight resolution makes one outcome actual through the party's
route changes, disclosures, bargains, material work, and risk. The vault does
not select it in advance.

## Unresolved

- Exact Crown district and the descent of Ninth Furnace, the Iron Shelter, and
  the reopened court from the first armament.
- Whether Ash Hook's missing fracture copy would justify its gate diversion.
- Identity and temporal operation of the slag-cistern feeder.
- Which answer-roll signs will appear and what any later silence means.
- Whether the current court becomes an armament site, shrine, fountain,
  mortuary jurisdiction, sealed ruin, or several of these after play.
