# The Ash-Halo Court

The Ash-Halo Court is a reopened Jamnai furnace court where the Ninth Furnace branch is preparing divine armour for its champion, Daro. Once fitted, he is meant to lead an assault on the East Collar heat gate. The rite draws on furnace machinery, prepared materials, and a grant from the Iron Shelter. Something beneath the court can reach that grant through the quench water.

This is a current Ghostlight situation. The cult has hired the party to clear the old slag conduit and certify access to the under-gallery. A mortuary witness has also entrusted them with a sealed answer roll: a record of familiar signs previously seen in the gallery and at neighboring shrines. Before the rite begins, a face appears in the quench water.

The first horn is sounding above the party.

## Why Ninth Furnace wants the gate

The Ash Hook branch controls the East Collar gate above Ninth Furnace. After a collar plate fractured, Ash Hook diverted the return heat into a siege foundry. The Ninth Furnace hostel and a tributary bakehouse now receive cold water for part of each shift.

Ash Hook's custodian says the diversion is necessary for safety. Iresa, who holds Ninth Furnace, calls it a conquest disguised as maintenance. She intends to retake the gate before the hostel stores spoil.

A Ninth Furnace victory would restore heat quickly. But opening a damaged collar during combat could flood the lower galleries with steam. Nobody at the court has Ash Hook's current inspection record. A courier sent with its claimed copy of the fracture record has not returned.

## What is ready, and who controls it

The furnace, racks, and weights fit prepared materials around Daro to make the armour. At the second horn, the Iron Shelter's grant enters both him and the machinery. Other presences can divert that power through connections such as the quench channel. Changing a channel or stopping a rack therefore changes more than the metalwork: it changes where the grant can go, and what can reach it.

| Part of the working | Present arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Intended result | Divine raiment for Daro, the branch champion |
| Materials | Iron scales, copper wire, ceramic splints, resin, charcoal, and quench water are staged beside the lifting weights and furnace machinery |
| Energy | A live heat trunk, furnace draft, stored weights, and prepared mechanical motion |
| Divine route | The Iron Shelter grants power through Orsa, its recognized intermediary, and Daro |
| Advance evidence | A sponsored Prophet supplied the assault interval; the report says nothing about the under-gallery |
| Material control | Sedren, the forge custodian, can sequence the racks, refuse an unsafe heat draw, stop the weights, and close the quench path |
| Allocation control | Vael, the potential steward, holds the grant, its expiry, the two withdrawn shrine entries, and a small recovery reserve |
| Mortuary evidence | Melka, the death witness, owns the sealed answer roll now in the party's custody |
| Feeder | Something in the slag cistern can reach the quench path; its identity, memory, Gift, and intention remain unresolved |
| Party authority | Inspect and clear the under-gallery, preserve hazards and records, and recommend proceeding, changing the working, or stopping it. The party cannot command the God, branch, forge, or dead |
| Schedule | The first horn opens stores, the second opens the grant, and the third closes the armour and begins the assault |

## People at the court

**Iresa — branch holder and assault patron.** Iresa paid for the armament and ordered the horns. She will accept a delay if Sedren stops the work on material grounds, or if one petition for a recognized share is filed before the second horn. That petition asks to admit a claimant to a negotiated portion of the working, with witnesses and terms; Orsa, Melka, and Taru have the required roles described below. Iresa treats any other delay as aid to Ash Hook.

If the party opens the answer roll against her order, Iresa allows its carrier to speak once, then marks the party's sponsorship for review after the assault. If they hide the roll and she discovers it, she orders the registrar to seize their delving return.

**Sedren — forge custodian.** Sedren decides how the furnace and machinery are configured. Thin ceramic or an open route through the slag cistern is enough for him to delay the second horn. After the grant opens, he will stop the weights if heat crosses his marked perimeter. Iresa can remove him afterward, but no other qualified custodian is present to sign the working.

Sedren lets anyone inspecting beside him examine his rack copies, maintenance throat, and furnace return. He knows neither what the feeder is nor how the divine route works.

**Vael — potential steward.** Vael controls the grant docket. With a signed custody receipt, the party can inspect the champion's grant, its expiry, the reserve, and the two withdrawn shrine entries. Copies omit contributor names unless a shrine keeper countersigns.

Before the second horn, Vael can divide the grant across several fitting intervals. Afterward, he can reserve or retract what remains. Evidence of a second feeder makes him disperse the grant. A warning that identifies no route leaves him committed to the assault allocation.

**Orsa — the Iron Shelter's intermediary.** Orsa speaks for the Iron Shelter during the rite. Before the second horn, she can admit one recognized share. Melka must present a recorded sign, and Taru must recognize the claimant, name the act being requested, and accept a witnessed term.

Once the divine route opens, the Iron Shelter treats an unrecognized diversion as an attack and pulls the grant inward through the raiment. A recognized claimant instead receives an offer of service, departure, or binding. Orsa knows those terms and the hierarchy's response. She never knows the claimant's complete identity.

**Melka — death witness and owner of the answer roll.** Melka will open the roll with one household recognizer and the party present. She allows a full copy to be made before she takes it below. Iresa's registrar may challenge that copy at the court threshold, but cannot erase the household witness.

Melka will carry the original into the gallery if the party keeps a route back to the stair. Her evidence establishes recorded signs and later appearances; it does not reach beyond them.

**Taru — household recognizer.** Taru carries a household copy concerning the defeated champion whose shrine went silent after the first armament. If that champion's sign returns, he asks for warning or withdrawal. He refuses a binding offered in the champion's name.

If Iresa keeps his copy outside the court, Taru leaves before the second horn to warn the withdrawn shrines. The party then loses the only Soiru'i claimant present.

**Daro — champion.** Daro can speak privately while his anchor wires are slack and a healer is present. After the second horn, he can communicate only through agreed hand signs until the fitting releases his jaw.

If Daro reports divided movement and it continues through two falls of the fitting racks, he will ask Sedren to stop. The situation establishes this as a sign of diversion in his body, but does not specify its exact appearance or sensation. At the third horn, he may refuse to march. That costs him his branch standing and leaves the East Collar assault without its champion.

**Nema — branch registrar.** Nema gives the party a certified extract naming Iresa's order, Orsa's authority, and the promise to restore withdrawn grants. She releases the complete branch copy only on Iresa's order or through a superior appeal. She records refusals exactly, then interprets them in Ninth Furnace's favor.

## The machinery, the champion, and the withdrawn protections

Workers restored the racks and weights using copies made after [[Spoilers/Events/The Ash-Halo Armament|the first armament]]. Several parts have recently been substituted. One ceramic run is thinner than the furnace return says it is. The quench drain passes through the old slag cistern before reaching a newer channel. Heat, ash, and fragments can carry the working along that path and beyond the court's marked perimeter.

Daro has been fasting. He wears the anchor wires beneath padded cloth, ready for the fitting. The healers can keep him alive through an ordinary fitting, but they have no training for a grant that changes course through dead flesh or a Beast remnant. While the route is open, Daro can report pressure, loss, and divided motion. He cannot identify what is touching the route.

To fund the armament, Vael withdrew minor protection from a branch hostel and a furnace-family shrine. Both withdrawals appear in the ledger. The assault patron has promised to restore them after victory. No reserve is assigned to keeping the under-gallery open if manifestations block the quench works.

## Decisions within the party's reach

### Open or withhold the evidence

The party can open the answer roll with Melka, leave it sealed under the branch order, copy it, or carry it into the gallery. It lists gestures, voices, sites, last appearances, and households able to recognize them. A match may support recognition. It proves neither identity nor hostility.

### Change the physical routes

A maintenance throat connects the slag conduit to the cistern. The party can clear the conduit, dam it, divert it into an empty cooling bay, or leave the old obstruction in place. Each choice changes where heat, quench water, debris, and potential can travel once the grant opens.

### Change or interrupt the fitting

Spreading the source racks across several fitting intervals makes the single visible armament weaker and exposes less at once. Removing the thin ceramic run delays the third horn until Sedren has inspected and signed a replacement configuration.

After the divine route opens, dropping the weights aborts the rapid assembly. It can also trap hot material around Daro.

### Request a recognized share

The branch permits a ritual share for named Soiru'i. Admitting a claim from the answer roll requires Orsa, Melka, and Taru. The party can bring that request forward, conceal it, or let the feeder enter through an unrecognized route.

Recognition can make negotiation or binding possible. It can also give a dangerous claimant a better foothold.

## Three horns

### First horn — stores

Workers open the racks and the heat path while Sedren performs his last material inspection. A face forms in the quench trough and copies a gesture recorded in the sealed answer roll. Orsa orders the water covered until Daro is ready.

### Second horn — route

The divine grant enters Daro and the prepared machinery. Nearby manifestations can reach it through the quench path, the source stores, a recognized share, or another foothold the party has left active.

The first evidence of diversion may be lost force, mistimed rack motion, cold patches, a foreign voice, or divided movement in Daro's body. Along a route it can perceive, the hierarchy can retract, disperse, redirect, contain, negotiate, bind, or strike. Every response changes the armour.

The party can change physical paths, carry evidence and requests, protect witnesses, support Daro, or demand that Sedren use a stop. None of these actions guarantees the God's compliance.

### Third horn — closure

The assault begins if Daro can move. A concentrated grant may close the raiment in one incandescent sequence. A dispersed grant may produce lighter armour while leaving more outer protections intact.

A feeder that succeeds may remain in the court as an independent claimant. If nobody controls the route, the cistern can become a fountain. If the working is aborted, the heat gate loses its promised champion, and the branch patron looks for whom to hold responsible for the refusal.

## Reachable outcomes

- **Concentrated armament:** The armour closes and Daro marches. Forceful interceptions can strip manifestations along the route. Comparing the answer roll with later shrine visits establishes which familiar presences have gone silent.
- **Dispersed fitting:** Daro receives partial raiment over several intervals. More hostel and shrine protection survives. The assault has less force, and rivals have more time to respond.
- **Recognized claimant:** An intermediary admits one manifestation to a negotiated share or a binding attempt. It may supply evidence, demand terms, betray the route, or stabilize as a new independent power. None of these results establishes its identity or Godhood by itself.
- **Hidden feeding:** The armour appears successful, while the cistern or another site gathers enough diverted potential to answer later.
- **Fountain:** Several dead presences and Beast remnants manifest through a route nobody controls. The party must decide whom to evacuate, what to contain, and whether closing the flow would extinguish the only available witnesses.
- **Aborted working:** Stores, machinery, and some grants survive. Daro, the branch patron, Sedren, and the exposed households give incompatible accounts of why the assault lost its miracle.

## What the records can establish

Different records preserve different parts of the event:

- The **forge return** records consumed material, damaged machinery, stopped work, injuries, and the condition of the heat trunk.
- The **potential return** records the grant, withdrawals, detected loss, retraction, reserve, and recovery still owed.
- The **answer roll** adds witnessed flashes, later appearances, and silences. It does not declare what died.
- **Covenant and branch records** preserve orders, recognized shares, refusals, promises, and Daro's use of the grant.
- The **party's delving return** records the routes they changed and the evidence they actually carried.

These records can support claims for food, heat, treatment, repair, safe lodging, containment, or restored grants. They can also support discipline or appeal. None proves divine intent or settles a manifestation's identity.

## After the third horn

The assault does not settle every consequence of the rite. Hostel protection, relations with the shrines, forge work, Daro's body, any new claimant, and the party's testimony all remain in play.

Ghostlight resolution determines what actually happens through the party's changes to the routes, disclosures, bargains, material work, and risks. The vault does not choose an outcome in advance.

## Unresolved

- The slag-cistern feeder's identity and how it operates across time.
- Which signs from the answer roll will appear, and what any later silence means.
- Whether play leaves the court as an armament site, shrine, fountain, mortuary jurisdiction, sealed ruin, or several of these.
