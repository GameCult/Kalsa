# Relic Custody

A relic is a surviving object, component, interface, structure, or process inherited from the [[Foundations/Ark and Fall|Ark]] and its descendants. Some relics are portable. Others are rooms, conduits, foundations, environmental works, or systems crossing several present borders. Their age makes them scarce; their continuing function makes them socially entangled.

**Relic custody** is the bounded authority to preserve, access, move, service, or hand off a named relic under stated conditions. It is not one universal property law. The polity holding the ground, the community relying on the output, the household possessing a component, the temple tending a shrine, the discoverer who recovered it, and the [[Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriesthood]] able to operate it may all have real claims without any one claim swallowing the rest.

## Claims that must remain separate

- **Possession:** who physically holds or controls access to the relic now.
- **Site authority:** who governs the land, district, ruin, or threshold containing it.
- **Operational custody:** who may inspect, isolate, service, restart, or refuse unsafe work on the named configuration.
- **Output claim:** who is entitled to water, heat, movement, shelter, information, or production the relic provides.
- **Service dependence:** whose life or livelihood will be harmed if it stops or moves.
- **Sacred claim:** who tends worship, burial, covenant, or ancestral obligations attached to it.
- **Recovery claim:** who accepted the labor and danger of finding or extracting it.
- **Title:** whatever durable ownership a relevant polity or community recognizes.

Possession proves none of the others automatically. Repair does not create title. Land control does not create competence. A sacred claim does not settle safe operation. Discovery does not erase prior dependence. A ruler may decide among claims inside their jurisdiction, but that decision remains a political act rather than a property of the machine itself.

## The custody record

A defensible custody record identifies the relic, location, current arrangement, visible condition, known function, active dependents, claimants, hazards, access limits, and the work presently authorized. It records removed or substituted parts, witnesses, failed attempts, and when the custody decision must be reviewed. For a portable relic, the record should follow the object. For a fixed system, it should remain available at more than one access point when possible.

The record does not need one universal medium. It may be written, marked onto a component, held in ritual memory, copied into diagrams, or witnessed by several parties. Its value lies in preserving the chain between action and consequence. A beautiful pedigree that omits a cracked seal or disputed claimant is ceremony wearing the skin of evidence.

Custodians may conceal routes, interfaces, or procedures whose disclosure would invite immediate harm. That exception should remain specific. Secrecy about one dangerous threshold does not justify hiding every failure, fee, claimant, or dependency attached to the relic.

## Custodian formation

A relic custodian is formed for kinds of work before being appointed to one configuration. Formation may come through a [[Institutions/The Techpriesthoods|Techpriest lineage]], a local maintenance body, apprenticeship under a prior custodian, recovered records, or several of these. No lineage name creates competence by itself.

A defensible formation record preserves:

- systems and configurations studied;
- supervised inspections, stops, repairs, restarts, and failures;
- tools, measures, languages, formulae, and interfaces actually used;
- hazards communicated to workers and dependants in speech they could act upon;
- boundaries the candidate recognized and work they correctly refused;
- workers, local keepers, translators, and prior custodians able to witness the record;
- conditions requiring supervision or further comparison.

Formation is not a universal license. A person trained on a Crown lift does not thereby own a terraforming intake. Analytical insight does not replace practice. An inherited formula does not prove its units survived unchanged. See [[Institutions/Measures, Formulae, and Machine Speech|Measures, Formulae, and Machine Speech]].

## Bounded appointment

Appointment binds a qualified person or team to a named relic configuration and its dependants. The appointing parties vary by site. They may include the ground authority, communities receiving the output, prior custodian, competent lineage witness, work crews, temple, or household possessing the threshold. There is no universal appointing court.

The appointment records:

- relic, threshold, configuration, and known system boundary;
- allowed inspection, isolation, service, substitution, restart, and refusal;
- outputs and people dependent upon them;
- access controller and admission conditions;
- records, tools, keys, formulae, and stores transferred;
- assistants, translators, workers, and relief coverage;
- support owed to the custodian and crew;
- reporting, secrecy, conflict, and review requirements;
- term, emergency authority, succession plan, and removal path.

A custodian owns operational decisions only inside that boundary. Appointment does not transfer land, output allocation, title, cult authority, salvage, or a possible person's identity. A dependent settlement can appoint a custodian without becoming competent to overrule a material stop. A lineage can recognize competence without acquiring the settlement's water.

## Support and dependency

Custody is labor sustained by other labor. An appointment that names responsibility without food, shelter, tools, assistants, access, replacement material, record copies, relief, and time to inspect is a future accusation disguised as an office.

Support terms identify who provides:

- ordinary livelihood and dangerous-work compensation;
- work crews, cleaning, carrying, guarding, and local observation;
- tools, measures, formula copies, and calibration access;
- interpreters for technical and public notice;
- medical care, rescue, and support for injured workers or dependants;
- substitutes and rest during prolonged failure;
- secure duplicate records outside the custodian's sole control.

Dependants may condition support on service, but cannot require a custodian to certify an unsafe act. A custodian may not close a public output merely to improve personal terms. When promised support fails, the custodian records the missing input, resulting limit, people exposed, and condition for safe continuation.

## Admission and work

Before work begins, the acting parties should establish which relic and configuration are in scope, what condition triggered access, who may enter, what may be changed, which observations must be preserved, and who decides whether service can resume. A Techpriest's operational judgment can close an unsafe work boundary. It does not decide how a safely restored output is distributed.

Entry can require several authorities at once. At [[Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]], Low Sere controls access to the drained cistern threshold while its appointed custodian controls the pressure seal and technical admission. Neither key alone grants a lawful descent.

## Discipline and removal

Custodians answer for acts within their authority. Grounds for discipline can include concealed intervention, work beyond formation, falsified record, sale of access, diversion of output, refusal to preserve evidence, abandonment without emergency notice, repeated failure to communicate hazard, or incapacity that makes continued operation unsafe.

Complaint does not prove failure. The inquiry separates:

- the operational claim and evidence available at the time;
- support, workers, formulae, and access actually supplied;
- public instruction and whether affected people could understand it;
- political orders and overrides;
- material outcome and later knowledge;
- actions inside and outside the appointment.

Immediate suspension is legitimate when continued access poses named danger. Suspension transfers only the minimum threshold, tools, and record control needed to stabilize the site. It does not establish guilt or permanent title.

Removal requires a preserved state, inventory, competent comparison, notice to dependants, a chance for the custodian and workers to answer, and a plan for handoff. The reviewing body is local: appointing parties, their successors, and an independent competent witness where one can be obtained. A lineage may testify to formation; it does not automatically decide the settlement's office. Dependants may testify to harm; dependence does not make a vote into technical truth.

Possible outcomes include warning, narrowed authority, supervised practice, added support, restitution, suspension, removal, referral of theft or violence to local law, and restoration after an unsupported complaint. A removed custodian retains wages, property, testimony, appeal, and credit for work unless the relevant authority separately decides otherwise.

## Succession and handoff

A planned handoff transfers only the authority named in it. Moving a component to a workshop can grant possession and permission to repair while leaving title and installation authority elsewhere. Lending a key does not transfer the chamber. Granting emergency entry does not create a permanent route. A successor who receives records and tools still has to show that they understand the live arrangement.

Custodians train relief because sudden incapacity is predictable even when its date is not. A handoff should include a witnessed walk of the live boundary, current configuration, known substitutions, active hazards, unresolved claims, recent failures, formula and unit differences, worker contacts, dependent notices, and acts the successor is not yet qualified to perform.

When a custodian cannot continue, dependants need warning before procedure becomes inheritance theater. An apprentice may receive provisional authority for isolation and ordinary service while a competent comparison is sought. Provisional office expires or narrows at a named review. It cannot become permanent merely because the settlement survived long enough to get used to it.

The outgoing custodian may refuse to hand over a dangerous formula to an unqualified successor while surrendering the threshold and public record. That refusal requires a competent review and safe escrow where available. Withholding every record to force reinstatement is breach; demanding every key before naming a qualified successor is also breach.

## Appeal

Appeal follows the disputed authority rather than one universal relic tribunal.

- A custodian contests formation or technical findings before an independent competent comparison accepted locally.
- Dependants contest support, output closure, secrecy, or misuse before the authority that appointed and sustains the office.
- Workers contest notice, wages, injury, and changed work through their compact or local forum.
- Kin and cult claims follow [[Institutions/Death and Binding Law|Death and Binding Law]].
- Site entry, recovery, and hazard follow [[Institutions/Relic and Ruin Law|Relic and Ruin Law]].
- A delving crew follows its [[Institutions/Delving Compacts|Delving Compact]].

Appeal does not compel unsafe restart. A stop remains until the named hazard changes or another qualified custodian records a different bounded claim. Appeal should not preserve an ousted custodian's monopoly by starving a settlement of review.

## Historical consequence: the Grey Scald

[[Events/The Grey Scald|The Grey Scald]] alone owns the adopted event facts. Its custody consequence is that emergency suspension, discipline, technical comparison, mortuary claim, handoff, and succession must remain separately reviewable. The current [[Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|Ashen Intake]] descent can recover the missing body and evidence; recovery enables review but does not decide Teren's appeal or Sera's appointment.

## Emergency custody

Immediate danger can justify acts outside ordinary permission: opening a sealed path, moving a relic away from fire or flood, interrupting a service, removing a failing part, or entering a restricted chamber to rescue people. Emergency custody lasts only as long as the named danger and the work needed to stabilize it.

After stabilization, the actor must preserve what they changed, identify losses, notify affected claimants, and return the relic or submit the continuing dispute to whatever authorities actually exist. “We saved it” is evidence of service and risk. It is not an all-purpose deed.

Refusal can also be necessary. A custodian may decline a ruler's order when the requested activation exceeds known limits or transfers danger to people excluded from the decision. Such refusal may be punished as sacrilege, theft, insubordination, or sabotage. The maintenance fact and the political judgment remain separate even when the same body must answer both.

## Recovery from ruins

The buried Crown Cities contain components detached by the Fall, abandoned through later failure, cached during war, or still attached to systems whose reach is no longer visible. Recovery begins with possession under uncertain conditions, not clean title.

A recovery party should record where an object sat, what remained connected, what changed when it moved, and which signs suggested continued service, warning, burial, habitation, or restraint. A part that appears inert may be holding a seal closed. A closed door may protect treasure, preserve quarantine, imprison a claimant, or do several at once. [[Foundations/Kaos|Kaos]] does not remove the need to inspect the causal system; no style reveals every remote dependency.

Recovery claims are strongest where labor and danger would otherwise go unrecognized. They weaken when extraction destroys the evidence needed to understand the relic, disables a public work, violates an acknowledged burial, or ignores a living claimant. The exact shares owed to discoverers, carriers, sponsors, local rulers, priesthoods, and dependent communities are not universal canon.

## Relics that answer back

The Ark may preserve imprisoned Posthumans, Promethean processes, adaptive systems, ritual projections, or none of these in any particular relic. [[Foundations/Projections, God Beasts, and Thaumavores|Thaumavores]] can also manifest where magical potential concentrates. A voice, refusal, prediction, repeated coincidence, or apparent preference therefore demands preservation and bounded inquiry without proving a single explanation.

Custody of the container does not settle custody of a possible person. Operators should record behavior, competing explanations, and any act that would erase the only available evidence. Immediate hazards may still require isolation or shutdown. Neither reverence nor skepticism grants permission to destroy what the dispute is about.

The present canon does not establish a universal personhood test for relic minds, ghosts, gods, or simulations. Nor does it establish that any specific Crown relic contains one.

## Ordinary dependence and dispute

Most people encounter custody through queues, closed doors, service dues, delayed harvests, guarded workshops, and arguments over whose district receives the working part. The system can preserve public memory: a removed component remains traceable, a repairer cannot quietly deny an intervention, and an emergency custodian must give authority back. It can also make survival depend on documents and specialists ordinary households cannot inspect.

Disputes are therefore fought through access as often as force. A town may block removal of a pump claimed by a distant polity. Workers may refuse to help extract a component from a system they service. A temple may admit inspection but forbid dismantling. A ruler may seize a relic and then discover that possession cannot compel it to work. Rival Techpriesthoods may disagree while each retains part of the necessary record.

These conflicts need not converge on one court. Settlement may involve local law, negotiated shares, sureties, exchange of hostages or witnesses, reciprocal access, temporary service priority, or continued partition of authority. Which forms dominate in each polity remains unresolved.

## Dungeon consequences

Relic custody turns a dungeon expedition into more than acquisition. Useful jobs include mapping a service boundary, restoring a record chain, witnessing a handoff, retrieving a named part, recovering a prior crew, isolating a spreading failure, or carrying evidence between claimants who cannot safely meet.

Success may produce several separate outcomes: the party possesses a relic; a Techpriesthood recognizes its configuration; a site authority permits movement; a village claims its output; a temple demands rites; or a possible inhabitant refuses activation. None implies the others. The return journey can therefore be as consequential as the descent.

No universal right of salvage, right of conquest, priestly veto, or polity monopoly has been adopted. Until a polity note establishes otherwise, dungeon finds remain burdened by site, service, hazard, sacred, recovery, and possible-person claims.

[[Dungeons/The Ashen Intake|The Ashen Intake]] is the canonical direct-entry pressure test for these claims. The settlement needs its output; a custodian controls technical entry; a former custodian contests removal; kin claim remains in the route; and a delving party can possess the missing calibration object without acquiring the right to sell or install it.
