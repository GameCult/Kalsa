# Selza'a Family Territories

Selza'a family territories are the primary organs of residence, protection, resource use, kinship, and local government in the [[Polities/Luck - Selza'a|Luck Crown]]. No central state owns the city. Families hold places and obligations through descent, adoption, marriage, work, compact, and defended use.

Territory is not one absolute title. A family can protect a path without owning every traveler, maintain a windtrap without owning the water after allocation, or tend a dimna shrine without owning the people it names.

## Owner map

| Surface | Owner | Does not own |
|---|---|---|
| Household dwelling and stores | The recognized household under family custom | Household members as property |
| Family territory and protection response | The family authority recognized by members and neighboring compacts | Every resource, person, shrine, or passage inside the boundary |
| Windtrap operation | The named maintainers and any recognized technical custodian | Water allocation merely because they can stop the mechanism |
| Cistern and catchment use | Households, families, or compact users under the relevant schedule | The atmosphere, every future flow, or a bearer alleged to affect it |
| Shared boundary work | The compact formed by contributing territories | Unrelated family offices or permanent city government |
| Membership, adoption, and marriage recognition | The persons involved and the family procedures they invoke | Bodily consent, metaphysical lineage, or automatic transfer of property |
| Dimna or seltanzda rite | The family, shrine, and witnesses conducting it | Author truth about a God Beast or a person's moral status |
| Immediate territorial defense | The family responding within its held ground | Permanent command of a wider federation |
| Citywide external defense | The temporary federation formed for the named threat | Internal rule after the threat or every family's stores |

Family government protects one real invariant: people who depend on a place must know who is expected to answer when its water, path, stores, or safety fails. It becomes domination when the answerer claims that dependence makes every dependent theirs.

## What makes a territory

A territory is built from use and responsibility:

- dwellings and sheltered courts;
- cistern shares, catchments, and water-carrying routes;
- windtrap structures and maintenance access;
- workrooms, stores, gathering or cultivation sites, and trade positions;
- paths marked through fog, warning stations, and safe resting places;
- family shrines, graves, archives, and compact meeting sites;
- obligations to defend, repair, witness, host, and compensate.

Boundaries may be marked by stone, texture, chime, scent, cord, air current, spoken route, witness memory, or maintained feature. A visual line is unreliable in fog. A boundary can move when a path collapses or a catchment changes, but no family may treat weather alone as a silent grant of a neighbor's use.

The practical question at a boundary is not only “whose ground?” It is “who responds, who may enter, whose work is due, who receives output, and where can a dispute pause without depriving anyone of water?”

## Family membership

Blood descent is one route into a family and carries political weight because Luck expression and dimna identity are remembered along lineages. It is not the only route.

Membership may arise through:

- birth and later recognition;
- adoption into household and territory;
- marriage or partnership under stated property and residence terms;
- client or protected status;
- apprenticeship and work obligation;
- refuge, debt settlement, or transfer from another family;
- affiliation with a shrine or seltanzda tradition.

The exact bundle must be recorded or witnessed. A person can be kin without holding a cistern share, a resident without joining a dimna rite, a worker without inheriting family office, or a spouse without transferring every prior obligation.

Families benefit from calling these relations one blood body. The simplification makes defense fast and succession legible. It also lets senior kin claim authority over adopted people, spouses, clients, and descendants who never accepted the same terms.

## Adoption, marriage, and mixed expression

Adoption can preserve a household, bring a skilled keeper into a territory, recognize an existing bond, protect an orphan or fugitive, or recruit a person thought likely to strengthen a seltanzda line. These motives can coexist. Adoption does not prove that a dimna accepted the person or that Luck expression will follow.

Marriage and partnership join labor, care, residence, lineage, and political alliance. A boundary marriage may support a water compact and also place one partner under incompatible family claims. Defensible terms name residence, children's membership, property, existing debts, exit, and what happens if the families feud.

Mixed-style, low-expression, and Null people belong to Selza'a society. Their presence can make them valuable mediators because they are not legible through one family's Luck story. It can also make them convenient scapegoats because no one can directly inspect Luck in the strongly expressed families accusing them.

No family may treat a phenotype, improbable event, survival history, or prophetic bias as proof of membership in a magical lineage. [[Foundations/Magic Styles#Expression and descent|Expression and descent]] remains uncertain.

## Internal government

The seed establishes territorial families and no central government, not one universal clan constitution. Some families may deliberate through household heads, work stewards, elders, shrine keepers, strong defenders, or people trusted to speak with neighboring territories. Others may be dominated by one patron and their close dependents.

A family authority owns decisions only within the powers its members and compacts recognize: stores, defense, work shares, admission, internal use, representation, or rite. Office should name the resource or consequence it carries. “Head of the blood” is not a blank deed over every person.

Records can be written, tactile, oral, marked into structures, or distributed among witnesses. Useful records distinguish household membership, territory use, work duty, dimna affiliation, and protection. A family that keeps only one genealogy can make everyone outside the preferred branch disappear from succession.

## Succession

Succession is divided because the family owns several different things.

- A household chooses who keeps the dwelling and dependents.
- A work group recognizes who can maintain a windtrap or route.
- Compact partners recognize who may speak for a territory.
- Store witnesses hand off pooled goods and outstanding claims.
- A shrine follows its own custodial and mortuary practice.
- Family defenders recognize immediate command under threat.

One person may inherit several roles, but no dimna sign or lucky survival transfers them automatically. A claimed sign enters family deliberation as religious evidence. It does not replace consent, competence, compact recognition, or the rights of other successors.

During dispute, maintainers continue essential water and route work. A succession faction that interrupts common survival to force recognition damages its own territorial claim. Powerful families can still win this way if neighbors fear the Luck surrounding them.

## Exit and affiliation change

A member may seek to leave a household, transfer family, establish an independent residence, or become unaffiliated. Exit requires settlement of entrusted goods, current work, dependents, and shared claims; it does not require surrender of personhood or every relationship.

Practical exit is expensive. Water, shelter, route knowledge, witnesses, risk coverage, and protection are organized through families. A person who leaves can become dependent on a new host before being free of the old one. Families can weaponize debts or dimna claims to make departure impossible.

Boundary compacts may recognize a cooling interval, protected route, third-family witness, and temporary water share for someone changing affiliation. No universal right or procedure exists. Leaving the city itself remains separate and unresolved; [[Reference/Story Seeds|the escaped bearer]] is not evidence that present residents routinely can or cannot do so.

## Shared works and commons

Windtraps and water reveal the limits of territorial independence. A structure may stand on one family's ground, catch fog moving across several territories, drain into a shared cistern, and require workers from every user.

A defensible shared-work compact names:

- structure and configuration;
- access path and inspection rights;
- maintainers and technical custody;
- labor, material, and store contributions;
- ordinary output schedule and emergency baseline;
- records of failure and missed work;
- stop authority and review;
- immediate repair pool;
- restitution and exit from the compact.

Operation and allocation remain separate. A keeper can stop a dangerous windtrap without claiming all water. A family that hosts the structure cannot deny inspection to every downstream user. A user that misses maintenance cannot be deprived of emergency water before the compact's dependents are protected.

No citywide water authority is adopted. Larger networks arise through linked compacts, and the same family may be powerful because many agreements pass through its territory.

## Boundary compacts

Neighboring families use compacts for passage, market access, marriage, work, shared water, restitution, nonaggression, and witness exchange. Each compact owns only its named relation. The network acts like government without a government standing above it.

When a dispute crosses a boundary, compact partners can invite a third family, unaffiliated domain worker, or mutually trusted witness to preserve evidence and mediate. The third party does not become a universal judge. Its standing comes from acceptance in the named case and whatever bonds support its work.

Compact failure can isolate a family from water, trade, marriage, and defense without a formal sentence. Wealthy families survive such pressure longer. Poor households inside them may bear the isolation despite having no voice in the original breach.

## Mutual defense and federation

External defense follows signal and proximity. The threatened family owns immediate response on its territory. Prearranged neighbors contribute guides, stores, defenders, shelter, and route closure. A wider federation forms when enough families recognize the same external threat.

Federated authority is bounded by threat, route, and duration. A coordinator may direct the muster without gaining control of family succession, dimna rites, water allocation, or internal punishment. Stores remain accounted to contributors and dependents.

The system is difficult to conquer because there is no single palace whose capture transfers the city. It is vulnerable to manufactured emergency. A dominant family can classify a refugee, dissident, or rival compact as an external probe and keep federation powers after danger passes.

Review asks what changed: which paths reopened, which stores returned, which exceptional patrols ended, and which detained people received ordinary family process. A federation that cannot name its ending has become the central government the seed says did not form.

## Ordinary dispute

Most disputes concern boundary use, missed work, water shares, damaged goods, marriage terms, debt, guest conduct, injury, or succession. The first owner is the family or compact whose promise was breached. Material evidence and witnesses matter more than theories of invisible Luck.

If a family cannot resolve a dispute internally, parties may choose a neighboring witness, invoke an existing compact, exchange sureties, separate use temporarily, or carry the dispute into wider relations by refusing trade and passage. Feud is one outcome, not the only institution available.

[[Institutions/Luck Harm and Restitution|Luck Harm and Restitution]] handles accidents and alleged magical harm. A family may believe a dimna was attacked. It still cannot present coincidence as proof against a person.

## Poor, unaffiliated, and outsider life

Poor households may contribute labor to windtraps and defense while holding insecure shelter or water claims. A family can praise their loyalty and exclude them from succession. Risk pools can demand contributions they cannot prepay. Work contribution, waiver, and baseline aid keep compact membership from becoming wealth certification where recognized.

Unaffiliated residents rely on rented use, employers, shrine networks, temporary compacts, or personal hosts. They can move information across family lines and are easy to blame because no large branch promises retaliation.

Outsiders enter through a host family or a compact that names route, duration, lodging, work, trade, bond, and responsibility for harm. A host owns the visit agreement, not the guest. Withdrawing a host does not erase wages, injuries, property, or the need for a safe path out.

Null and mixed people are not reliable magical detectors or magically neutral witnesses. Their testimony is judged like anyone else's. The absence of expressed Luck makes them less useful to lineage mythology and sometimes more exposed to it.

## Historical consequence: the Broken Windtrap Settlement

[[Events/The Broken Windtrap Settlement|The Broken Windtrap Settlement]] alone owns the adopted event facts. Family-territory practice retains its reciprocal-restraint procedure, shared-work accountability, and the warning that a host family can preserve membership and territory while failing to preserve an adopted member's livelihood or reputation.

## Institutional failure

Family territories fail when:

- lineage becomes a claim to own members or descendants;
- a host uses protection to imprison a guest;
- windtrap operation and water allocation collapse into one monopoly;
- a compact excludes households unable to contribute cash or stores;
- an emergency federation keeps command after the threat;
- a dimna claim chooses office without consent or competence;
- unaffiliated residents provide work but receive no baseline use;
- family copies of a boundary or adoption record disappear;
- alleged Luck replaces material evidence and retaliation becomes self-confirming.

The order can survive these failures for a long time. Luck may protect a powerful family from the consequences it exports.

## Dungeon seams

- A buried Ring corridor carries a fog current that defines three present boundaries; opening a door will change every path marker above.
- An old cistern below family territory contains a dormant filtration relic and records showing a downstream family's forgotten use share.
- A dimna shrine manifests only when delvers carry concentrated potential through it; families disagree whether this proves identity or predation.
- A collapsed windtrap foundation contains the original anchor and evidence that a celebrated feud began as neglected maintenance.
- An unaffiliated refuge uses maintenance tunnels omitted from family route records.
- A lost compact archive can restore adoption, marriage, or water claims to living descendants—and expose which family erased them.
- A dungeon hazard produces improbable survivals around one guide. The party must manage risk without converting survival into guilt.

## Unresolved

- Family names, numbers, territory map, internal constitutions, and native office terms.
- Exact forms of membership, adoption, marriage, succession, and exit across families.
- Windtrap technology, custody, water baseline, and compact network.
- Whether any durable federation spans the whole city outside external attack.
- Rights and population of poor, unaffiliated, mixed, Null, and outsider residents.
- Relation among family, seltanzda, dimna, shrine, and actual God-Beast hierarchy.
- Authority governing entry to and exit from the isolated city.
