# The Broken Windtrap Settlement

The Broken Windtrap Settlement is adopted history. Its absolute date, location within the Luck Crown, participating family names, and relationship to other dated events remain unresolved.

## Failure and accusation

Three family territories shared a high windtrap and lower cistern. Several small faults preceded the failure of a corroded anchor, which injured workers, broke catchment surfaces, and contaminated stored water. An adopted mixed-heritage keeper had been present during the recent incidents. Two families treated that pattern as evidence that the keeper or host family carried hostile Luck.

A visiting prophet found persistent forecast bias around the failing system and refused demands to identify a bearer or culprit. The report established uncertainty, not guilt.

## Restraint and repair

The shared compact opened emergency stores before deciding cause. It suspended armed crossings and public culprit-naming, preserved the wreckage, continued baseline water, and admitted an independent windtrap worker.

Inspection found corrosion hidden beneath a covering none of the families had removed. Each family had deferred part of the shared maintenance schedule while preserving better records of its neighbours' failures than its own. The case table found a shared-system failure and proven maintenance breaches. It found no magical culprit.

The risk pool paid immediate care, replacement water, and repair. Later contributions shifted according to missed maintenance and use. Reciprocal restrictions ended after the rebuilt trap passed inspection.

## What remained broken

The keeper received a corrected record and no liability but lost contracts and changed family affiliation. Poor households consumed emergency rations and lost work while families with private cisterns remained comfortable. Later compacts added lost-work support and required every user to record completed maintenance, not only a neighbour's failures.

Territorial councils remember the settlement as proof that distributed government can stop a feud. Victims remember that procedure repaired water before reputation and never fully repaired the person it cleared.

## Institutional consequences

- [[Polities/Luck - Selza'a|The Selza'a polity]] treats the settlement as a limit on turning family suspicion into collective defence.
- [[Institutions/Selza'a Family Territories|Family Territories]] draws reciprocal restraint and shared-work accountability from it.
- [[Institutions/Luck Harm and Restitution|Luck Harm and Restitution]] owns the continuing repair, evidence, pool, restitution, and record-correction procedures.

The settlement does not prove that Luck caused the failure, that the keeper carried Luck, or that every Selza'a compact uses the same remedy.
